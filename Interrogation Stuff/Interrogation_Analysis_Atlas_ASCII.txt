INTERROGATION ANALYSIS ATLAS A typed system for claims,
context, evidence, and inference
Version 1.0 | 15 October 2026 General specification, worked examples, and bounded verification
ABSTRACT
An interrogation is a sequence of actions performed through language under unequal information,
uncertain memory, shifting context, and institutional constraints. A transcript records part of that sequence.
It does not automatically identify every speaker, resolve every reference, authenticate every described
event, or preserve the distinction between a proposed interpretation and an admitted fact. The present
system makes those distinctions explicit. Its objects are typed propositions, utterance records, contexts,
event times, source lineages, assumptions, questions, answers, transformations, and proof obligations. Its
architecture is an atlas of linked logical blocks whose shared interfaces retain the identity and scope of
claims as an analysis develops.
The system combines a record layer, an interpretation layer, a model layer, and an assessment layer. The
record layer preserves evidence and uncertainty about transcription. The interpretation layer represents
competing readings and speech acts. The model layer tests declared premises, dependencies, and
alternatives. The assessment layer states what is established, excluded, unresolved, or inconsistent within
the stated abstraction. Source support is tracked separately from model truth. A positive statement, a
negative statement, and conflicting reports are evidence states; they are not interchangeable with logical
truth, falsity, and contradiction in a chosen world model.
The central mathematical discipline is preservation under transformation. A summary must retain the
relevant actor, object, time, modality, quantifier, question scope, and provenance unless a separately
justified transition changes one of those fields. A source chain cannot acquire independence merely by
being copied. A conditional answer cannot establish its assumed antecedent. A response cannot confirm a
later proposition merely because a transcript places them close together. A rate cannot move between
populations or event units without a valid mapping. A general admission cannot automatically identify the
handling of one particular file, person, transaction, or episode.
Thirty chapters provide the main construction. Each contains three worked examples that connect the
formal notation to ordinary situations. The final chapters supply complete invented cases, while the
appendices collect notation, rule obligations, proof records, annotation forms, and reproducibility
guidance. The companion software implements a deliberately finite core and adversarial checks. It can
enumerate declared Boolean models, preserve typed records, expose countermodels, and reject specified
invalid transitions. It does not interpret unrestricted natural language or decide historical truth without
external inputs.
STATUS OF THE WORK
This is a general analytical specification and a tested reference kernel. It is not an empirically validated
psychological instrument, a legal decision system, or a universal solver for every possible conversation. Its
elementary formal claims have explicit assumptions and proofs. Its executable claims have explicit input
families, bounds, and result records. Completeness means coverage of the declared abstraction and
termination of the stated finite procedure. The completeness of a real-world account remains an
evidentiary question.
The design follows the supplied linked logic-array preference and presupposition audit framework. Those
project documents supply the requested architecture and analytic distinctions, not independent empirical
validation. Public research is used sparingly to locate the framework within established interviewing and

CHAPTER 01. PURPOSE AND THE COMPLETENESS
CONTRACT
01.1 The object of analysis
Interrogation analysis reconstructs what an exchange establishes, what it merely proposes, and which
transformations connect its questions, answers, summaries, and subsequent reports. Its object is a sequence
of communicative events together with competing interpretations. A useful system must retain genuine
admissions, genuine denials, uncertainty, corrections, and interviewer contributions without deciding their
significance in advance. The same architecture applies to interviews, administrative calls, incident
reviews, negotiations, testimony, and ordinary disagreements. Domain differences enter through explicit
predicates, evidence standards, and decision rules.
DEFINITION 01-D1. An analysis specification is S=(U,Ent,L,E,H,Q,A), where U is the immutable event
record, Ent is a typed entity registry, L is a declared formula language, E is an evidence ledger, H is a set
of hard premises and bridge rules, Q is the permitted query family, and A is an abstraction policy. An
abstraction policy states which distinctions are represented and which are omitted. An analysis result is
always indexed by S and its version.
This definition prevents a transcript, a psychological interpretation, and a world reconstruction from
silently becoming the same object. A sentence's occurrence can be established while its content remains
disputed. The word "complete" consequently requires an object: complete transcription coverage,
complete enumeration of a finite model family, complete checking of a proof, or complete treatment of
declared issue categories. None alone means complete knowledge of the historical event.
EXAMPLE 01.A
A supervisor asks, "So the pump was switched off deliberately?" A technician answers, "The switch was
down when the alarm started." Let p mean deliberate shutdown and q mean the observed switch position.
U records the question and answer; E supports Assert(technician,q). The response supplies no direct
assertion of p. A model with accidental shutdown and a model with deliberate shutdown both fit these
speech records. Result: the deliberate-shutdown query is unresolved unless an explicit, defensible bridge
connects the observation to intention.
01.2 Four boundaries of completeness
DEFINITION 01-D2. Coverage completeness means every event in the declared input interval has a
disposition: interpreted, marked irrelevant with a reason, or retained as unresolved. Semantic completeness
means the represented alternatives exhaust the possibilities allowed by the declared semantics. Procedural
completeness means a decision procedure returns a correct answer for every input in its stated class.
Empirical completeness would require every materially relevant real circumstance to be known; this atlas
does not claim that property.
OBLIGATION 01-O1. Every report states input boundaries, abstraction choices, permitted queries,
model semantics, and unfinished checks. A missing speaker, uncertain word, or omitted gesture remains an
uncertainty marker. The marker cannot become evidence of silence, agreement, refusal, or deception
merely because the available text lacks a corresponding entry.
A finite system can exhaustively check represented combinations even when its interpretation of language
remains contestable. The two claims must travel separately. This distinction gives mathematical checking
a useful role: it can guarantee that the encoded premises were handled consistently without pretending to
authenticate those premises. A larger vocabulary may reveal distinctions that an earlier, smaller
vocabulary could not express.

EXAMPLE 01.B
A clerk says, "There is no dispatch entry." An investigator summarizes, "The parcel never left." Define r as
no entry in the examined log and s as no physical dispatch. The ledger supports a report of r. A declared
administrative rule might say every dispatch creates an immediate, durable, correctly searched entry. Only
with that rule and its applicability does r support s by contraposition. Without it, delayed entry and
wrong-log search remain cases. Result: checking all Boolean assignments to r and s is complete for that
tiny model, while the adequacy of the administrative rule remains separately reviewable.
01.3 The linked block construct
The organizing structure is a cluster of linked topic cubes with a shared center, rather than a single solid
cube. The center holds statements true throughout the declared nonempty model family. South/-x holds
alternatives, assumptions, and local cases. North/+x holds successive checks of types, consistency,
coverage, propagation, witnesses, and conclusions. The -y direction records dependencies and witness
construction. The +y direction records sources, proof records, and scope. Relative to a declared focal
proposition P, +z contains contexts satisfying P and -z contexts satisfying NOT P. Spatial position is an
index, never a truth measure or probability. [SRC-PREF]
DEFINITION 01-D3. An interface is an explicit map identifying the meanings shared between topic
blocks. It lists preserved entity identities, formula interpretations, contexts, and time scopes. Distinct edge
types represent implication, identity, compatibility, temporal accessibility, and provenance. A joint
constraint involving several propositions is stored as a hyperedge, rather than approximated by unrelated
pairwise links.
The architecture can expose an exact failure point. A claim may be well typed but unsupported, supported
as testimony but not entailed as world fact, locally consistent but incompatible with another block, or valid
under one assumption but invalid without it. Each failure belongs to a different diagnostic stage. Coloring
all of them "false" would erase the reason that the analysis was needed.
01.4 Minimum preservation guarantees
PROPOSITION 01-P1. If every conclusion added to the center is satisfied by every model of a nonempty
M(H), then no center conclusion excludes a model retained by H. Proof: take any retained model m and
any added center conclusion phi. The admission rule gives m |= phi. Therefore m also satisfies H together
with phi. The same argument applies to each admitted conclusion, so all retained models survive their
addition. This is preservation relative to H, not a guarantee that H describes reality.
The nonempty condition matters. With no models, "every retained model satisfies phi" would hold
vacuously for every phi. The atlas instead labels that situation INCONSISTENT and suspends skeptical
world conclusions. An inconsistency report includes the conflicting premises and their provenance,
allowing repair without inventing a resolution.
The system also distinguishes a proposed investigation from a completed analysis. A checklist can specify
an excellent sequence without showing that any item was performed. Each northern diagnostic stage
therefore has an execution record: input version, method, outcome, remaining uncertainty, and evidence
location. An item marked inapplicable records why it is inapplicable. A failed test records its failure rather
than disappearing from the report. These administrative details make the mathematical claims reviewable
because they identify the exact object to which a proof or computation applies.

EXAMPLE 01.C
Two maintenance records state respectively that exactly one technician opened a cabinet and that neither
listed technician opened it. A third premise says those two technicians exhaust all possible openers.
Together the premises have no model. The system returns INCONSISTENT, not "both technicians opened
it" and not "the cabinet never existed." Removing the exhaustion premise permits an unlisted opener;
removing the opening premise permits no opener. Result: alternative repairs occupy separate case blocks
until evidence justifies a particular revision.
01.5 What the deliverable certifies
OBLIGATION 01-O2. Each conclusion carries its claim identifier, exact formula, premise identifiers,
rule, assumption set, source lineage, model scope, and verification status. Finite checks certify the
implemented finite procedure on the declared fixtures and ranges. Elementary proofs support the stated
uniform results. Neither replaces inspection of the annotation that translated ordinary language into
formulas.
The analyst should seek asymmetric errors as well as obvious contradictions. A procedure can preserve a
suspect's admission while incorrectly broadening its time range. It can correctly quote a denial while
attaching it to the wrong item. It can discover a valid inference while counting its source twice. Success
therefore requires preservation of content, ownership, scope, and dependency together. The remaining
chapters make these obligations explicit enough to audit, reproduce, challenge, and extend.

CHAPTER 02. ENTITIES, ROLES, IDENTITY, AND
CO-REFERENCE
02.1 Stable entities and changing descriptions
An interrogation often contains more people, objects, and events than its grammar makes visible. "He"
may refer to a sender, an account holder, a depicted person, or someone mentioned by a sender. "The
report" may refer to a draft, a printout, an attachment, or a revised file. The first protection against
confusion is a registry whose identifiers survive changing names and interpretations. Stable identifiers
organize uncertainty; they do not announce that an uncertain identification has been resolved.
DEFINITION 02-D1. Ent is a set of identifiers equipped with a type function tau. Basic types include
Person, Account, Device, Document, PhysicalItem, Event, Place, Organization, and Proposition. The
registry may use richer types, but relation signatures remain explicit. Owns(Person,Device),
Uses(Person,Account), Depicts(Document,Person), and OccurredAt(Event,Place) have different argument
positions and meanings. A literal name is an attribute or a mention, not automatically an identity key.
DEFINITION 02-D2. A mention is m=(span,candidates,context,status). Its candidate set contains entities
compatible with the available interpretation. Resolution selects an entity only when an asserted
justification warrants that restriction. A role is expressed as Role(e,r,c,t), where r is contextual, c specifies
context, and t specifies time. Role membership does not replace entity identity.
EXAMPLE 02.A
A dispatcher asks, "Did the owner authorize the repair?" A mechanic answers, "The person using the van
agreed." Let a be the registered owner, b the caller, and v the van. The answer supports Uses(b,v) and a
reported authorization by b. It does not establish a=b or that b had authorization authority. A model with
an owner calling and another with a temporary driver calling both survive. Result: owner consent remains
unresolved while caller consent can be recorded at the speech level.
02.2 Identity needs its own evidence
DEFINITION 02-D3. Coref(m1,m2) means that two mentions denote the same entity in the interpretation
under examination. Equal(e1,e2) is object identity within that interpretation. SameLabel(e1,e2),
SimilarAppearance(e1,e2), SharedAddress(e1,e2), and SharedDeviceUse(e1,e2) are separate relations.
None implies Equal without an explicit bridge rule.
OBLIGATION 02-O1. Every merge stores its justification, the previous identifiers, the affected
propositions, and a reversible alias map. A proposed merge remains a hypothesis until accepted as a hard
premise. If later evidence splits the entities again, propositions dependent on the merge become stale and
require rechecking. Records are not silently rewritten to conceal the earlier identification decision.
Identity is an especially strong claim because it permits substitution across every applicable predicate. A
weak observation such as a matching display name can therefore acquire disproportionate effects if
mistaken for identity. It may transfer another person's actions, knowledge, relationships, or age into a
subject's record. The error then spreads through many apparently independent topic blocks.
LEMMA 02-L1. Substitution along genuine equality preserves satisfaction in an ordinary typed model: if
m |= Equal(a,b), then replacing a by b in a well-typed predicate instance preserves its truth. Proof: equality
means a and b denote the same domain object. The predicate interpretation receives the same argument
object after replacement, so its truth is unchanged. This lemma cannot justify substitution from
SimilarAppearance(a,b), because that relation need not identify the denotations.

EXAMPLE 02.B
An interviewer asks about messages from "Depot Manager." A worker says, "That name appeared on two
accounts." Let account identifiers a1 and a2 share a display label. The registry retains SameLabel(a1,a2)
and leaves their operators unresolved. If one account sent instructions on Monday and the other sent a
cancellation on Tuesday, a common label does not establish one speaker's change of mind. Result: the
apparent contradiction becomes two source claims with an unresolved operator relation, rather than an
established inconsistency within one person's account.
02.3 Co-reference as constrained interpretation
DEFINITION 02-D4. A co-reference assignment rho maps each unresolved mention to one candidate
entity, respecting type, explicit inequality, time, and discourse constraints. The admissible set R contains
all assignments that satisfy the declared constraints. Formula evaluation uses rho explicitly whenever
unresolved mentions occur. A query is robust to co-reference uncertainty only if its classification is
unchanged across every admissible rho and associated retained world model.
This approach allows uncertainty without pretending that every interpretation is equally plausible. An
analyst may rank interpretations heuristically, but elimination requires a stated exclusion rule or evidence.
The preferred interpretation may guide an inspection task while alternatives remain represented. Such
priorities are operational choices, not hidden premises in the logical conclusion.
PROPOSITION 02-P1. If a conclusion phi holds under every admissible co-reference assignment and
every retained model for that assignment, then selecting any admissible assignment preserves phi. Proof:
selection produces a subset of the previously checked assignment-model pairs. A universally satisfied
formula remains satisfied on that subset. The converse fails: truth under one selected assignment need not
hold under the others. This explains why a convenient pronoun resolution cannot independently establish a
robust conclusion.
02.4 Counts, uniqueness, and role changes
OBLIGATION 02-O2. One-to-one matching constraints are imposed only when the domain supplies
them. Several accounts may have one operator; several people may use one account; several copies may
derive from one document; one person may hold several roles. A logic-grid layout is not a license to
impose exactly one match in every row and column. [SRC-PREF]
Descriptions also change over time. The current manager may differ from the manager at the event. An
adult's earlier photograph depicts that same person at another age. A former owner can authorize an earlier
action without holding current authority. The entity remains stable while attributes and roles retain their
time arguments. Dropping those arguments can create false contradictions or transfer present properties
into the past.
EXAMPLE 02.C
A reviewer says, "The manager approved the order, but the manager denies approving it." The first
statement concerns March; the denial concerns a person appointed in April. Let p be the March manager
and q the April manager. Role(p,manager,c,March) and Role(q,manager,c,April) do not imply p=q. If the
approval concerns March and the denial belongs to q, the statements can coexist without personal
inconsistency. Result: the role phrase is indexed by time, and any claim of contradiction must first
establish shared actor, action, and event scope.

02.5 Registry checks and unresolved identity
The northern checks first reject ill-typed links, then examine identity assumptions, cardinality constraints,
and time-sensitive roles. The provenance block records why an entity was introduced and why mentions
were associated with it. The southern cases preserve competing assignments. Topic-block interfaces
expose exactly which identifiers are shared; similarity of labels is insufficient.
Uncertainty can concern whether an entity exists at all. A purported subcontractor mentioned in a message
initially warrants a mention record, not an authenticated Person object with confirmed real-world
existence. The registry can represent a discourse referent whose existence is a query. Similarly, two
mentions of a fictional or mistaken name need not create two real people. The type system should
distinguish discourse references from authenticated entities when that difference matters. Existence,
identity, role, and authenticity then become separate constraints, preventing a detailed narrative from
acquiring real-world participants solely through repeated reference.
OBLIGATION 02-O3. An identity report distinguishes established equality, established inequality,
unresolved equality, and conflicting support about equality. It also distinguishes an unresolved real
identity from an unreadable name. The former concerns who an entity is; the latter concerns what a
recording says. Keeping both uncertainties explicit prevents a confident transcription from masquerading
as verified identification.

CHAPTER 03. EVIDENCE RECORDS, TRANSCRIPTION, AND
PROVENANCE
03.1 Preserve the observation before interpreting it
A transcript is a representation of recorded speech, and a recording is an observation of an interaction.
Neither should be silently equated with an exhaustive description of the historical event discussed. An
edited recording may omit pauses, gestures, interruptions, or earlier exchanges. A transcript may merge
voices or normalize an uncertain phrase. Those transformations can be useful, but their locations and
consequences must remain inspectable.
DEFINITION 03-D1. An utterance record u in U contains an immutable identifier, original content or
reference, source interval, observed utterance time, recording time where known, speaker candidate set,
channel, transcription status, and collection provenance. Event time mentioned within the utterance is
stored separately. Analysis time records when an interpretation was entered. Unknown times are
represented as unknown, never copied from a convenient neighboring field.
DEFINITION 03-D2. A claim record is c=(id,phi,utterer,introducer,speech_act,event_time,utterance_tim
e,context,modality,quantifier_scope,source_spans,dependencies,qualifications,corrections,version). The
record stores a proposed interpretation of a communicative event. Its formula phi is not automatically a
hard world premise. Different admissible interpretations may share the same source span while receiving
distinct claim identifiers.
The introducer and utterer can differ. An interviewer may introduce a proposed explanation that a
respondent later accepts. That acceptance may be meaningful, but the history must preserve both acts.
Replacing the sequence with a single spontaneous statement changes the evidentiary account even when
the resulting sentence is grammatically fluent.
EXAMPLE 03.A
An investigator asks, "Was the warning ignored because production was late?" A worker answers, "Yes,
production was late; I did not see the warning." Three records are needed: the investigator's proposed
causal explanation, the worker's affirmation of delay, and the worker's denial of seeing the warning. A
compressed note reading "worker admitted ignoring warning due to delay" changes both action and
causation. Result: the source spans support the separate claims, while the compressed causal claim fails a
provenance-preserving paraphrase check.
03.2 Versions without historical erasure
DEFINITION 03-D3. A derivation edge d=(inputs,output,operation,assumptions,operator,time) records a
transformation such as transcription, translation, excerpting, paraphrase, annotation, or inference. A
version graph preserves previous outputs and identifies superseding versions. Supersession changes the
active interpretation; it does not assert that the earlier file never existed or that every conclusion derived
from it remains valid.
OBLIGATION 03-O1. Every normalized quotation points to its original span and declares changes that
affect meaning. Punctuation added to raw captions is an interpretation when it determines question
boundaries or answer attachment. Missing punctuation, an uncertain negation, or an unclear speaker
remains a review item if its alternatives change a material conclusion.
Cryptographic file digests can demonstrate that two byte sequences match or that a file differs from a
recorded version, subject to the digest mechanism's stated assumptions. They cannot establish speaker
identity, recording completeness, truthful content, or lawful acquisition. Authenticity questions require

evidence about the observation process. A perfectly preserved false statement is still a preserved statement
rather than a true event report.
LEMMA 03-L1. Copying a source record through any finite sequence of declared copying
transformations does not create a new origin. Proof: a direct copy inherits its input origin. If every record
after k copying steps inherits the original origin, a further copy inherits the same origin from its input.
Induction gives the claim for every finite k. Independent observations require separately identified
acquisition events; the presence of multiple files alone does not provide them.
EXAMPLE 03.B
A safety memo quotes a witness email, an audit report quotes the memo, and a meeting summary quotes
the report. Each says that a valve was open. Let e be the email origin and m,a,s the derived documents. The
lineage paths are e->m->a->s. Four documents are present, but the stated observation has one recorded
origin. Result: a corroboration count based on document number is rejected. A separate sensor record
could add another origin, although its causal dependence on the same measurement process would still
need assessment.
03.3 Coverage and the meaning of absence
DEFINITION 03-D4. A coverage map assigns each interval of the declared source a disposition:
transcribed, unresolved, nonverbal, unavailable, omitted with reason, or outside scope. A coverage gap is
information about the record. It is not automatically a historical absence of speech or action. The map may
use overlapping channels so that silence in audio and a visible gesture can coexist.
OBLIGATION 03-O2. Absence-based inferences state the expected observation rule. An inference from
"no response appears" to "no response occurred" needs a premise that the observation would capture every
relevant response. An inference from "no entry was found" to "no event occurred" additionally needs
reliable entry creation, retention, retrieval, and a correct search scope. Each component can be tested
separately.
Provenance therefore has two directions. Upstream tracing asks where a conclusion came from.
Downstream tracing asks which conclusions must be reconsidered when a source or interpretation
changes. Both directions matter: a corrected negation can alter many conclusions, while several summaries
may all depend on the same uncertain syllable.
PROPOSITION 03-P1. If every derived record stores all direct dependencies, then dependency traversal
with a visited-node set over a finite graph reaches every ancestor relevant to that declared derivation.
Proof: any ancestor has a finite directed path to the output. Traversal visits its final predecessor, then the
preceding node, and continues until the ancestor is reached. A visited-node set prevents cycles from
causing repeated expansion. This establishes completeness relative to recorded edges; omitted
dependencies remain an annotation failure rather than a traversal failure.
EXAMPLE 03.C
A transcript ends an interviewer question at 10:02 and begins a new topic at 10:07, with no answer written
between. The recording is unavailable. Hypothesis h1 contains silence; h2 contains a nod; h3 contains an
omitted spoken reply. The coverage record marks the interval unresolved. None of these cases is
eliminated by the transcript alone. Result: "no answer is transcribed" is established at the record level,
while refusal, assent, and silence remain distinct historical possibilities.

03.4 Source reliability as an explicit bridge
A source may be dependable for one purpose and weak for another. A clocked access log may reliably
show that a credential was used while failing to identify the person holding it. A transcript may accurately
preserve words while failing to assign a voice. Reliability therefore attaches to a claim type and
observation process, not indiscriminately to an entire document.
An analyst's explanatory note is another provenance-bearing record. It should identify whether it reports an
observation, proposes an interpretation, selects an assumption, or supplies a mathematical consequence.
Revisions can then alter one layer without corrupting another. If a translator changes a verb from "kept" to
"received," the source recording remains fixed, the translation version changes, and the dependent
retention inference becomes stale. This layered procedure also permits disagreement among annotators:
competing interpretations coexist as alternatives until a declared adjudication process or additional
evidence justifies a narrower reading.
OBLIGATION 03-O3. Any bridge from a record to a world premise names its scope, applicability
conditions, exceptions, and review status. A blanket rule asserting that every recorded sentence is true is
inadmissible unless deliberately adopted as a hypothetical assumption and clearly labeled. Even then,
conclusions remain conditional on that assumption. This discipline preserves the value of evidence while
preventing the administrative fact that a claim was recorded from becoming an automatic guarantee of its
content.

CHAPTER 04. FORMULA LANGUAGE, QUANTIFIERS, AND
SCOPES
04.1 A formula has more than a sentence label
The logical scaffold begins with a controlled language whose symbols have declared meanings. The
purpose is not to translate every nuance into Boolean letters. It is to expose distinctions whose
disappearance would change an inference. Actor, action, object, event time, context, modality, and
quantifier range often carry more significance than the main verb. A short paraphrase can preserve
vocabulary while changing all of these relationships.
DEFINITION 04-D1. A signature Sigma specifies entity types, constant symbols, predicate signatures,
function signatures where used, and temporal or contextual parameters. Well-formed atomic formulas use
arguments of the required types. Compound formulas are built with NOT, AND, OR, ->, <->, and declared
quantifiers. Additional modal or temporal operators require their own semantic adapter; their labels alone
do not supply interpretation.
DEFINITION 04-D2. An atomic proposition identifier p_i denotes a fixed typed formula with fixed actor,
predicate, arguments, context, and event-time scope. If any of those components changes, a new
proposition identifier is required. A proved equivalence can connect the formulations through a
documented alias or translation, but does not silently mutate the original identifier. Syntactic atomicity
means no logical connective appears at the selected representational level. It does not mean psychological
simplicity or complete knowledge of the event.
EXAMPLE 04.A
A maintenance lead asks whether a worker "received the diagram." The worker agrees. A summary says
the worker "downloaded, reviewed, and retained the diagram." Let r,d,v,k represent receipt, deliberate
downloading, review, and retention of the same identified document. The answer supports an assent to r.
Without added premises, r does not entail d AND v AND k. A countermodel sets r true and the other atoms
false, representing an automatically delivered unopened attachment. Result: the summary introduces three
additional predicates requiring separate support.
04.2 Quantifier order and witness dependence
DEFINITION 04-D3. A bound variable ranges over a stated domain, possibly restricted by a predicate.
The formula forall x in D exists y in E R(x,y) permits the witness y to depend on x. The formula exists y in
E forall x in D R(x,y) requires one common witness. A claim record preserves quantifier order,
restrictions, and witness dependencies rather than merely recording the words "all" and "someone."
LEMMA 04-L1. With ordinary semantics, exists y forall x R(x,y) entails forall x exists y R(x,y), but the
reverse is not generally valid. Proof: a common witness works for each x, giving the forward implication.
For the reverse counterexample, take D={a,b}, E={c,d}, and only R(a,c) and R(b,d) true. Each x has a
witness, but no y works for both. The counterexample shows why merging locally available witnesses can
create an unsupported common actor.
EXAMPLE 04.B
An administrator says, "Every package was approved by a manager." An interviewer summarizes, "One
manager approved all the packages." Let packages be p1,p2 and managers be m1,m2. The original formula
is forall p exists m Approved(m,p). A permitted model has m1 approving p1 and m2 approving p2. The
summary instead requires exists m forall p Approved(m,p), which that model falsifies. Result: a shared
managerial role does not create one shared witness, and each approval retains its local person dependency.

04.3 Negation and restricted domains
DEFINITION 04-D4. Quantified scope is part of a proposition's meaning. NOT forall x P(x) means at
least one domain member fails P, under the declared classical semantics. Forall x NOT P(x) means every
domain member fails P. Neither "not all" nor "not always" may be strengthened to "none" or "never"
without additional information.
Domain emptiness requires explicit treatment. A universal statement over an empty domain is classically
true, while an existential statement is false. Consequently, "every missing form was checked" does not
establish that a form was missing. Presuppositions of ordinary usage may suggest existence, but those
suggestions must be represented separately from the truth conditions of a bare universal formula.
OBLIGATION 04-O1. An annotation states domain boundaries and existential commitments. "All
messages" must indicate the account, period, message type, and inspected collection. "No relevant file"
must indicate relevance criteria and search scope. A later broadening of any boundary creates a new claim
and requires a coverage argument. Counts and probabilities additionally require a counting unit and
denominator.
Natural-language statements can also remain intentionally open. "Perhaps a manager signed" may express
possibility rather than assert a signer. The formula should retain the modal qualification. Stripping it
before reasoning changes the premise set. A system unable to interpret a modal expression should preserve
an unresolved annotation rather than force the phrase into an unqualified Boolean fact.
EXAMPLE 04.C
A worker states, "Not every warning appeared on the control screen." A reviewer records, "No warning
appeared." Let the relevant warnings be w1,w2,w3. The first statement is NOT forall w Displayed(w); the
second is forall w NOT Displayed(w). A model displaying w1 and w2 but not w3 satisfies the first and
falsifies the second. Result: one missing warning defeats universal display but does not establish universal
absence. If the warning set itself is uncertain, its membership becomes another declared case dimension.
04.4 Conservative vocabulary and explicit bridges
Predicates such as Received, Saved, Opened, Recognized, Intended, Authorized, and Caused should
remain distinct. Their relationships may be constrained by technical or institutional facts, but those facts
belong in H with sources and exceptions. A device may save an attachment automatically. A person may
recognize an item later without recognizing it at receipt. An authorization may permit an action without
causing it.
PROPOSITION 04-P1. A type-preserving, capture-avoiding renaming preserves satisfaction when its
interpretation and every occurrence are transformed consistently. Proof: each atomic formula receives the
same domain relation under the renamed symbol, and each connective or quantifier receives unchanged
inputs and bindings. Induction on formula construction therefore preserves truth. A replacement that
changes interpretation, scope, or only selected occurrences is not covered by this proof.
This proposition supports readable aliases without allowing semantic drift. The display label "transfer" can
abbreviate a precisely defined transmission relation. It cannot shift between copying, sending, ownership
change, and physical delivery inside one proof. The dictionary accompanying the atlas supplies definitions
and links each label to its exact formula.

04.5 Interface obligations
OBLIGATION 04-O2. When topic cubes exchange a proposition, their interface map preserves its
signature, actor identity, object identity, time parameters, variable bindings, and assumptions. A formula
imported under a different local vocabulary requires a translation and a stated preservation claim.
Unproved translations remain candidates rather than shared-center facts.
The language consequently grows by explicit addition of distinctions and checked connections. Formal
elegance is useful only when the resulting formulas still express the intended claims. A perfectly valid
proof of a mistranslated proposition remains a proof about the wrong proposition; the annotation and
semantic interface checks are therefore indispensable parts of the mathematical scaffold.
Finite grounding requires another explicit boundary. Replacing "every authorized employee" with a
conjunction over five listed names is valid only when those names exhaust the relevant domain, or when
the query is deliberately restricted to them. If an unlisted employee remains possible, the grounded
conjunction represents a narrower statement. A finite implementation can still analyze it accurately, but its
result must preserve the restricted scope. Domain completeness is therefore a premise subject to audit, not
a consequence of the convenience of listing a small number of individuals.

CHAPTER 05. MODELS, TRUTH, AND EVIDENCE SUPPORT
05.1 Two different questions
An evidence ledger answers whether material supports or opposes a proposition under a stated assessment.
A model analysis answers whether a proposition holds in every, some, or none of the interpretations
satisfying declared hard premises. These questions interact, but they are not identical. A credible report
can support a proposition without logically forcing it. Contradictory reports can coexist in the record even
when the underlying event cannot have occurred in both described ways.
DEFINITION 05-D1. For a declared semantics and hard premises H, M(H) is the family of models
satisfying H. In a finite Boolean implementation with n world atoms, a model is an assignment in {0,1}^n
satisfying every encoded premise. Speech-record atoms can be represented separately, so Assert(s,p) and p
have distinct coordinates. A bridge between them is added only when explicitly justified or adopted as a
labeled assumption.
DEFINITION 05-D2. For a query phi, define M_plus={m in M(H):m |= phi} and M_minus={m in
M(H):m |= NOT phi}. If M(H) is empty, status(phi)=INCONSISTENT. Otherwise phi is ENTAILED
when M_minus is empty, REFUTED when M_plus is empty, and UNDETERMINED when both are
nonempty. These labels describe truth across the declared family, not authenticity or guilt.
EXAMPLE 05.A
A clerk reports that a cabinet was locked. The immutable record establishes
Assert(clerk,Locked(cabinet,t)). No observation rule guarantees the report's accuracy. One model contains
a locked cabinet and another an unlocked cabinet with the same report. Both satisfy the speech premise.
Result: the report's occurrence is ENTAILED while Locked(cabinet,t) is UNDETERMINED. If a reliable
mechanical record is later adopted as a hard premise, the world classification may change through that
explicit addition.
05.2 Support without accidental truth semantics
DEFINITION 05-D3. Evidence support is e(phi)=(e_plus,e_minus), where each component indicates the
presence of qualifying positive or negative support under a declared policy. The four labels are
NEITHER=(0,0), POSITIVE_ONLY=(1,0), NEGATIVE_ONLY=(0,1), and BOTH=(1,1). Support entries
retain their sources, scope, and assessment rationale. These pairs are not automatically four truth values
and do not prescribe connective truth tables.
A proposition can have POSITIVE_ONLY support while remaining UNDETERMINED across M(H). It
can have BOTH support while world premises still consistently entail one side, because conflicting
statements are facts about reports rather than automatically conflicting world premises. It can be
ENTAILED by structural premises while receiving no direct witness report. A table that merges these
dimensions would conceal how a conclusion was reached.
OBLIGATION 05-O1. A report displays evidence status and model status separately whenever both are
used. It states which ledger entries entered H, which remained defeasible support, and which bridge rules
justified that promotion. Labels such as "established" must identify their intended layer. An observed
assertion may be established as an utterance while its embedded content remains disputed.


PAGE 017 OF 108

EXAMPLE 05.B
One employee reports that an alarm sounded at noon; another reports that it did not. Let a mean the alarm
sounded during the specified interval. E gives e(a)=BOTH. H contains the two assertion records but
neither embedded world claim. M(H) still includes models with a and with NOT a, so a is
UNDETERMINED rather than logically inconsistent. If both a and NOT a are mistakenly inserted into
classical H, M(H) becomes empty. Result: the contradiction belongs either to testimony or to hard
premises, depending on an explicit modeling decision.
05.3 Why the nonempty requirement is structural
PROPOSITION 05-P1. For a nonempty classical model family, exactly one of ENTAILED, REFUTED,
and UNDETERMINED applies to a Boolean query. Proof: every model satisfies exactly one of phi and
NOT phi, so M_plus and M_minus are disjoint and their union is M(H). Because that union is nonempty,
at least one part is nonempty. The three possibilities are therefore only-plus, only-minus, or both; they are
mutually exclusive and exhaustive.
The proof depends on the declared classical semantics. A system adopting another logic must provide the
corresponding classification rules rather than borrowing these labels without their assumptions. The empty
family is treated separately because both parts would then be empty. Calling that situation entailed and
refuted simultaneously would obscure an inconsistent premise set behind a misleading appearance of
certainty.
OBLIGATION 05-O2. Consistency is checked before skeptical classification. If H is inconsistent, the
diagnostic result identifies conflicting constraints or a bounded conflict set and suspends ordinary world
conclusions. Alternative premise repairs remain distinct unless an evidentiary decision selects one.
Deleting an inconvenient premise without recording the change is not a logical repair procedure.
05.4 Shared center and local witnesses
DEFINITION 05-D4. The center theory C(H) consists of permitted query formulas true in every model of
a declared nonempty M(H). Case-specific formulas remain in their local blocks. An existential formula in
C(H) need not have one individual witnessing it in every model. Witness dependence remains visible on
the -y side of the atlas, while proof scope and origins remain on +y. [SRC-PREF]
EXAMPLE 05.C
A cabinet can be opened only by credential A or credential B, and a reliable mechanism record establishes
that it was opened by one of them. No record identifies which credential was used. The center contains
Used(A) OR Used(B), or equivalently an existential claim over the two credentials. One retained model
uses A and another uses B. Neither Used(A) nor Used(B) belongs to the center. Result: existence of an
opener is established within the model, while selecting a representative credential supplies no new
evidence about the actual opener.
05.5 Strengthening and observational equivalence
PROPOSITION 05-P2. If H is strengthened to H'=H union K and both model families are nonempty,
every formula entailed by H remains entailed by H'. Proof: every model satisfying H' satisfies H, hence
M(H') subseteq M(H). A formula true throughout the larger family is true throughout its nonempty subset.
A formerly unresolved formula may become resolved, but only through the added constraints and their
declared justification.
This monotonic property does not describe retractions, changed meanings, or source corrections; those
produce revised specifications and need fresh checks. Adding vocabulary alone can also preserve all old


PAGE 018 OF 108

possibilities without strengthening any old conclusion. A more elaborate display is therefore not evidence
of progress unless it adds distinctions, verifies reasoning, or introduces justified information.
Two models can agree on every observed record while disagreeing about a focal event. That observational
equivalence is a useful finding: it identifies what the current evidence cannot distinguish. An effective
follow-up seeks an observation predicted differently by the surviving models. The mathematics locates the
missing distinction; obtaining trustworthy evidence remains an empirical task.
OBLIGATION 05-O3. Model families declare whether unnamed possibilities are excluded, represented
by an explicit other-case category, or left outside the analysis. A binary atom such as "A opened the
cabinet" partitions the represented possibilities into A and not-A, but not-A may contain many different
actors and mechanisms. The negative side must not be relabeled "B opened it" unless additional
constraints establish that equivalence. A closed list of suspects or causes is a substantive assumption
whenever it excludes a plausible unlisted alternative.
The cardinality of a surviving case set is also not a probability by itself. Splitting one case into ten
descriptively finer cases does not make its parent ten times more likely. Model counting supports
probability only after a measure over models, its interpretation, and its invariance under representation
changes are justified. Until then, one countermodel is sufficient to defeat entailment regardless of how
many other models support the proposed conclusion.

CHAPTER 06. INFERENCE, PROOF OBLIGATIONS, AND
COUNTERMODELS
06.1 Every transition needs a rule
An interrogation analysis contains many transitions that sound natural but are not logically automatic:
receipt becomes possession, a suggested explanation becomes an admission, a repeated claim becomes
corroboration, or a local estimate becomes a general preference. The appropriate response is to expose the
transition as an inference candidate. Its premises, rule, assumptions, and conclusion can then be examined
without presuming either deception or innocence.
DEFINITION 06-D1. A proof step is
d=(id,premises,conclusion,rule,assumptions,scope,source_links,verification). Premises reference stable
proposition or claim identifiers. The rule supplies a semantic condition under which those premises justify
the conclusion. Verification records whether the step has been checked, rejected with a witness, left
unproved, or found inapplicable. A sequence or graph of such steps is a proof record, not merely a fluent
explanation.
OBLIGATION 06-O1. No inference may silently change speaker, actor, object, time interval, modality,
quantifier scope, denominator, or evidence layer. A change can be legitimate, but it requires an explicit
bridge. When the bridge is defeasible, its assumptions and defeat conditions remain attached to the
conclusion. The ledger distinguishes a proposed rule from an adopted hard rule.
EXAMPLE 06.A
A supervisor reasons, "Every authorized shutdown has a signed entry. There is no signed entry. Therefore
the shutdown was unauthorized." Let a mean authorized shutdown and s mean signed entry for that same
event. H contains a->s and NOT s, which entail NOT a. The inference is valid by contraposition. Its
applicability still depends on immediate entry creation, complete search coverage, and shared event
identity. Result: the formal step can pass while the premises remain evidentially contestable; logical
validity and premise reliability receive separate findings.
06.2 Soundness of elementary propagation
DEFINITION 06-D2. A rule is sound for a semantics when every model satisfying its premises satisfies
its conclusion. Soundness concerns all models in the rule's intended class. A finite fixture that passes
demonstrates behavior on that fixture; it does not by itself prove uniform soundness over unrestricted
future inputs.
LEMMA 06-L1. From phi and phi->psi, inference to psi is sound in classical semantics. Proof: suppose a
model satisfies phi and phi->psi. An implication is false only when its antecedent is true and consequent
false. Since the antecedent is true and the implication true, the consequent cannot be false. Therefore the
model satisfies psi.
PROPOSITION 06-P1. A finite derivation using only sound rules from premises H produces only
consequences of H. Proof: initial nodes are satisfied by every model of H. Assume all earlier nodes are
satisfied by every such model. Soundness of the rule at the next node makes its conclusion true in every
such model. Induction over the finite derivation establishes the claim. A cyclic dependency graph is not
covered merely by drawing arrows; it needs an independent semantics or an acyclic proof expansion.
This result establishes the discipline of local checking. Every step can be inspected separately, and a
verified derivation can be reused while its premises and meanings remain unchanged. Reuse fails when an


PAGE 020 OF 108

identifier keeps its label but changes its underlying scope. The version and interface records prevent that
hidden substitution.
EXAMPLE 06.B
A reviewer states, "If the document was signed, it entered the archive. It entered the archive, so it was
signed." Let s mean signed and a mean archived. H={s->a,a}. The assignment s=false,a=true satisfies H
and falsifies s; an unsigned document may have been archived by mistake. Result: the candidate inference
fails through an explicit countermodel. The same conclusion might become valid if an additional rule a->s
were independently justified, but that rule cannot be borrowed from the failed inference itself.
06.3 Countermodels as diagnostic objects
DEFINITION 06-D3. A countermodel to H |= phi is a model m satisfying H AND NOT phi. A certificate
includes m, the interpretation of relevant symbols, the premises checked, and a readable account of the
distinguishing case. It disproves the proposed entailment within the declared semantics. It does not prove
that the represented alternative actually occurred.
OBLIGATION 06-O2. A negative entailment result contains a countermodel whenever the checking
method can produce one. An unresolved result contains both a model satisfying phi and a model satisfying
NOT phi. Failure to find a proof within a resource limit is reported as an incomplete search, not as a
countermodel or a refutation. The distinction prevents computational exhaustion from becoming an
evidentiary conclusion.
Countermodels should preserve the features under dispute. A model that changes an event date, person
identity, or source scope without acknowledging it may refute a different claim. Useful certificates
therefore include the precise proposition keys and interface mappings. The reader can trace the alternative
from the southern case block through dependencies to the northern conclusion.
06.4 Assumptions, defeats, and conditional conclusions
DEFINITION 06-D4. A conditional proof record H;A |= phi identifies a separate assumption set A. Its
conclusion may enter a local case containing A, but it enters the shared center only if the assumptions are
themselves established throughout the retained family or the conclusion is independently proved without
them. A defeasible edge additionally records conditions that withdraw its operational applicability.
EXAMPLE 06.C
An interviewer proposes, "Assuming the roster was current, only the night team could have entered." Let r
mean current roster, e mean entry occurred, and n mean entry by the night team. H contains (r AND e)->n
and e. A local proof under A={r} yields n. Another retained case has NOT r and entry by an unlisted
contractor. Result: n belongs to the roster-current case, while the center retains only the conditional
connection. Repeating the conditional conclusion later without r would be an unsupported strengthening.
06.5 Minimal explanations and proof maintenance
A proof can contain unnecessary premises. Removing unused premises improves clarity but does not
automatically produce a unique minimum explanation. Several independent derivations may establish the
same conclusion, and different assumptions may yield it through different routes. The atlas retains
alternatives when they matter to robustness or future correction.
Proof discovery and proof checking also have different roles. An exploratory search may guess a useful
intermediate statement, choose a promising case split, or prioritize an apparent contradiction. These
choices are permitted without pretending that the guessed statement is established. The checker then
examines the submitted derivation using declared rules. A persuasive search narrative cannot replace the
certificate, while a valid certificate need not reproduce every abandoned exploratory route. The report

preserves unsuccessful routes when they reveal material limitations, hidden assumptions, or unresolved
rival explanations rather than treating them as irrelevant simply because another route succeeded.
PROPOSITION 06-P2. If H |= phi and H subseteq J, then J |= phi, provided entailment is interpreted
classically; operational classification additionally requires nonempty M(J). Proof: any model of J is a
model of H and therefore satisfies phi. The extra nonempty check prevents a contradictory enlargement
from being reported as a successful evidentiary strengthening. A conclusion's validity can persist formally
while the whole analysis becomes unusable through inconsistency.
OBLIGATION 06-O3. When a premise changes, all dependent proofs are marked stale until revalidated.
A correction can preserve a conclusion through another independent derivation, but that surviving route
must be recorded. The final report distinguishes verified consequence, supported hypothesis, refuted
inference, unresolved alternative, inconsistent premises, and unfinished computation. This vocabulary
makes the reasoning's exact achievement visible without collapsing every uncertainty into one generic
warning.

CHAPTER 07. SPEECH ACTS, EFFECTS, AND ATTRIBUTED
INTENTION
07.1 Records before interpretation
A spoken sentence can describe an event, request an answer, challenge a prior statement, grant permission,
or perform several functions together. Its wording, conversational function, observed effect, and suspected
purpose require separate records. The atlas stores the immutable utterance u in U before assigning any of
these interpretations. A speaker hypothesis belongs to the entity interface; an act hypothesis belongs to the
speech interface. Neither overwrites the original string. This separation matters because an apparently
declarative sentence can function as a question, and a formally interrogative sentence can communicate an
accusation without obtaining an answer.
DEFINITION 07-D1. An act annotation is a=(u, s, phi, k, c, tau, sigma), where u is its source record, s the
attributed speaker, phi the scoped content, k the proposed act kind, c the conversational context, tau the
utterance time, and sigma the annotation status. The context includes the event-time scope of phi. Possible
kinds include Assert, Deny, Ask, Request, Propose, Summarize, Challenge, and AcceptForDiscussion. An
uncertain classification creates alternative annotations, rather than a fictitious blended act.
The locution is the recoverable wording and its candidate literal content. The illocution is the action
proposed to be performed through that wording, such as asserting or requesting. The perlocutionary effect
is a subsequent effect attributed to the utterance, such as an answer, correction, silence, or change of topic.
Causal attribution remains additional work: temporal succession alone does not establish that the utterance
produced the effect. Intention is another variable, especially when the proposed purpose is to mislead,
reassure, intimidate, or obtain agreement.
07.2 Commitments are real records with nonfactive content
DEFINITION 07-D2. Commitment(s,phi,c,tau) records a communicative commitment attributed to s in
context c at tau. It does not mean phi holds in the world. A clear assertion can justify adding an assertion
record and, under a declared conversational rule, a commitment record. A quotation, rehearsal,
hypothetical, or ambiguous reply requires its own act classification before that rule applies.
LEMMA 07-L1. No general inference from an assertion record alone to its content is valid. For
contingent phi independent of record constraints, the assertion does not entail phi. More generally, adding
a record-only assertion cannot remove a preexisting NOT phi world when the annotation is conservative.
Proof for the isolated record theory: construct two models with the same assertion record and context,
assigning phi true in one and false in the other. Both satisfy that record theory, so its shared center includes
the assertion occurrence, but neither phi nor NOT phi. With additional premises, the conclusion is only
that conservative annotation preserves their world possibilities; those premises may independently
establish phi. Sincerity alone does not add factivity, because sincerity concerns belief and belief is
separately nonfactive.

EXAMPLE 07.A
An invented museum exchange contains curator C saying, "The west cabinet remains unlocked," followed
by attendant A locking it. Source U07A records both turns and the later action. Let p07A mean
Unlocked(cabinet_west) during inspection interval t0 in museum context c07A. The sentence supports an
Assert(C,p07A) annotation; a Request(C,Lock(cabinet_west)) annotation is also plausible if an established
work convention supplies that interpretation. The observed locking is compatible with compliance,
independent inspection, or an already planned task. Result: the atlas can record the assertion and
subsequent action while leaving the request interpretation and causal effect on separate southern branches.
Neither the act classification nor the later lock proves that the cabinet was initially unlocked.
07.3 Multiple functions without automatic accusation
One utterance can contain a question and an embedded assertion. "The seal is broken; when did the parcel
arrive?" separates naturally into an asserted seal condition and a requested arrival time. The source span
for each is retained. Conversely, "The seal is broken?" can be a polar question, an incredulous repetition,
or a request to confirm another person's observation. Punctuation added by a transcriber does not settle
prosody. An act lattice or candidate set can represent these alternatives without assigning unjustified
numerical confidence.
OBLIGATION 07-O1. Every conclusion about a speaker's intention must identify its supporting
behavior, alternative explanations, and bridge assumptions. The presence of a leading form establishes a
feature of the form. It does not, by itself, establish deliberate deception. Successful influence also differs
from intended influence: an unsuccessful attempt may still have an intention, and an unplanned influence
may still have an effect.
EXAMPLE 07.B
An invented shop manager says, "I am sorry the delivery caused trouble," after a customer reports a

Let p07B mean Missing(component_k,d7,t1), while q07B means TroubleReported(customer,d7,t1). An
apology may acknowledge inconvenience or accept responsibility, but the sentence does not uniquely
assert p07B. A later summary stating "M admitted losing the component" changes predicate, agency, and
commitment. Result: q07B can be linked to the complaint record; responsibility for loss requires an
additional supported act or world premise. A socially accommodating apology remains meaningful
without carrying every allegation nested in the complaint.
07.4 Dynamic context and the boundary of acceptance
Conversation changes what participants can reasonably treat as available for further discussion. Let C_tau
be the annotated conversational state immediately before an utterance, and let Update(C_tau,a) be a
declared update rule. A proposal can add a pending issue; a denial can mark opposition; acceptance can
provisionally close an issue. These operations concern the conversational state. They cannot silently
replace the separate model family M(H) describing possible events.
PROPOSITION 07-P1. A conversational update is conservative with respect to world conclusions if its
new predicates concern only acts and commitments, its added record theory has a satisfying interpretation
independent of world valuations, and no new bridge restricts those valuations. Proof: combine each old
world model with that satisfying record interpretation while preserving its old world assignment.
Consequently every formerly permitted world remains represented. If a reliability or stipulation bridge is
added, this proof no longer applies; that addition must be logged as evidential strengthening rather than
mere annotation.

An explicit, well-attached affirmative answer may genuinely communicate acceptance of the proposition

07.5 Atlas routing and audit output

annotations and the reasons for resolution. A useful output reports act, commitment, effect, and intention

CHAPTER 08. QUESTIONS, PRESUPPOSITION, AND
ACCOMMODATION
08.1 A question contains more than an answer slot
A question can request one fact while treating another as already available. "When was the lock replaced?"
requests a time and presents replacement as background. The analyst must preserve both structures. An
answer giving a time may communicate acceptance of replacement; a request for clarification, refusal,
correction, or response to a different clause may not. The wording alone cannot establish that replacement
occurred, that the respondent accepted it, or that the questioner deliberately manipulated the exchange. The
relevant task is to identify the proposed background, its source, and its later use.
DEFINITION 08-D1. A question is Q=(id,u,s,c,tau,B,A,V,G), where u is the source, s the questioner, c
the context including event scope, tau the utterance time, B the candidate background propositions, A the
answer slots, V the offered alternatives, and G the conditions governing interpretation. B may contain
asserted information, conventional presuppositions, pragmatic suggestions, or analyst-inferred
assumptions. These categories require separate tags rather than an undifferentiated list of facts.
For every b in B, record IntroducedBy(b), PriorSupport(b), TargetScope(b), and ProjectionMode(b).
Projection describes whether a background assumption is interpreted globally or only inside a local
supposition. A proposition already established independently is different from one appearing for the first
time in a question. The source of the background remains the question even if later speakers repeat it. This
structure implements the source-sensitive accommodation checks requested by the framework.
[SRC-PRESUP]
08.2 Accommodation is a family of possible updates
DEFINITION 08-D2. Accommodation is an annotated update in which a participant proceeds with a
proposition as usable background despite incomplete prior establishment in the conversational record.
Distinguish AcceptForDiscussion(s,b,c), an attributed public commitment to b, and Believe(s,b,c). None is
automatically identical to another. Accommodation may coexist with sincere belief, explicit acceptance,
temporary cooperation, uncertainty, or disagreement suppressed for a local task.
There is therefore no valid universal rule that accommodation excludes acceptance. There is equally no
valid universal rule that continued conversation establishes acceptance of every background proposition.
The actual response, the conversational task, and later corrections determine which updates are supported.
Silence is especially underspecified: the transcript may omit a gesture, the speaker may await a turn, or the
question may have several unresolved components.
LEMMA 08-L1. Presuppose(Q,b) together with AnswerTo(r,Q) does not entail b without a rule
specifying the content and interpretation of r. Proof: let r be an explicit rejection, "No replacement
occurred." Then the premises concerning question structure and response linkage hold while b is false.
Even restricting r to an apparently cooperative answer does not establish world truth, because a sincerely
mistaken respondent supplies a countermodel.

EXAMPLE 08.A
In an invented repair-shop exchange, inspector I asks when clock k was repaired. Owner O answers, "It

08.3 Background can be carried across several turns
A recurrent failure occurs when an initially tentative premise is reused until its introduction becomes hard
to see. At tau1 a questioner proposes b; at tau2 a respondent explains what would follow if b; at tau3 the
questioner summarizes the explanation; at tau4 b is reported as confirmed. The diagnostic is not repetition
itself. It is the absence of a licensed transition from conditional or discussion-level use to an assertion of b.
OBLIGATION 08-O1. Before a background proposition enters the shared center as a world conclusion,
require either explicit hard-premise adoption with stated justification or a proof from existing H. Before it
enters as a respondent commitment, require a supported act interpretation and response attachment. These
are separate gates. Passing the commitment gate does not pass the world gate.
EXAMPLE 08.B
An invented clinic caller C asks, "Since patient P has left, is the discharge form delayed?" Clerk K says,
"Forms sometimes reach the desk later." Source U08B fixes desk lookup time tB, patient identifier p4, and
clinic context c08B. Let p08B mean Departed(p4,by_tB), q08B mean FormDelayed(p4,tB), and g08B
mean SomeFormsArriveLate(clinic,c08B). K's sentence supports an assertion of g08B. It neither singles
out p4 nor supplies q08B or p08B. Result: a later report that the clinic confirmed discharge would require
unsupported changes of individual scope and predicate. If K instead explicitly confirmed departure after
checking a record, that additional answer would be retained as a meaningful endorsement with its own
source and limits.
08.4 Local supposition versus global commitment
Background introduced inside a hypothetical must remain inside its scope unless an independent premise
supports projection. "If the parcel arrived damaged, which carrier handled it?" can inquire about a
conditional scenario without asserting damage. The clause may nevertheless influence later discussion.
The atlas represents the hypothetical branch explicitly and preserves the alternative branch, rather than
treating the conditional's antecedent as a center fact.
PROPOSITION 08-P1. In classical semantics, adding b -> r does not by itself justify adding b. Proof: a
model assigning b false satisfies b -> r regardless of r. Consequently the southern branch NOT b remains
permitted unless another premise excludes it. A conversation may pragmatically suggest b, but that
suggestion requires a separate non-deductive annotation and cannot alter this logical result.
EXAMPLE 08.C
An invented transit inquiry states, "Assuming the bridge was closed at six, which detour would the bus
take?" Dispatcher D replies, "The eastern road." Source U08C binds bridge b2, route r9, date dC, and
traffic-planning context c08C. Let p08C mean Closed(b2,18:00,dC), and q08C mean Detour(r9,east,dC).
The supported commitment is conditional on p08C, subject to the planning convention. Both an actually
open bridge and an actually closed bridge remain compatible with the answer. Result: the route cube
receives a conditional dependency; the closure cube retains both signed branches. A later unconditional
closure report would fail the scope-preservation check.

08.5 Neutrality and repair as measurable structures
A question may be evaluated structurally without inferring a hidden motive. Relevant properties include
the number of unsupported background propositions, whether correction is permitted, whether alternatives
are explicit, whether several commitments are bundled, and whether the later recap preserves the response.
These are audit features, not a universal numerical manipulation score. The vocabulary of "safe,"
"obvious," or "surely" can encourage an expected answer, but no individual word supplies a psychological
diagnosis.
The question cube's interfaces therefore carry background propositions, answer slots, local assumptions,
and repair events separately. The northern checks search for origin loss, unjustified projection, and
conversion of general statements into individual facts. The positive provenance wing records the exact
introducing span and every subsequent transformation. The final report can identify an inserted premise,
describe its possible conversational effect, and state whether acceptance is established, disputed, or
unresolved. It should also record successful correction, because an accommodated premise later
withdrawn must not remain silently active downstream.

CHAPTER 09. ANSWER ATTACHMENT, ELLIPSIS, AND
COMPOUND AGREEMENT
09.1 A response requires a target
An answer has evidentiary meaning only after its target and scope are identified. A short "yes" can answer
the latest question, accept a requested procedure, acknowledge hearing, or endorse one clause within a
longer formulation. An interrupted exchange may contain multiple live questions. Transcription
punctuation can obscure these possibilities, and a subsequent summary can accidentally attach an answer
to words spoken afterward. The response cube therefore stores candidate attachment edges rather than
assuming adjacency always determines meaning.
DEFINITION 09-D1. A response linkage is L=(r,Q,a,c,tau,kind,grounds,status), where r is an immutable
source response, Q its proposed target question, a its answer-slot interpretation, c the conversation and
event scope, tau the response time, kind the linkage relation, and grounds the supporting sequence,
wording, or verified nonverbal record. Kinds include answers, acknowledges, corrects, refuses,
requests_clarification, and resumes. Linkage uncertainty remains explicit.
The candidate targets form a finite relation between response records and question records. One response
may answer several questions, and one question may require several responses. A one-to-one matching
algorithm would therefore impose a false structure unless the task specifically guarantees it. Timing,
lexical repetition, grammatical compatibility, turn allocation, and explicit reference can constrain the
relation. A heuristic ranking is not a proof that the highest-ranked target is correct.
09.2 Temporal attachment is necessary but insufficient
OBLIGATION 09-O1. A retrospective answer must target an already available question or proposition,
unless the record supplies an explicit anticipatory convention or shared prior formulation. Ordinary
answers cannot be silently assigned to later propositions merely because those propositions appear in the
same paragraph of a transcript. When timestamps are coarse, preserve the verified within-turn order or
record order as uncertain.
LEMMA 09-L1. If r occurs before proposition p is introduced, and the declared interpretation rules permit
only retrospective attachment to previously available content, then r cannot constitute an answer to that
introduction of p. Proof: an allowed target must belong to the content set available immediately before r.
By hypothesis p's introduction does not belong to that set. Hence the proposed attachment violates the
target-domain condition. The lemma concerns the specified introduction, not the possibility that equivalent
content existed earlier.
EXAMPLE 09.A
In an invented committee exchange, chair C asks, "Was the maintenance budget reviewed?" Secretary S
answers, "Yes." C then adds, "And the emergency purchase was approved." Source U09A preserves that
order in meeting context c09A on date dA. Let p09A mean Reviewed(budget_m,dA), and q09A mean
Approved(purchase_e,dA). The response can support S's endorsement of p09A. It does not endorse the
later q09A without an additional linkage source. Result: a recap reporting both as confirmed by S fails the
ordering check. A prior question explicitly asking both would create a different attachment candidate and
would have to be cited separately.

09.3 Compound questions and partial assent
DEFINITION 09-D2. A compound polar question has proposed content phi = p1 AND ... AND pn, where
each pi retains its own actor, predicate, arguments, source introduction, and event scope. A whole-question
affirmative interpretation attributes a commitment to phi. A partial interpretation targets a proper subset or
a different clause. Ambiguity between these interpretations must survive until resolved.
PROPOSITION 09-P1. Under a uniquely established whole-question affirmative interpretation,
commitment to a conjunction licenses attributed commitment to each conjunct according to the declared
commitment-closure rule. Proof: the semantic content accepted is the conjunction; each conjunct is part of
that accepted content. This proves an attribution under the adopted conversational rule, not the historical
truth of any conjunct. Without the whole-question interpretation, the inference fails because a model in
which assent targets only p1 preserves the response record while omitting commitment to p2.
EXAMPLE 09.B
An invented courier interview asks, "Was the parcel received intact and signed for by the tenant?" Clerk K
answers, "Yes, it was received." Source U09B fixes parcel p6, delivery interval tB, and building context
c09B. The proposed conjunction contains Received(p6,tB), Intact(p6,tB), and SignedBy(tenant,p6,tB).
The explicit elaboration identifies receipt; it does not clearly resolve condition or signature. Result: receipt
endorsement is supported, while the remaining components require clarification. If K instead says, "Yes to
all three: received, intact, and signed by the tenant," whole-question assent is directly supported, still
subject to the distinction between commitment and truth.
09.4 Ellipsis preserves hidden arguments
Ellipsis omits wording recoverable from context, but contextual recovery is itself an inference. "Twice"
needs an activity, interval, counting unit, and participant. "Not then" needs a temporal anchor and a
predicate under negation. "Probably" may modify the entire proposition or a particular identification. The
completion must be represented as a candidate formula with its supplying context and any unresolved
alternatives. A fluently reconstructed sentence is not automatically a verified expansion.
DEFINITION 09-D3. Complete(r,Q,c) is a set of candidate scoped contents for an elliptical response. A
unique completion can be used when grammar, explicit context, and source evidence exclude rivals.
Otherwise the response denotes an interpretation set. Skeptical reporting retains only commitments
common to all permitted completions, provided that the set is nonempty. An empty set indicates a failed
interpretation or inconsistent annotation, not confirmation of arbitrary content.
EXAMPLE 09.C
An invented inventory exchange contains auditor A asking whether the annual report was filed during the
first quarter. Officer O replies, "Not yet." Source U09C records the interview in July, with a March filing
deadline. Let p09C mean Filed(report_r,first_quarter,dC), and q09C mean
Filed(report_r,by_interview,dC). "Not yet" ordinarily relates to the present interview, potentially denying
q09C and thereby implying NOT p09C under monotonic filing history. But an incomplete transcript may
preserve a quoted earlier reply from March. Result: the source-time and quotation annotations decide
which completion is admissible. The words alone cannot collapse March knowledge, July status, and the
deadline into one temporal claim.

09.5 Negative questions, repairs, and missing channels
Negative questions create an additional polarity problem. After "The door was not locked?", a bare "yes"
may confirm the negative formulation or contradict it under different conversational conventions. The atlas
should retain the full exchange and any elaboration rather than assign polarity from a universal dictionary.
Repetition of the predicate, such as "It was locked," can resolve the ambiguity more directly.
Cross-language interpretation requires a declared linguistic adapter, because agreement particles do not
share one invariant mapping.
Corrections can attach to content, wording, attribution, time, or the question's relevance. "No, on Monday"
may reject only the date rather than the entire event. A correction edge therefore names its target
component. Missing nods, pauses, overlap, or gestures remain missing evidence unless the recording
supplies them. An annotation of unrecorded assent cannot substitute for observation.
The northern diagnostic sequence checks available targets, permissible polarity, clause scope, completion
alternatives, and temporal order before propagating a commitment. Its counterexample test deliberately
reattaches a short response to another live question and asks whether the available record excludes that
reading. Successful exclusion supports a precise attribution; failure produces a bounded ambiguity. The
final report states what was answered, what remained open, and which confidence came from the response
itself rather than from the interviewer's later recap.

CHAPTER 10. MODALITY, CONDITIONALITY, BELIEF, AND
KNOWLEDGE
10.1 Different operators create different propositions
"The door was open," "the door might have been open," "the guard believed it was open," and "if it was
open, the alarm would sound" express different propositions. Their shared embedded clause does not erase
their different commitments. The atlas assigns separate stable identifiers to each. A report that removes
"might," "believed," or "if" changes the formula and must pass a new inference test. The same discipline
applies to permission, obligation, ability, intention, expectation, and approximate certainty.
DEFINITION 10-D1. A modal record is c=(id,Op,phi,s,u,ctx,t_event,t_utter,parameters), where Op
identifies the operator, phi its typed scoped content, s its attributed holder or speaker, u its source, and
parameters specify the chosen semantics. Distinguish epistemic possibility, practical ability, permission,
prediction, and conversational uncertainty. Identical words can denote different operators, so a language
label alone is not a complete semantic annotation.
For a declared possible-world model, let R_a(w,v) mean v is accessible from w under operator
interpretation a. The clauses below define two standard operators, without claiming that ordinary speech
always uses them exactly:
Possible_a(phi,w) iff exists v: R_a(w,v) AND phi(v). Necessary_a(phi,w) iff forall v: R_a(w,v) -> phi(v).
Accessibility assumptions, including reflexivity or seriality, must be stated. They are semantic premises,
not facts supplied by a diagram. Different agents, times, and information states can require different
relations.
10.2 Possibility and assertion remain separate
LEMMA 10-L1. Possible_a(phi,w) does not entail phi(w) in an unrestricted accessible-world model.
Proof: take actual world w with phi false and an accessible world v with phi true. The possibility clause
holds at w while phi does not. Reflexivity, making w accessible from itself, supplies a general factivity
guarantee: Necessary_a(phi,w) entails phi(w) for arbitrary phi under that semantics. Without this
guarantee, accessible worlds can all satisfy phi while w does not. Other premises can independently
establish particular formulas at w even when accessibility is not reflexive.
EXAMPLE 10.A
In an invented station inquiry, dispatcher D states, "The late train might have used platform four." Source
U10A fixes train t8, arrival evening eA, and dispatch context c10A. Let p10A mean
Used(t8,platform_4,eA). Two retained worlds assign p10A opposite values, while both reproduce D's
uncertainty statement. A later summary claiming a definite platform-four arrival removes the possibility
operator. Result: the source supports an attributed modal assertion. A platform log could separately
establish p10A, but that would be new evidence rather than a clarification of what the modal sentence
already proved.
10.3 Conditional statements do not supply their antecedents
DEFINITION 10-D2. Conditional content is stored as Cond(phi,psi,kind,ctx), where kind distinguishes
material implication, a procedural rule, a causal proposal, and a counterfactual. These kinds cannot be
interchanged without an adapter and justification. The source and event scope of both antecedent and
consequent remain explicit, including dependencies between their variables.
PROPOSITION 10-P1. Under classical material implication, an application of modus ponens to phi ->
psi requires phi as its other premise. Proof: in every retained model, true phi together with phi -> psi


PAGE 032 OF 108

requires true psi. The implication alone does not entail psi: the valuation with false phi and false psi
satisfies the implication. Other premises in H may independently entail psi, even when phi is not
established. Reverse inference from psi and phi -> psi to phi also fails: NOT phi with psi satisfies both
premises. These are countermodels to the isolated inference patterns, not judgments about a speaker's
honesty.
EXAMPLE 10.B
An invented laboratory manager says, "If cabinet c3 was opened after midnight, the access counter should
increase." Technician T reports that the counter increased. Source U10B fixes cabinet c3, night nB, and
laboratory context c10B. Let p10B mean Opened(c3,after_midnight,nB), q10B mean
CounterIncreased(c3,nB), and r10B mean MaintenanceReset(c3,nB). The procedure supplies p10B ->
q10B, while another permitted mechanism supplies r10B -> q10B. The observed counter change is
compatible with opening or reset. Result: opening remains an abductive candidate, not a deductive
conclusion. A proven exclusive mechanism would be an additional premise and would change the model
family transparently.
10.4 Belief attribution and factive knowledge models
An assertion about another person's belief is itself testimony. "The clerk knew the package was damaged"
can communicate the speaker's interpretation of the clerk's mental state; it does not automatically establish
that state. The atlas therefore distinguishes Assert(s,Know(k,phi)) from an internally adopted Know(k,phi)
premise. Even the latter is factive only under the declared epistemic semantics.
OBLIGATION 10-O1. Before using a knowledge attribution to derive phi, identify the adopted factivity
axiom, the evidence supporting actual knowledge rather than alleged knowledge, and the relevant time. A
later admission of awareness cannot automatically establish earlier awareness. A quoted claim of
knowledge remains a speech record unless an explicit reliability bridge promotes its content.
In a standard reflexive knowledge model, Know_a(phi,w) entails phi(w) because the actual world is
among the agent's accessible alternatives. In a nonfactive belief model, Believe_a(phi,w) need not entail
phi(w). These are declared formal interpretations. Ordinary uses of "know," including irony, exaggeration,
mistaken certainty, and reported speech, still need linguistic analysis before entering either formal
operator.
EXAMPLE 10.C
In an invented planning meeting, engineer E says, "I knew one valve was defective." Source U10C fixes
engine g5, inspection period tC, and utterance time tauC. Candidate content includes Know(E,exists v
Defective(v,g5,tC)) and exists v Know(E,Defective(v,g5,tC)). The first allows E to know that some valve
failed without identifying one; the second attributes a particular known defective valve. Two possible
worlds can alternate which valve is defective while each contains a defect. Result: knowledge of an
existential does not generally identify a constant witness. The witness cube must retain world-dependent
valves rather than select one and present the selection as historical knowledge.
10.5 Scope, graded language, and implementation limits
Operator order matters. NOT Possible(phi) differs from Possible(NOT phi); a belief about a future
possibility differs from a current possibility of having a belief. The parser records nesting explicitly.
Quantifiers also cross modal boundaries: a different person may satisfy an existential in each accessible
world. Shared existential support cannot silently become a universally fixed witness. This dependency is
carried through the negative y interface of the relevant cube.
Terms such as "probably," "usually," and "almost certain" may express graded commitments without a
calibrated numerical probability. The atlas can preserve the lexical qualification and its target while


PAGE 033 OF 108

declining to invent a percentage. A probability adapter requires a sample space, event definitions, priors or
reference frequencies, and dependence assumptions. An ordinal confidence tag is not a substitute for those
requirements.
Normative and practical modalities require the same restraint. Permission to open a cabinet does not
establish an opening, ability to retrieve a file does not establish retrieval, and an obligation to report
damage does not establish compliance. Intention concerns a proposed future action and may survive
alongside failure or abandonment. Each operator receives its own source and validity interval. A policy
document can support a permission rule without supporting a particular person's knowledge of that rule.
The resulting connections may be relevant to an inquiry, but every transition from authority, opportunity,
or intention to completed conduct needs an independently justified bridge.
The executable Boolean kernel may treat a modal statement as an annotated atomic record and check that
its operator survives transformations. That does not amount to implementing general modal logic. A richer
adapter must declare worlds, accessibility, satisfaction rules, and search bounds. Reports distinguish a
checked operator-preservation result from a modal validity proof, and distinguish both from a claim that
the model accurately represents a person's actual beliefs.

CHAPTER 11. TEMPORAL ORDER, EVENT TIME, AND
KNOWLEDGE TIME
11.1 Two clocks and additional timestamps
An interrogation concerns events occurring at one time through statements produced at another. These are
the two primary clocks: event time t_event and utterance time t_utter. A statement made on Friday can
concern a Tuesday delivery. Later transcription, ingestion, correction, and discovery introduce further
timestamps that serve different functions. Treating all dates as one sequence can transform later
knowledge into earlier awareness, attach an answer to a future question, or make a delayed entry appear to
be a contemporaneous observation.
DEFINITION 11-D1. A temporal annotation
T=(event_interval,utterance_interval,recording_interval,acquisition_interval,zone,precision,source)
records distinct time roles. Missing components remain unspecified. An interval can represent exactness
limits, such as a minute-rounded timestamp, or genuinely prolonged activity. These reasons receive
different metadata. Context c identifies the calendar, time-zone convention, entities, and event predicates
to which the annotation applies.
A statement's historical content and its availability to an agent require separate predicates. Occurs(e,t)
concerns an event; AvailableTo(s,record,t) concerns access to information; Assert(s,phi,tau) concerns a
speech act. Knowing phi requires more than information availability, since access does not establish
reading, comprehension, acceptance, or truth. Each bridge must be declared rather than folded into a
timestamp label.
11.2 Order constraints and explicit uncertainty
DEFINITION 11-D2. A precedence edge e_i < e_j is a strict order claim with a source, scope, and
precision tag. A finite graph G=(V,E_before) contains these edges for events with stable identifiers. Edges
can concern utterances, deliveries, observations, or information acquisition, but their node types remain
distinct. A cross-type edge needs a stated relation rather than a merger of the nodes.
LEMMA 11-L1. A finite set of pure strict precedence constraints has a real-valued timestamp assignment
exactly when its directed graph is acyclic. Proof: a directed cycle requires t1 < t2 < ... < t1, which is
impossible. Conversely, a finite acyclic graph has a node with no incoming edge; otherwise repeatedly
following predecessors would eventually repeat a node and form a cycle. Remove that node, repeat, and
assign increasing integers in removal order. Every edge then points to a larger timestamp. This result
excludes additional fixed dates, duration bounds, and interval-overlap constraints, which require further
checks.
EXAMPLE 11.A
An invented parcel log records dispatch at 09:00 on Monday but enters the record at 15:00 on Tuesday. A
clerk discusses the entry at 16:00 Tuesday. Source U11A fixes parcel p2 and logistics context c11A. Let
p11A mean Dispatched(p2,Monday_09:00), and q11A mean EntryCreated(p2,Tuesday_15:00). The
record's creation time supports q11A directly; its content asserts p11A with separate provenance. A late
entry does not establish a late dispatch, and an early claimed dispatch does not establish an early record.
Result: the two clocks remain distinct, allowing investigation of delayed entry without rewriting the
asserted event chronology.

11.3 Temporal scope is part of proposition identity
The predicates Open(door,10:00) and Open(door,11:00) are different propositions. Their opposite truth
values need not conflict. "Never," "already," "still," "again," "before," and "not yet" impose temporal
scope or transition assumptions that ordinary paraphrases can lose. A denial of an event during one shift is
not a denial across the person's entire employment. An interviewer's later broad recap creates a new
proposition if it expands the interval.
OBLIGATION 11-O1. Every temporal generalization must state its quantified interval, observation
coverage, and event identity conditions. A claim that no event appears in records is a record-level negative.
Promoting it to absence of the event requires a completeness premise about recording and retrieval,
together with a match between the searched interval and the claimed interval.
EXAMPLE 11.B
In an invented clinic inquiry, receptionist R checks the current desk list at 18:00 and says, "No admission
appears on this list." Source U11B fixes patient identifier p7, current list l4, and evening context c11B. Let
p11B mean ListedAdmission(p7,l4,18:00), q11B mean EverAdmitted(p7,clinic,day_d), and r11B mean
Discharged(p7,day_d). R denies p11B. An earlier admission with a delayed update and a day with no
admission both remain possible. Neither alternative alone establishes discharge. Result: the list-state cube
connects to historical events through an explicit completeness and update model; absence at the current
lookup does not retrospectively determine the whole day's sequence.
11.4 Knowledge time and hindsight
Later information can change an agent's beliefs without changing the earlier event. Suppose a file is
discovered at t3 concerning an action at t1, followed by an interview at t4. Awareness at t3 or t4 does not
entail awareness at t1. A statement such as "it was obvious" can concern present interpretation rather than
contemporaneous perception. The analysis asks what information was available at the relevant earlier time
and what evidence supports actual uptake.
PROPOSITION 11-P1. Know(s,phi,t2) does not entail Know(s,phi,t1) for t1 < t2 without a
backwards-persistence premise. Proof: construct a temporal model in which phi is true throughout, s's
accessible worlds include NOT phi at t1, and a reliable disclosure removes those worlds at t2. The later
knowledge holds while the earlier knowledge does not. Conversely, earlier knowledge does not guarantee
uninterrupted later recall without a forward-persistence assumption. These conclusions concern the chosen
epistemic model, not an automatic diagnosis of memory.
EXAMPLE 11.C
An invented factory interview concerns an alarm at noon. Operator O saw a warning lamp at 11:50 but
learned at 14:00 that it indicated coolant loss. Source U11C binds machine m9, production day dC, and
operator context c11C. Let p11C mean Saw(O,lamp,11:50), q11C mean
Understood(O,coolant_loss,11:50), and r11C mean Informed(O,lamp_meaning,14:00). The later
explanation supports a route to later understanding; it cannot establish q11C merely by reinterpreting the
earlier sighting. Result: perception, interpretation, and later instruction occupy separate cells. Evidence of
prior training could constrain q11C, but that would require its own source and uptake assumptions.
11.5 Temporal identity, witnesses, and testing
Temporal arguments also interact with identity. A claim that some guard was present each hour does not
establish that one guard remained throughout. The formula forall t exists g Present(g,t) permits different
witnesses across hours, whereas exists g forall t Present(g,t) fixes one guard. A witness-selection operation
cannot establish the stronger statement. Person identity and shift schedules must supply the connection.


PAGE 036 OF 108

The atlas stores partial orders when exact times are unavailable. If two events have no ordering path, the
model family can retain both orders or overlap where the semantics permits it. A display's row order must
not become an evidentiary precedence edge. Likewise, a timestamp from an unverified device clock may
require an offset parameter; changing that parameter should visibly change the ordering conclusions that
depend on it.
A further safeguard concerns repeated events with similar descriptions. Two entries labelled "door
opened" may represent one duplicated report, two separate openings, or one event described with different
clock offsets. Their merger requires an identity rule supported by location, actor, sequence, and recording
provenance. Keeping them separate provisionally can also overcount activity, so the atlas retains the merge
and nonmerge alternatives until the evidence resolves them. Temporal proximity is a constraint on possible
identity, not a sufficient identity certificate. This issue connects event chronology directly to source
independence and quantity counting.
The northern checks detect cycles, incompatible interval bounds, illicit backwards knowledge, shifted
temporal quantifiers, and unsupported identity persistence. A temporal adapter may implement bounded
operators such as always_during(phi,I) or eventually_during(phi,I), but a Boolean kernel checking only
timestamp order does not thereby verify general temporal logic. The report names the implemented
fragment and preserves unresolved timing branches, keeping the event chronology distinct from the history
of statements and the history of information acquisition.

CHAPTER 12. PARAPHRASE, SUMMARIES, AND SEMANTIC
CHANGE
12.1 Compression requires an account of what changed
A summary reduces wording while preserving selected meaning. That operation can clarify a rambling
exchange, but it can also alter actor, action, modality, time, quantity, source, or certainty. The atlas
therefore treats a summary as a new record linked to the material it summarizes. The original remains
available. A summary's fluency, confidence, or institutional status does not certify equivalence with its
sources. An accurate audit must identify what the summary preserves, omits, adds, and leaves ambiguous.
DEFINITION 12-D1. A transformation T maps a source package X of utterances and claim records to a
summary package Y, with a declared intended query set Q and context c. Its semantic delta is
Delta(T)=(actor,predicate,arguments,time,modality,quantifier,scope,source,commitment,qualification),
each coordinate recording preserved, changed, omitted, or unresolved status. The classification is
supported by source spans and interpretation hypotheses, rather than by word similarity alone.
The content comparison and the source comparison are different. Two sentences may express equivalent
propositions while attributing them to different speakers. Conversely, an accurate attribution of an
interviewer-supplied proposition does not make that proposition equivalent to the respondent's earlier
words. The provenance interface carries these differences even when a simplified display shows only the
content formula.
12.2 Equivalence and directional entailment
DEFINITION 12-D2. For a declared nonempty M(H), source formula phi and summary formula psi are
equivalent relative to H when every retained model satisfies phi <-> psi. The summary weakens content
when H entails phi -> psi but not psi -> phi; it strengthens content when the reverse entailment holds but
the forward entailment fails. If neither direction holds, the transformation changes content in incomparable
ways. These definitions apply only after both formulas have been interpreted within the same typed scope.
OBLIGATION 12-O1. The comparison premises H must not already assume the disputed summary.
Otherwise the test can certify the conclusion by importing it. Report whether equivalence holds
unconditionally, under explicitly added assumptions, or only for a restricted query set. An interpretation
set for an ambiguous source must be preserved rather than selecting whichever reading makes the
summary appear accurate.
LEMMA 12-L1. Relative equivalence can be tested by seeking countermodels to both implications.
Proof: phi <-> psi fails exactly where either phi AND NOT psi or psi AND NOT phi holds. If neither set
has a model and M(H) is nonempty, equivalence holds throughout M(H). If one set has a witness, that
witness identifies the failed direction. If M(H) is empty, the result is INCONSISTENT rather than a useful
equivalence certificate.
EXAMPLE 12.A
An invented storeroom statement says, "Two cartons arrived, and one had an intact seal." A recap says,
"Both cartons arrived sealed." Source U12A binds cartons c1 and c2, receipt interval tA, and store context
c12A. The source content permits IntactSeal(c1,tA) with NOT IntactSeal(c2,tA); the recap requires both.
That valuation is a countermodel to source-implies-summary. Result: the recap strengthens the seal claim.
A separate source confirming the second seal could justify the stronger historical conclusion, but it would
remain additional evidence, not meaning already contained in the first statement.

12.3 Qualification and scope can disappear independently
Common changes include turning possibility into actuality, reported belief into fact, occasional conduct
into habitual conduct, an encountered category into a preferred category, and a general mechanism into an
individual event. Each change needs its own proof obligation. Removing a word is not intrinsically
weakening: deleting a qualification can strengthen a commitment. Adding a word is not intrinsically
strengthening either: "possibly" can weaken it. Surface length provides no reliable semantic direction.
Attribution should also retain response conditions. A statement made under an explicit assumption remains
conditional unless another source establishes the assumption. A respondent's affirmative answer may
genuinely endorse a proposed account while leaving its origination with the interviewer. A summary can
accurately report this as endorsement. Reporting it as an independently volunteered narrative changes
provenance even if its content is otherwise preserved.
EXAMPLE 12.B
In an invented service review, employee E states, "During the spring shift, I sometimes saw complaints
arrive after closing." A recap states, "E routinely delayed customer complaints." Source U12B fixes
employee E, office o3, spring interval tB, and interview context c12B. The source concerns observation of
arrival timing; the recap concerns E's causal action, habitual frequency, and responsibility. A world where
another department transmitted occasional late complaints satisfies the source while falsifying the recap.
Result: the delta includes predicate, agency, frequency, and possibly interval. These are independent
additions requiring separate support; a single label such as exaggeration is less informative than the
component analysis.
12.4 Repetition does not create a new independent origin
DEFINITION 12-D3. RootSources(c) is the set of evidentiary origin identifiers reachable from claim
record c through documented derivation edges. A faithful copy or paraphrase inherits the union of its input
roots. A new observation can add a root, but the mere existence of a new document, speaker, or timestamp
does not establish independence from the inherited roots.
PROPOSITION 12-P1. A finite chain consisting only of root-preserving transformations cannot increase
its set of evidentiary origins. Proof: the first transformation inherits its input roots by definition.
Inductively, each subsequent transformation inherits the union of roots already present in its inputs. If no
step introduces a new observation root, every output root belongs to the initial union. This proposition
concerns documented lineage; undisclosed source dependence or genuinely independent observations
require separate investigation.
EXAMPLE 12.C
An invented accountant A says, "The deposit may have reached the bank on Tuesday." Supervisor S writes
that Tuesday receipt is possible. A later memorandum quotes S, and a hearing summary cites both the
memorandum and S as corroborating definite Tuesday receipt. Source U12C binds deposit d9, Tuesday
interval tC, and accounting context c12C. The chain preserves one reported possibility origin. The definite
receipt claim drops modality, and the apparent corroboration double-counts descendants. Result: the
provenance cube links all three records to the same root while the content cube retains receipt and
nonreceipt branches. A bank transaction record would constitute a potentially new origin requiring
authentication and matching.

12.5 Reporting deltas and repairing downstream use
The analyst should distinguish harmless compression from material change. A summary can omit
greetings or repeated wording without affecting the declared query set. It may also omit a qualification
irrelevant to one narrow question while becoming unsafe for a broader purpose. Therefore every
compression certificate names the questions it preserves. A statement safe for identifying the speaker may
be unsafe for estimating frequency or assigning motive.
When a faulty summary has propagated, correction must follow its dependency edges. The original
wording remains fixed; the summary receives a revised version or a superseded status, and affected
conclusions become pending until rechecked. A correction of attribution does not automatically resolve
content truth, and correction of content does not automatically authenticate the source. The northern
pyramid verifies both dimensions before restoring conclusions to the shared center. The resulting report
presents the supported formulation, the rejected transformation, its explicit countermodel or missing
premise, and the exact scope of the repaired conclusion.

CHAPTER 13. LINKED CUBE ATLAS AND SEMANTIC
INTERFACES
13.1 The atlas as an indexed semantic structure
The atlas is a cluster of topic cubes with a shared center. It organizes a demanding audit without
pretending that three physical dimensions contain every logical distinction. A cube can concern identity,
chronology, receipt of a document, an allegation, a particular answer, or the provenance of a summary. Its
cells contain typed records and model restrictions. Geometric adjacency means that an interface has been
declared; it never establishes agreement, causation, similarity, probability, or evidential strength. This
construction implements the linked-block preference while leaving each inference available for
independent checking. [SRC-PREF]
DEFINITION 13-D1. A topic cube C_i consists of a language L_i, declared premises H_i, a local model
family M_i, stable cell identifiers, and interfaces I_ij with other cubes. A cell is a named restriction on
models together with its assumptions, source references, and status. An empty cell represents an excluded
case only after its exclusion has been justified. A blank display represents absent annotation and must not
silently mean FALSE.
The coordinates are fixed. Center contains statements shared by the declared nonempty global model
family. South/-x contains cases, alternatives, assumptions, and local witnesses. North/+x contains the
diagnostic pyramid: typing, consistency, coverage, propagation, witness and counterexample checks, then
conclusions. Negative y contains dependencies and witness construction. Positive y contains provenance,
sources, proof records, and conclusion scope. Positive z contains contexts satisfying the focal P; negative z
contains contexts satisfying NOT P. Negative y is not logical negation. Each topic cube may have its own
explicitly named focal P.
13.2 Interfaces preserve meanings, not merely names
DEFINITION 13-D2. An interface specifies a shared language L_ij and interpretation-preserving maps
into L_i and L_j. For atomic records, preservation requires matching actor, predicate, arguments,
event-time scope, context, modality, and quantifier scope. A display label can change while identity
remains fixed; a change to any meaning-bearing field requires a new proposition or an explicit translation
obligation.
The required relation type accompanies each connection. Identity declares the same entity or proposition.
Implication is directed and carries premises. Compatibility is symmetric but states only that a joint model
exists. Temporal or accessibility relations connect states under declared semantics. Provenance connects a
derivative record to its source. None of these types licenses the rules of another type. In particular, two
records with common ancestry are connected even when their propositions conflict.
EXAMPLE 13.A
An inventory clerk says, "Alex received the parcel on Monday." An interviewer later asks whether Alex
stored it on Tuesday. Let r denote Receive(Alex,parcel,Monday) and s denote Store(Alex,parcel,Tuesday).
The receipt cube and storage cube share the person and parcel identifiers, but their event predicates and
times differ. An identity interface maps Alex to Alex and parcel to parcel; it does not map r to s. The
model r=TRUE, s=FALSE satisfies the reported receipt while denying subsequent storage. A proposed r
-> s edge therefore needs an additional rule and supporting premises. The atlas retains the question about
storage in its own cell and reports the storage claim as unresolved.


PAGE 041 OF 108

13.3 Gluing local views into a global family
DEFINITION 13-D3. A compatible tuple of local models agrees on every declared shared interface and
satisfies every cross-cube constraint. Its amalgamation is a global interpretation restricting to those local
models. Let M be the set of all such amalgamations. If the languages are Boolean variables with consistent
shared naming, amalgamation is simply union of agreeing assignments.
PROPOSITION 13-P1. Under the Boolean naming condition, assume each M_i is exactly Mod(H_i) on
its declared local language, every common atom is covered by a shared interface, and every premise lies
within a cube or an explicitly checked cross-cube constraint. Then M equals the models of their combined
premises. Arbitrarily selected local subfamilies do not satisfy this completeness assumption.
Proof. A combined-premise model restricts to a local model in every cube, agrees with itself on every
interface, and satisfies the cross-cube constraints. Conversely, the union of an agreeing tuple assigns each
shared variable a single value. It satisfies each local premise because its restriction does, and it satisfies
every cross-cube premise by the compatibility requirement. These constructions are inverse on the
declared variables.
The scope condition is essential. Local satisfiability alone cannot certify global satisfiability. Nor can
agreement on only a conveniently selected subset of interfaces. Unrecorded global restrictions make the
join appear larger than the intended model family; omitted local alternatives can make it appear smaller.
Both errors change conclusions.
EXAMPLE 13.B
Three witnesses each discuss two switches. The first says A and B match; the second says B and C match;
the third says A and C differ. The cubes contain A <-> B, B <-> C, and NOT(A <-> C). Every cube is
satisfiable, and every pair of constraints is satisfiable. Their combined family is empty because the first
two equalities imply A <-> C. The northern diagnostic must classify the combined premises
INCONSISTENT before placing anything in the shared center. Separate local green status indicators
would conceal the contradiction.
13.4 The center and dependent witnesses
DEFINITION 13-D4. For nonempty M and a declared query language Q, the center is Center(M,Q) =
{phi in Q : forall m in M, m |= phi}. This is an intersection of model theories, not an intersection of
arbitrary strings or a list of claims repeated by several speakers. Record-level facts can occupy the center
while the historical allegations embedded in them remain unresolved.
An implementation need not print the entire center. It can classify a finite query set and retain certificates.
Calling that query set complete requires naming its boundary. Extending Q can reveal previously unasked
distinctions without changing M. Empty M never supplies a useful universal center; it triggers conflict
handling instead.
EXAMPLE 13.C
A supervisor reports, "Someone opened the cabinet." Candidate A has the key in one retained
reconstruction; candidate B has it in another. The two worlds satisfy Opened(A) OR Opened(B), but one
denies Opened(B) and the other denies Opened(A). The shared existential claim has a local witness in each
world. The negative-y wing stores the dependency from world to witness, while the positive-y wing
records the supervisor's source. Neither individual opening claim reaches the center. Selecting A as a
convenient display representative cannot remove the B-world or identify A as the actual opener.

13.5 Required audit outputs
OBLIGATION 13-O1. Every exported cube states its language, premises, focal proposition, interface
maps, cross-cube constraints, and restriction to the global family. Every displayed conclusion identifies
whether it is local or central. Every source record remains traceable through paraphrases to immutable
evidence spans.
A practical audit first validates types, then forms the global join, checks nonemptiness, and evaluates
queries. It records excluded tuples with their violated constraint rather than silently dropping them. Signed
layers remain query-specific: changing the focal proposition changes the partition, not the underlying
evidence. The cube layout makes missing connections inspectable, while semantic checks determine
whether those connections justify any conclusion.
Compatibility cannot be propagated as though it were identity. An account can be compatible with a
second account, and that second account compatible with a third, while the first and third conflict. Identity,
in contrast, is transitive within its declared type, but each asserted identification still needs justification. A
spreadsheet-style one-to-one matching rule is especially dangerous here: a person may occupy several
roles, several people may use one account, and one utterance may mention several events. The atlas
introduces uniqueness only when a source or adopted domain constraint supports it. Missing identity
evidence remains an unresolved interface, rather than being filled by visual proximity.

CHAPTER 14. DEPENDENCY HYPERGRAPHS AND
PROVENANCE PROPAGATION
14.1 Why ordinary pairwise links are insufficient
An interrogation conclusion often depends on a conjunction: a particular person controlled a particular
account, a particular transaction occurred, and the transaction time falls inside that control interval. Each
ingredient can be plausible while the conjunction is unsupported. A dependency structure must therefore
represent several premises entering one inference together. Pairwise association cannot express the
difference between needing all prerequisites and having several alternative ways to establish a result.
DEFINITION 14-D1. A directed inference hyperedge e = (T,h,r,a,d) has a finite tail set T of required
premises, a head h, an identified rule r, explicit assumptions a, and defeat conditions d. An active
application requires every member of T, every assumption in a, and satisfaction of the declared treatment
of d. Hard logical inference and defeasible recommendation use distinct edge types. A defeasible result
never silently enters H.
An AND-tail means all listed premises are required. Alternative sufficient tails are separate hyperedges
with the same head. A shared witness identifier binds occurrences that must concern the same person,
item, event, or interval. Dropping that identifier can manufacture a conjunction from individually true
statements about unrelated events. A typed edge must also preserve the distinction between an assertion
record and its asserted content.
14.2 Sound propagation and its boundary
DEFINITION 14-D2. For finite nodes V, trusted starting nodes S, and sound hard edges, let F(X) equal X
together with every head whose tail and hard assumptions are contained in X. Define X_0 = S and
X_(k+1) = F(X_k). The closure is the first fixed point. Rule checking and premise acceptance are separate
obligations: a valid rule cannot repair an unsupported starting proposition.
PROPOSITION 14-P1. The closure terminates after at most |V| strict additions stages and contains only
consequences of S and the active sound rules.
Proof. F never removes a node, and every strict stage adds at least one member of finite V. Once a stage
adds nothing, the next is identical. For soundness, starting nodes hold by the declared premises.
Inductively, every newly added head follows from premises already holding and its sound rule. This
proves each stage sound. It does not prove that the chosen rules derive every logical consequence.
EXAMPLE 14.A
A clerk says, "The account was assigned to Dana," and a transaction log records an export at noon. The
interviewer concludes, "Dana performed the export." Define a = Assigned(Dana,account,noon), x =
Export(account,file,noon), and e = ExclusiveEffectiveControl(Dana,account,noon). Even a AND x does
not imply PersonalAction(Dana,export). A proposed attribution edge requires e and a justified
action-attribution rule; delegated sessions or automation remain countermodels otherwise. The hyperedge
exposes the missing conjunct. Its conclusion remains conditional until the exclusivity premise and the
rule's domain have been established.


PAGE 044 OF 108

14.3 Provenance is propagated alongside conclusions
DEFINITION 14-D3. A derivation record stores the edge identifier, ordered premise references,
assumption references, rule version, and root evidence identifiers. Its provenance set is the union of root
identifiers in its dependency ancestry. Different derivations of the same head remain distinguishable even
when they produce the same formula. A set of roots describes ancestry, not a probability model or a count
of independent observations.
For finite acyclic proof records, ancestry is computed recursively. Cyclic dependency graphs require
rooted derivations or a separately justified fixed-point semantics. A cycle of paraphrases is not an original
observation. At least one accepted basis is needed before forward propagation derives anything. If a node
has several proof routes, deleting one route invalidates the node only when every remaining route also
fails.
EXAMPLE 14.B
A receptionist says, "Release seems likely." A supervisor repeats that the patient was released, and an
incident summary cites the supervisor. Let u be the receptionist's utterance record, v the supervisor's
paraphrase, and w the summary. Provenance edges u -> v -> w show one root, not three confirmations.
The move from likelihood to categorical release is a separate semantic strengthening requiring
justification. If w is later cited to confirm v, the extra edge creates a cycle without adding an independent
root. The graph preserves three speech events while rejecting the claim of three independent release
observations.
14.4 Minimal supports and alternative explanations
DEFINITION 14-D4. A support set for a derivation is a set of accepted premises and assumptions
sufficient for that derivation. A minimal support set has no proper subset sufficient through the declared
rule system. A node can have several incomparable minimal supports. Keeping only one arbitrary support
conceals alternative derivations and makes retraction unreliable.
Minimality is relative to the rule inventory. A support minimal under forward rules can fail to be minimal
under full Boolean entailment. The bounded checker may validate a proposed support by exhaustive
entailment and test each premise deletion. That procedure establishes subset minimality: no single premise
can be removed while retaining entailment. For a monotone premise relation, single-deletion checks
suffice, since any successful smaller subset would also make at least one single deletion successful.
PROPOSITION 14-P2. A premise-deletion audit is sound for subset minimality under monotone
entailment and nonempty checked support models. Proof. If a proper subset entailed the conclusion,
choose an omitted premise. The full set without that premise contains the successful subset and therefore
also entails the conclusion. This contradicts the failed single-deletion test. An inconsistent support is
flagged separately instead of receiving a vacuous minimal proof.
EXAMPLE 14.C
Two reports concern a damaged box. One says a box entered the depot; another says a box left the depot
damaged. The interviewer asks, "So the arriving box became damaged inside." Let b1 witness the arrival
and b2 witness the damaged departure. The proposed inference needs b1=b2, continuity of custody, and a
rule relating entry condition to later damage. Neither existential report supplies those conditions. A
countermodel uses different boxes, while another uses the same box already damaged on arrival. The
witness-preserving hypergraph retains both alternatives and identifies exactly which additional records
could distinguish them.

14.5 Constraints, checks, and change impact
Inference hyperedges derive heads from premises; constraint hyperedges forbid joint assignments. The
constraint NOT(p AND q AND r) permits each pair to coexist while forbidding their triple. Encoding it as
three pairwise exclusions would wrongly remove permitted cases. Conversely, storing only compatible
pairs would miss the forbidden triple. Every constraint therefore retains its full arity and its interpretation.
OBLIGATION 14-O1. Each edge exports its relation type, full tail, witness bindings, scope keys,
assumptions, source ancestry, and checking status. Every invalidated root triggers a dependency traversal
and re-evaluation of affected conclusions. Unaffected conclusions retain their records; affected
conclusions remain valid only through surviving checked support routes.
The negative-y wing carries construction dependencies, while positive y carries proof and source records.
A common root establishes dependence of reporting history, not agreement of content. A logical
implication establishes a semantic relation, not a causal one. Preserving those distinctions makes the
hypergraph useful for finding missing premises, circular corroboration, and stale conclusions without
claiming to determine historical truth from graph shape.
Proof graphs also need version-sensitive edges. If an interviewer replaces "possibly present" with
"present," the new proposition cannot inherit the old proof identifier merely because its topic is unchanged.
A downstream conclusion depends on the exact premise version that licensed its derivation. Conversely,
correcting a spelling error in a source label need not invalidate a semantically unchanged proposition,
provided the identity map records the correction. A change-impact check therefore compares meanings
and dependencies as well as file timestamps. This prevents both silent strengthening and needless
abandonment of valid conclusions when presentation details change.

CHAPTER 15. ORTHOGONAL CASES AND JOINT
CONSTRAINTS
15.1 Orthogonality is a semantic partition
Ordinary interview claims overlap. An event can be discussed, remembered, denied, and attributed to
several sources. Rearranging their labels into rows does not make them exclusive. Logical
orthogonalization constructs mutually exclusive cases whose union preserves every permitted
interpretation. It operates on model sets, with each split retaining both signs unless a checked restriction
excludes one. The resulting cases are Boolean cells, which must not be confused with syntactically atomic
sentences. [SRC-PREF]
DEFINITION 15-D1. Let M be a nonempty model family and P_1,...,P_k a fixed finite list of formulas.
For each sign vector b in {0,1}^k, define C_b = {m in M : for every i, m |= P_i exactly when b_i=1}.
Empty C_b are excluded cells; nonempty C_b are retained cells. The signature of a cell is its complete sign
vector together with the meanings and versions of the P_i.
OBLIGATION 15-O1. Branch removal requires a certificate of emptiness relative to the declared H, or
an explicitly logged new evidential restriction. Failure to discover a witness is not itself a certificate. Every
retained cell keeps its ancestry and all dependencies relevant to its existence.
15.2 Disjointness, coverage, and reconstruction
PROPOSITION 15-P1. The retained cells are pairwise disjoint and cover M. Every Boolean formula
built from P_1,...,P_k is the union of precisely those cells whose signatures satisfy it.
Proof. If b and c differ, choose an index j where their signs differ. A member of both cells would satisfy
P_j and NOT P_j, impossible under classical Boolean semantics. Thus distinct cells are disjoint. Each m in
M determines one signature by evaluating every P_i, so it belongs to a cell; that cell is nonempty because
m witnesses it. Therefore the cells cover M. Finally, an induction on formula construction shows that every
model in a cell gives the same truth value to a Boolean combination of the P_i: negation complements a
value, conjunction intersects values, and disjunction unions values. Selecting the true signatures
reconstructs the formula's model set.
The proof is relative to the chosen generators. A formula mentioning an untracked predicate can vary
within one cell. Reconstruction of that formula then requires refinement. The cell inventory is not a
universal atomic basis for every possible language extension.
EXAMPLE 15.A
A manager asks whether a package arrived and whether its receipt was signed. Let A mean arrival and S
mean signing of that same receipt. Suppose H contains S -> A. The candidate cells are (A,S) = 00, 01, 10,
11. Cell 01 violates H; the other three remain possible. Arrival is reconstructed by cells 10 and 11. Signing
is reconstructed by cell 11. Failure to sign is reconstructed by 00 and 10. The interviewer cannot infer no
arrival from no signature, because cell 10 is a concrete countermodel. Orthogonalization makes the
missing implication visible without changing the meanings of arrival or signing.
15.3 Repeated splitting and stable ordering
For an existing case a, splitting on P produces a AND P and a AND NOT P. Their union equals a and their
intersection is empty. Repeating this construction yields the same semantic partition for the same
generators regardless of split order, although tree shape, intermediate memory, and display order differ.
Stable cell identifiers should therefore derive from semantic signatures or explicit ancestry mappings,
rather than accidental row numbers.


PAGE 047 OF 108

PROPOSITION 15-P2. If every step preserves the parent as the union of its retained children, successive
refinement preserves global coverage. Proof. Substitute the child union for each parent in the previous
covering union. Each substitution preserves equality. Induction over the finite sequence of substitutions
preserves the union M. A single unjustified omitted child invalidates the induction at its exact removal
step.
Unknown is represented by survival of both signed possibilities, not by assigning a third classical truth
value. A source-support state of BOTH is also different: conflicting reports can coexist as records even
while their historical contents occupy opposite cells. The partition and evidence ledger answer different
questions.
EXAMPLE 15.B
Three employees state that routes P, Q, and R were open. A verified capacity rule says at most two of those
routes could be open simultaneously. Each pair of statements is compatible with H: signatures 110, 101,
and 011 provide witnesses. Their conjunction requires 111, which H excludes. A matrix recording every
pair as compatible therefore cannot establish joint compatibility. The arity-three constraint NOT(P AND Q
AND R) must survive in the case checker. The result identifies a conflict between the three embedded
claims and the capacity rule, while leaving the fact that all three employees made their statements
consistent.
15.4 Orthogonal cases do not erase other relations
The intersection matrix of retained cells is diagonal in the sense that distinct cells have empty intersection.
Dependency, implication, temporal accessibility, and provenance matrices generally are not diagonal. A
conclusion in one cube may depend on a premise represented in another; a later state may be accessible
from an earlier state with a different signature. Removing those links to improve a visual pattern destroys
the analysis.
Cases also need not have equal weight. A partition establishes exclusivity and coverage, not frequencies or
probabilities. Assigning equal probability to every cell requires an independently defended distribution.
Further splitting one cell into two would otherwise change apparent confidence without adding evidence.
Counting signatures measures the chosen abstraction's assignments, not the historical likelihood of their
contents.
EXAMPLE 15.C
A service worker says, "Several calls concerned repairs, and some were urgent." Let R classify a particular
call as repair-related and U classify that same call as urgent. The four cells distinguish urgent repairs,
nonurgent repairs, urgent other calls, and nonurgent other calls. A later question about the proportion of
urgent repair calls needs a declared denominator: all calls, all repairs, or all urgent calls. The partition
supports all three calculations only after counts for the same period and population are supplied. Adding
unrelated afternoon calls to a morning denominator changes the proposition and requires a new scope, not
merely a different arrangement of the existing cells.
15.5 Operational obligations
DEFINITION 15-D2. A reconstruction certificate names the parent region, the retained children, their
defining signatures, and a verified equality between the parent and their union. A disjointness certificate
verifies empty intersections for distinct children. In the bounded Boolean kernel both can be represented
by exact sets of satisfying assignments, making omission and overlap directly testable.
OBLIGATION 15-O2. Every report distinguishes candidate cells, nonempty cells, unsearched cells, and
rejected cells. Every reconstruction states its generator language. Refinement of quantified or temporal
claims uses an appropriate semantic adapter rather than silently treating quantifier dependencies or event

sequences as independent switches. The orthogonal partition supplies a disciplined accounting of
possibilities; evidence and sound rules determine which possibilities remain.
An existential statement can be true throughout a cell even when its witness varies among the cell's

Assigning one named witness to the entire cell would add a constraint absent from the partition proof.

CHAPTER 16. REFINEMENT, EXTENSION, AND PRESERVED
MEANING
16.1 Four distinct forms of growth
An audit grows by adding vocabulary, distinguishing cases, accepting additional constraints, or enlarging
the collection of contexts. These operations have different effects. New vocabulary can express
distinctions without removing any old possibility. New evidence can legitimately exclude possibilities. A
finer transcript can correct an earlier reading. Additional contexts can invalidate a former generalization.
Every update must state which operation occurred instead of describing all change as improved precision.
DEFINITION 16-D1. For languages L_0 subseteq L_1 with unchanged meanings, restriction rho sends
an L_1 model to its interpretation on L_0. For declared model families M_0 and M_1, a valid restriction
map rho:M_1 -> M_0 requires every new model's old-language restriction to satisfy the old hard premises.
It is surjective when every m_0 in M_0 has at least one extension in M_1.
Restriction forgets new distinctions but never changes old truth values. If an old predicate has acquired a
different event time, actor, or definition, literal restriction is unavailable. An explicit translation map and
preservation proof are required. Reusing the old identifier conceals semantic revision rather than
preserving meaning.
16.2 Conservative extension and its certificate
PROPOSITION 16-P1. If rho:M_1 -> M_0 is well-defined and surjective, with both families nonempty,
every old-language formula has the same ENTAILED, REFUTED, or UNDETERMINED status in both
families.
Proof. Old formulas have identical truth values in a new model and its restriction. If all old models satisfy
phi, all new restrictions and hence all new models satisfy phi. Conversely, if every new model satisfies phi,
surjectivity supplies a new extension of each old model, forcing phi in that old model. Apply the same
argument to NOT phi. If both signs have old witnesses, surjectivity supplies new witnesses for both;
restriction proves the reverse direction.
DEFINITION 16-D2. A conservative-extension certificate comprises the restriction map, checks that old
meanings are preserved, and an extension witness for each old model. Exhaustive checking is available for
the declared bounded Boolean representation. A collection of several successful examples is not a
certificate of surjectivity over a larger family.
EXAMPLE 16.A
A warehouse record initially tracks only R, whether a parcel arrived. Both R values remain permitted. A
refined record adds S, whether its receipt was signed, together with S -> R. The new assignments are 00,
10, and 11 in (R,S) order. Restriction reaches both old assignments: 00 extends R=FALSE, and 10 or 11
extends R=TRUE. The extension is conservative for the old language even though S is constrained. The
refined system can distinguish unsigned arrival from signed arrival without becoming more certain that
arrival occurred.
16.3 When stronger evidence removes old possibilities
If a genuinely new premise is adopted, rho can remain well-defined but cease to be surjective. The lost old
models are the precise evidential effect of the update. Such loss is legitimate when the added premise is
justified and its adoption recorded. It is illegitimate when the loss comes from default values, incomplete
search, changed semantics, or discarded ambiguity.


PAGE 050 OF 108

OBLIGATION 16-O1. Every nonsurjective update records the new constraint, its source, the old cases
excluded, and the conclusions whose status changed. Previously issued conclusions retain version
identifiers. A conclusion based on earlier assumptions becomes stale rather than silently rewritten as if it
had always followed.
EXAMPLE 16.B
A supervisor initially knows that either Dana or Eli authorized an export, possibly both. Let D OR E be the
old premise. A newly authenticated event record establishes NOT D for that event. The new models retain
only D=FALSE,E=TRUE. Restriction omits the previously permitted D=TRUE models, so this is
evidential strengthening, not conservative refinement. E becomes entailed from the adopted premises. If
the new record concerns another export, its scope differs and it cannot exclude those models. The map
check therefore depends on event identity as well as Boolean evaluation.
16.4 A finite converse and its limits
PROPOSITION 16-P2. For finite full Boolean languages, a well-defined restriction map from the new
model family into the old family is surjective if it introduces no new old-language consequences.
Equivalently, the two families have exactly the same old-language consequence theory. Merely retaining
previously established consequences is insufficient.
Proof. Suppose an old valuation v has no new extension. Form its characteristic formula delta_v by
conjoining each old atom positively when v assigns TRUE and negatively otherwise. Every new model
then satisfies NOT delta_v, since no new restriction equals v. But the old family contains v, which falsifies
NOT delta_v. A new old-language consequence has appeared, contradicting preservation. Hence every old
valuation has an extension.
The proof relies on characteristic formulas for complete finite valuations. It must not be asserted
unchanged for arbitrary infinitary structures, restricted query languages, or richer logics. Preserving a
selected query list is weaker than preserving every old possibility. A missing old model might differ only
on a formula absent from that list.
A structural refinement of cases also needs a parent map. Each child maps to the old case containing its
restriction. Coverage requires every permitted old model to occur under at least one child. Disjointness and
reconstruction are checked separately. Multiple new extensions of one old model are normal; uniqueness is
an additional property, not part of conservativity.
EXAMPLE 16.C
An early interview note says, "The file was obtained." The later annotation distinguishes reception from
another person, direct download, and local creation. Suppose the agreed meaning of obtained is reception
OR download, excluding creation. Let O be old obtaining, and R,D,C be new predicates. The defining
bridge O <-> (R OR D) preserves that meaning. If local creation is mistakenly substituted for reception in
the bridge, old claims change meaning even when every old O value still has some extension. Surjectivity
alone cannot catch a false interpretation map. The source-based definition check must precede the
model-based extension check.
16.5 Continuation through many stages
For finite stages i<j<k, coherent restriction requires rho_(i,k) = rho_(i,j) composed with rho_(j,k).
Composition of surjective maps is surjective: an old model first receives an intermediate extension and
that extension receives a later extension. Consequently a finite chain of certified conservative extensions
preserves old query statuses throughout. This elementary result does not certify unlimited future updates or
supply an infinite limiting construction without further hypotheses.

OBLIGATION 16-O2. Each stage declares its language, model family, parent version, restriction or
translation map, coverage certificate, and any new evidence. Witness choices remain indexed by the model
being extended. A witness chosen for one old case cannot be assumed to work in every other case. The
resulting sequence supports continued analysis while exposing exactly where certainty increased through
evidence and where only descriptive resolution increased.
Coarsening requires its own preservation argument. Two states can agree on every current factual query
yet differ in what future observations or actions permit. Merging them is safe for the current query list if
every listed formula has the same value in both. Safety for future temporal or action queries additionally
requires an explicit condition preserving the relevant transitions. Current observational agreement alone
supplies no such condition. An interrogation annotation may therefore collapse identical present
descriptions for display while keeping their distinct histories and accessible continuations in the
underlying records. The export must identify which distinctions were hidden and which were actually
proved irrelevant.

CHAPTER 17. CONFLICTS, INCONSISTENT VIEWS, AND
CONTROLLED REASONING
17.1 Conflict has several locations
Two incompatible accounts can be recorded consistently. A conflict arises in the historical content if both
embedded accounts are adopted as hard premises about the same event. Another conflict can arise from
transcription, identity merging, shifted time scope, or a faulty inference rule. These locations require
different repairs. The system therefore identifies the level of conflict before changing any evidence or
drawing conclusions.
DEFINITION 17-D1. A record conflict is incompatible annotation of the same source feature. A content
conflict is joint unsatisfiability of adopted world propositions. A derivation conflict occurs when a claimed
rule application fails its formal conditions. A provenance conflict concerns incompatible ancestry or
authentication claims. More than one kind may occur in the same case, but none automatically establishes
deception.
The evidence-support state BOTH means positive and negative support records coexist. It does not make a
Boolean world satisfy P AND NOT P. Nor does it establish that both records deserve equal weight. Source
quality, scope, and dependence remain separate annotations. Content may remain undetermined even when
support is abundant on both sides.
EXAMPLE 17.A
A technician says, "The door was locked at nine," while a guard says, "The door was open at ten." With L9
and L10 representing different event times, L9 AND NOT L10 is consistent. Replacing both with a single
L manufactures a contradiction. Alternatively, if both statements concern nine, the records
Assert(technician,L9) and Assert(guard,NOT L9) still coexist consistently. A world-content conflict
appears only after bridges adopting both L9 and NOT L9 are introduced. The audit first repairs the scope
or identifies those bridges; it cannot infer lying merely from opposed reports.
17.2 Empty models halt ordinary classification
OBLIGATION 17-O1. Before classifying a classical world query, verify that M(H) is nonempty. If
empty, return INCONSISTENT and a conflict record. Do not report arbitrary propositions as established
through vacuous universal quantification. Local consistency checks never override a discovered global
inconsistency.
This operational rule controls a familiar hazard: under ordinary semantic entailment, an impossible
premise set has no countermodel to any conclusion. That mathematical convention is unsuitable as an
evidential success indicator. The checker therefore places inconsistency before ENTAILED or REFUTED
in its decision order. A separate proof of inconsistency remains a useful diagnostic result.
DEFINITION 17-D2. An unsatisfiable core K is a subset of adopted premises with no model. It is
subset-minimal when every proper subset has a model. Minimum-cardinality cores are smallest by size, a
stronger optimization requirement. Neither kind identifies which premise is historically false; both identify
combinations that cannot all hold under the declared semantics.
17.3 Extracting a conflict without overstating its diagnosis
PROPOSITION 17-P1. A finite deletion procedure yields a subset-minimal unsatisfiable core. Start with
an unsatisfiable finite H. Visit each premise once and remove it whenever the remaining set stays
unsatisfiable. The final set is unsatisfiable, and removing any retained premise makes it satisfiable.


PAGE 053 OF 108

Proof. Every accepted removal preserves unsatisfiability. When a premise is retained, its deletion would
leave a satisfiable set. Later operations only remove other premises; the resulting smaller set without that
retained premise remains satisfiable by the same witness. Thus every retained premise is necessary in the
final set. The procedure can produce different cores under different visit orders and does not guarantee
minimum size.
EXAMPLE 17.B
An audit adopts A: a verified instruction was received; B: the designated clerk controlled the release; R: (A
AND B) -> C, where C means the release was authorized; and N: NOT C. All four together are
inconsistent. Dropping A allows A=FALSE; dropping B allows B=FALSE; dropping R permits C=FALSE
with A and B true; dropping N permits C=TRUE. Each deletion restores a model, so the four-premise set
is subset-minimal. The result directs examination toward receipt, control, the authorization rule, and the
negative finding. It supplies no basis for selecting one as the false member.
17.4 Controlled local reasoning and repair families
Conflict isolation can permit useful reasoning in a declared consistent subsystem. A conclusion derived
from H_local is labelled with that scope and its dependency set. It is not promoted to a conclusion of the
inconsistent combined case. Cross-boundary use requires checking whether a bridge reintroduces the
conflict. A subsystem containing only device identity records may remain usable even when the account of
who operated the device conflicts.
DEFINITION 17-D3. A repair family R is an explicitly selected collection of consistent premise subsets
or revisions, with a stated selection policy. Its model family is M_R = union over K in R of M(K).
Skeptical repair entailment requires phi in every model of M_R. Credulous compatibility requires at least
one model of M_R satisfying phi. These are policy-relative statuses, not discoveries about which repair is
true.
Repairs can be inclusion-maximal consistent subsets, priority-respecting revisions, or source-specific
alternatives. These policies differ. Ranking a police report above an interview answer, for example,
requires an express premise about authority or evidential priority; institutional identity alone supplies no
logical priority rule. A preferred repair cannot silently become the only possible reconstruction.
EXAMPLE 17.C
An interview yields P: the request was approved, NOT P: the request was rejected, and P -> Q: approval
would authorize shipment. Under inclusion-maximal consistent-subset repair, one branch retains P and P
-> Q, while another retains NOT P and P -> Q. The first entails Q. The second permits both Q and NOT Q
because shipment might have another authorization route. Therefore Q is not skeptically entailed across
the repair family. Choosing only the first repair would manufacture certainty by discarding the rejection
account. The report identifies the conditional authorization result and leaves unconditional shipment
authorization unresolved.
17.5 Conflict certificates and nonclassical adapters
A conflict certificate records the exact premise versions, their source bridges, the failed joint assignment
conditions, and a reproducible emptiness check. For a minimal core it also includes a satisfying witness for
each single-premise deletion. These witnesses explain why every member matters. Failure to find a smaller
core during a limited search must be reported as a search limitation, not a minimum-size guarantee.
Deliberately paraconsistent reasoning requires a separately declared consequence relation and connective
semantics. The four evidence-support labels alone do not define such a logic. The classical finite kernel
instead preserves conflicting records, checks explicit world-premise subsets, and refuses ordinary
conclusion classification for an empty family. That division allows contradiction to remain visible while


PAGE 054 OF 108

preventing it from contaminating unrelated conclusions or becoming an apparent proof of guilt, credibility,
or intention.
A repair revises adoption of premises; it does not erase the original interview. If a speaker retracts a claim,
the first assertion, the retraction, and their order remain evidence records. A model of the speaker's current
position can cease adopting the old content while a chronology model still records that it was asserted.
These models answer different questions. The audit also distinguishes an explicit retraction from an
analyst's choice to distrust a statement. Conflating those events falsely attributes the analyst's judgment to
the speaker and can make a repaired account appear voluntarily corrected when no correction occurred.

CHAPTER 18. FINITE DECISION PROCEDURES AND PROOF
CERTIFICATES
18.1 A deliberately bounded executable semantics
The executable kernel decides explicitly encoded Boolean problems and validates typed record transitions.
It does not translate an ordinary conversation into formulas automatically. Human annotation supplies
speakers, question boundaries, proposition meanings, scope keys, source lineage, and candidate inference
rules. Computation then exposes consequences or defects of that annotation. A successful test establishes
behavior on a declared representation; it does not authenticate the conversation or establish the truth of its
historical content.
DEFINITION 18-D1. A kernel instance contains n declared Boolean atoms, a finite hard-premise list H,
and a query phi, with n <= 12. Every atom has a stable identifier and meaning record. Every formula uses
only declared atoms and supported connectives. A valuation is a mapping from those n atoms to
{FALSE,TRUE}. There are exactly 2^n valuations, including one empty valuation when n=0.
OBLIGATION 18-O1. Reject unsupported syntax, undeclared atoms, duplicate identities with conflicting
meanings, invalid types, and instances above the bound. Rejection is an input or scope error, not
REFUTED or INCONSISTENT. Richer quantified, temporal, or epistemic claims require a documented
finite encoding or a different semantic engine.
18.2 Exhaustive classification
Evaluate every premise and phi under every valuation. Retain only assignments satisfying all members of
H. Separate retained assignments into M_plus, where phi is true, and M_minus, where phi is false. Return
INCONSISTENT if both sets are empty; ENTAILED if only M_plus is nonempty; REFUTED if only
M_minus is nonempty; otherwise return UNDETERMINED. The order prevents an inconsistent theory
from being presented as evidentially successful.
PROPOSITION 18-P1. This procedure terminates and exactly classifies the instance under its declared
finite Boolean semantics.
Proof. The valuation inventory has finite size 2^n. Formula evaluation terminates by recursion on finite
syntax trees. Every valuation appears once, and conjunction of H is tested exactly, so the retained set
equals M(H). Classical evaluation makes M_plus and M_minus disjoint and exhaustive within M(H).
Their four possible emptiness patterns correspond exactly to the four reported statuses. No appeal to an
untested inference heuristic is needed.
The runtime grows exponentially with n and proportionally to the total evaluated formula size. The cap
makes exhaustive checking practical for small audit modules. A larger conversation is handled by verified
modular decomposition or a different engine; silently omitting atoms to remain under the cap changes the
problem.
EXAMPLE 18.A
An interviewer asks whether an employee worked on Monday. A recorded affirmative reply follows, and
only afterward the interviewer asks whether the employee deleted a file. The annotation assigns
proposition W to the first question and D to the second. A transition claiming that the earlier affirmative
explicitly answers the later question fails the ordinary forward-answer linkage check. A distinct
spontaneous disclosure of D would need its own source span and justification. The kernel identifies the
ordering defect without pretending to infer sarcasm, nods, omitted speech, or the speaker's actual intention
from timestamps alone.


PAGE 056 OF 108

18.3 Certificates make outputs inspectable
DEFINITION 18-D2. A countermodel certificate contains a complete declared valuation satisfying H and
falsifying the proposed conclusion. An UNDETERMINED certificate contains two valuations satisfying
H, one for each sign. An ENTAILED or REFUTED certificate identifies the exact instance and records
exhaustive rejection of the opposing sign, together with at least one model establishing consistency. An
INCONSISTENT certificate records exhaustive failure of H or another independently checked
unsatisfiability proof.
In a small kernel, the exhaustive valuation list or a reproducible deterministic run can serve as an audit
certificate. A bare label cannot. A checksum binds a result to bytes, but does not prove the evaluator
correct or the premises true. The certificate therefore states engine version, semantics, atom ordering,
premise versions, query, model counts, and witness assignments. A second evaluator can verify the result
from the same instance.
EXAMPLE 18.B
Let P mean a key was valid, Q mean the cabinet could be opened with it, and R mean a visitor arrived.
Adopt H = {P, P -> Q}, leaving R unconstrained. Exactly two of the eight assignments satisfy H:
P=TRUE,Q=TRUE with either value of R. Query Q is ENTAILED; query NOT Q is REFUTED; query R
is UNDETERMINED with the two assignments as witnesses. These results are model-relative. The
implication represents a declared operating rule and would require revision if a broken lock invalidated
that rule in the relevant context.
18.4 Transition validation and adversarial fixtures
Logical classification and record validation are separate outputs. A formula can be satisfiable while its
purported source attribution is wrong. Conversely, an accurately linked answer can report a false historical
proposition. Typed validators therefore check source existence, answer order, proposition identity, witness
bindings, scope and denominator keys, lineage, and rule-specific permissions without substituting those
checks for model evaluation.
Positive fixtures demonstrate permitted transformations. Negative fixtures deliberately alter one field or
dependency and require a failure for the relevant reason. Permutation tests reorder premises or rename
atoms bijectively while preserving meanings and expect unchanged semantic results. Boundary tests
include n=0, n=1, the n=12 limit, and rejection beyond it. Contradictory premises, tautological queries,
unsupported operators, and missing source records test different failure paths.
EXAMPLE 18.C
Return to the parcel model H = {S -> A}. Its retained (A,S) cases are 00, 10, and 11. A mutation
accidentally drops 10 during refinement. The remaining list now makes NOT S -> NOT A appear entailed,
because unsigned arrival has vanished. The reconstruction check compares the parent family with the
union of retained children and returns assignment 10 as the missing witness. Classification on the
corrupted list could be internally correct yet answer the wrong problem. Coverage verification detects the
earlier defect that produced the misleading conclusion.
18.5 Claims warranted by verification
PROPOSITION 18-P2. Agreement of two independently implemented evaluators on a finite fixture suite
establishes agreement on that suite, not universal correctness. Proof. The observation quantifies only over
tested inputs; any untested input remains outside its stated scope. Uniform correctness instead rests on the
decision-procedure proof and a justified correspondence between implementation and specification. Tests
supply evidence about that correspondence and find concrete violations.

OBLIGATION 18-O2. The verification report separates formal proofs, executed checks, expected
failures, unimplemented specifications, and unresolved source interpretation. It reports actual counts and
includes reproducible fixtures. A counterexample is attached to the precise failed claim, not generalized to
every part of the analysis. Within this boundary the kernel can completely check each accepted finite
instance, while the broader atlas continues to manage ambiguity, provenance, and assumptions that
computation cannot settle by enumeration alone.
A failed entailment check and a failed annotation check need different explanations. A countermodel says
the conclusion is not forced by the adopted premises; it need not contradict any source. An annotation
failure says a claimed transcription, scope preservation, or response link violates its stated contract; a
revised annotation might still support the same conclusion through another route. The verification package
should preserve both outputs when they coexist. It should also include successful controls beside
intentional failures, so that an implementation rejecting every input cannot pass merely by detecting
defective examples. Each mutation records the changed field and the specific obligation expected to fail.

CHAPTER 19. SOURCE INDEPENDENCE AND CIRCULAR
CORROBORATION
19.1 Origin is a separate dimension from agreement
Several people can report the same proposition while all reports originate in one observation, one
suggestion, or one mistaken document. Agreement describes relations among report contents.
Independence describes relations among their production mechanisms. Truth describes the world
proposition. None of these descriptions can silently replace another. The provenance wing at +y records
ancestry; the dependency wing at -y records how a conclusion uses that ancestry. Distinct speaker
identifiers do not establish distinct origins, and distinct origins do not establish statistical independence.
DEFINITION 19-D1. A provenance graph G=(V,A) contains immutable acquisition events, utterances,
documents, transformations, and derived claim records. An edge a -> b means that production of b used a
in the declared manner. Edge kinds include copying, paraphrase, prompting, observation, inference, and
translation. A root is a declared acquisition event with no represented predecessor. Roothood means a
boundary of the recorded investigation; it does not certify that no earlier influence existed.
Let Anc(v) be all ancestors of v and Root(v) the acquisition roots reachable backwards from v. The
certificate for v records both sets where finite representation permits. Unknown ancestry receives an
explicit unresolved marker. Treating unknown ancestry as an empty set would make missing
documentation appear maximally independent.
19.2 Several meanings of independence
DEFINITION 19-D2. Document-distinct means different artifact identifiers. Speaker-distinct means
different attributed utterers. Acquisition-distinct means different recorded acquisition events.
Lineage-disjoint means known root sets do not intersect and no ancestry is unresolved. Conditional
probabilistic independence additionally requires a declared probability model satisfying the relevant
factorization. These five predicates are stored separately.
For evidence variables E1 and E2 and hypothesis P, the factorization needed for multiplying likelihood
ratios is conditional under both P and NOT P. It is stronger than simply observing that two documents
were produced independently of one another.
Pr(E1,E2|P)=Pr(E1|P)Pr(E2|P). Pr(E1,E2|NOT P)=Pr(E1|NOT P)Pr(E2|NOT P).
OBLIGATION 19-O1. Any independence claim must name its level, conditioning information,
production process, and support. Shared access to a notice, common training, coordinated questioning, or a
common device can defeat a proposed probabilistic factorization even where copied wording is absent.
Conversely, common ancestry does not make a proposition false. It changes the amount and kind of
corroboration represented.
EXAMPLE 19.A
A warehouse supervisor states, "The loading door was unlocked." A clerk repeats the statement in an
incident log. An auditor later says, "Two accounts establish an unlocked door." Let r be the supervisor's
observation report, c the copied log statement, and a the auditor's conclusion. The recorded graph has r ->
c, r -> a, and c -> a. Therefore Root(c)=Root(r), subject to the same acquisition boundary.
The log establishes that a second artifact contains the allegation. It does not introduce a second
observation. In a model where the supervisor was mistaken, all three records can still occur exactly as
described. In a model where the supervisor was correct, the same graph also occurs. The source count is


PAGE 059 OF 108

one recorded observational origin; the artifact count is two. The correction is a provenance correction, not
a verdict on the door.
19.3 Cycles and founded support
Repetition can create a circle: a summary cites an interview, an interview refers to the summary, and a
later report treats both as corroboration. Directed cycles should be collapsed into strongly connected
components for inspection, while individual record identities remain available. A component indicates
mutual reachability, not mutual truth. Its incoming acquisition edges are the relevant places to inspect for
founded support.
DEFINITION 19-D3. A derivation is founded when every leaf is a declared premise or acquisition record
and every internal step has an authorized inference rule. A finite cyclic reference structure is not itself such
a derivation. A separate cyclic proof calculus would need an explicitly stated soundness condition;
ordinary reference loops supply none.
LEMMA 19-L1. Under a purely positive forward rule system, a cycle with no seed and no satisfied
incoming rule does not derive any member from the empty initial set. Proof: at stage zero no member is
present. If none is present at stage n, each internal rule lacks its required predecessor, and no incoming rule
is satisfied. None is added at stage n+1. Induction gives the claim for every finite stage; the least closure is
the union of those stages.
This result concerns rule activation, not the truth of arbitrary cyclic propositions. Both A -> B and B -> A
can hold when A and B are false, or when both are true. Their equivalence leaves the state unresolved until
an appropriate premise enters.
EXAMPLE 19.B
A clinic note says, "Discharge confirmed by reception." Reception later explains, "The discharge note
indicated completion." No discharge action, clinician observation, or completed workflow record is
identified. Set A=the note asserts completion and B=reception asserts completion. The speech records A
and B are supported. The content P=discharge occurred has two mutually referring accounts.
The atlas stores the note and statement in the center as records, and places P and NOT P in opposing
signed contexts. A real discharge could have occurred without its primary record being supplied;
alternatively, the two statements could have recycled an assumption. The circle therefore establishes an
audit gap. It does not establish either discharge or absence of discharge.
19.4 Provenance propagation without inflation
For a conclusion c derived from premises d1,...,dk, ancestry propagates by union. The union operation
preserves a repeated root once; it does not assign a fresh evidentiary origin to every derivation path. A
derivation can nevertheless have multiple valid proof paths. Proof multiplicity and observational
multiplicity are distinct properties.
PROPOSITION 19-P1. Repeated copying cannot enlarge the root set when each copy has exactly its
predecessor as its only parent. Proof: for a first copy c1 of r, Root(c1)=Root(r). If Root(ci)=Root(r), then
Root(ci+1)=Root(ci)=Root(r). Induction establishes the result for any finite chain. Branching copies
likewise preserve the union because union is idempotent: R union R=R.
The proposition does not say copying is useless. A dated copy can support a chronology of dissemination
or reveal that a person had access to wording. Such conclusions concern transmission and knowledge
opportunities, rather than fresh observation of the underlying event.

EXAMPLE 19.C
Two engineers independently photograph a cracked valve after both received an alarm from the same
sensor. The photographs are distinct acquisitions of visible condition, although both inspections share a
trigger. Let P mean a visible crack existed at inspection, and Q mean the sensor correctly detected the
original failure mechanism. The photographs can provide additional observational support for P. They do
not automatically provide two independent tests of Q.
The +y records distinguish shared alarm ancestry from separate camera acquisitions. The -y interface maps
each photograph to the proposition it can actually support. If both photographs show the same old cosmetic
mark, the original causal mechanism remains unresolved. Independence is consequently query-relative and
mechanism-dependent; a single global "independent source" flag would erase the distinction.
19.5 Required audit output
The northern checks report the number of artifacts, utterers, acquisition roots, unresolved ancestry links,
and cyclic components separately. Any corroboration conclusion names the proposition and independence
level involved. A reduction from four documents to one origin changes the certificate and any dependent
weighting, while leaving the embedded proposition available for separate investigation. An unknown
source is neither an independent witness nor an automatically unreliable witness. This discipline prevents
repeated language from acquiring authority merely through circulation.

CHAPTER 20. MEMORY, CUEING, CONTAMINATION, AND
OBSERVATION LIMITS
20.1 A report has several possible production paths
An utterance about a past event is a present action with historical content. The atlas separates the event,
available perception, retained information, later exposures, reconstruction, and current report. These
distinctions describe possible mechanisms. They do not authorize diagnosis of a speaker's memory or
intent from a transcript alone. The immutable record establishes what was recorded at the reporting time;
its historical content requires a separately declared evidentiary bridge.
DEFINITION 20-D1. A memory analysis record contains event e, event time te, perception opportunity
o, prior report r0, intervening exposures C, current report r1, reporting time tr, and observed qualifications.
A latent reconstruction state m may be introduced for a causal hypothesis but is not treated as observed.
The claim "the speaker remembers P" and the claim P have different types.
Possible paths include event -> perception -> reconstruction -> report, and cue -> reconstruction -> report.
A cue can also prompt a report without changing memory, such as an answer offered only to discuss a
hypothetical. Multiple paths can coexist. Their representation therefore requires a causal hypergraph or
explicit alternatives, rather than a single arrow asserting that the last question caused the answer.
20.2 Exposure and contamination claims
DEFINITION 20-D2. Cue exposure is a recorded opportunity to encounter content. Source contamination
is a hypothesis that later reported content derives partly from an external cue rather than the represented
original observation. Demonstrated dependence requires evidence beyond temporal succession alone. An
answer repeating a supplied term warrants a provenance flag even when the psychological mechanism
remains unresolved.
OBLIGATION 20-O1. Preserve the first recoverable formulation, the cue's wording and timing,
intervening questions, later repetitions, explicit source attributions, and uncertainty. Mark a missing
baseline as missing. Absence of an earlier recorded detail does not establish absence from memory,
especially when earlier questions did not solicit that detail. Conversely, later fluency does not establish
earlier independent recollection.
Research-informed interviewing guidance cautions against guessing and supplied information that can
contaminate an account [SRC-HIG-REVIEW]. The formal scaffold needs only the narrower operational
rule that provenance and alternatives remain recorded. It does not require a universal claim that every
leading question corrupts every answer.
EXAMPLE 20.A
An equipment operator initially states, "A panel light appeared." An interviewer asks, "Was the amber
warning flashing?" The operator responds, "Amber, perhaps; flashing seems possible." Let P=amber and
Q=flashing. The initial report supports LightAppeared, while the later record supports qualified responses
to P and Q after both were introduced.
Three compatible mechanisms are independent recollection prompted by relevant wording, reconstruction
influenced by the cue, and conversational willingness to consider the suggestion. No recorded observation
distinguishes them. The result is a valid record of qualified assent with cue provenance attached. Reporting
"the operator spontaneously remembered a flashing amber warning" fails both origin and certainty checks,
even if a later instrument log happens to establish P AND Q.

20.3 What behavior can and cannot settle
Pauses, gaze changes, corrections, emotional displays, answer length, and apparent confidence can be
transcribed as observations when the recording supports them. Their meaning depends on a model
connecting those observations to a target claim. The atlas provides no automatic rule from any such
behavior to truth, deception, guilt, impaired memory, or intent. A description of behavior and a
psychological explanation belong in separate cells.
LEMMA 20-L1. If two retained models agree on every represented behavioral observation but disagree
on P, no deterministic classifier using only those observations can correctly decide P in both models.
Proof: the classifier receives the same input in both models and therefore returns the same output. Since P
differs, at least one output is incorrect. Abstention avoids that forced error but does not resolve P.
This is an identifiability result relative to the retained models. It neither estimates real-world error rates nor
establishes that every conceivable behavioral measure is useless. A proposed model excluding one of the
witnesses must state and justify its exclusion rule. Merely naming nervousness, evasiveness, or sincerity
does not satisfy that obligation.
EXAMPLE 20.B
A technician pauses for twelve seconds before answering whether a backup ran. Model M1 assigns the
pause to careful retrieval of dates and makes the backup successful. Model M2 assigns the pause to
planning a false account and makes it unsuccessful. Both contain the same recorded pause and answer.
The pause belongs to the observational center; the two explanations occupy alternative cases. An
investigator may seek the scheduler log or ask for the remembered sequence, but cannot eliminate M1 by
renaming the pause "deceptive hesitation." The result remains UNDETERMINED for the backup, subject
to other evidence. A record of the pause is preserved without converting interpretation into observation.
20.4 Source memory and repeated interviews
A person may accurately recall a detail while being uncertain whether it came from direct observation, a
document, or another conversation. The proposition about the event and the proposition about its
remembered source therefore require separate variables. Repeated interviews can reveal stable content,
changed wording, newly supplied details, or explicit corrections. Stability can arise from accurate
memory, rehearsal, repeated suggestion, or reliance on a fixed document. Change can arise from
correction, confusion, new information, or changed willingness to disclose.
DEFINITION 20-D3. For a detail p and interview times t1,...,tn, the report trajectory is
T(p)=((ti,content_i,source_i,cues_i,qualification_i)). A missing report at ti is an omitted entry, not
Deny(s,p). Content identity requires the same actors, event, time scope, and modality; matching keywords
alone is insufficient.
OBLIGATION 20-O2. A comparison across interviews must specify whether the earlier questioning
provided a reasonable opportunity to report p, whether documents were consulted, whether the same event
was under discussion, and whether the later response corrects or merely supplements the earlier one. These
annotations support analysis without requiring an unsupported psychological verdict.
EXAMPLE 20.C
A receptionist says on Monday, "No cancellation is visible." On Tuesday, after reading a supervisor's
email, the receptionist says, "The client cancelled yesterday." The email states only that cancellation was
being considered. Let V=visible cancellation record on Monday, A=actual cancellation on Monday, and
E=email mentions possible cancellation.

The first report concerns NOT V. The second asserts A after exposure to E. Neither NOT V -> A nor E ->
A is licensed. An independent telephone record might later establish A, but that would not make the
receptionist's Tuesday source independent of the email. The result separates historical truth, screen
observation, and remembered explanation. The useful follow-up is to identify an actual cancellation action
and its source, with no assumption that the receptionist deliberately fabricated anything.
20.5 Preserving the uncertainty boundary
PROPOSITION 20-P1. Adding only a record that a cue occurred cannot, by itself, entail either truth or
falsity of its suggested content when both extensions remain permitted. Proof: take one model satisfying P
and one satisfying NOT P; extend each with the same cue record. Both still satisfy the augmented
premises. The signed alternatives therefore survive.
At +y, the system records exposure history; at -y, it records proposed mechanisms and required witnesses.
The northern checks ask whether a report was transformed into independent recollection, whether reported
certainty was upgraded, and whether behavior was used as an unauthorized truth rule. The center can
safely state that a detail was introduced after a cue while remaining silent on whether the cue altered
memory. That distinction makes contamination analysis useful without converting every imperfect
interview into a finding of false memory.

CHAPTER 21. QUANTITIES, DENOMINATORS, UNITS, AND
BOUNDS
21.1 A number requires an identity contract
A numerical answer is a claim about a counting operation, measurement, or estimate. The number alone
does not identify what was counted. Five files, five viewing occasions, five people, five days, and five
successful transfers can describe different aspects of the same activity. Numerical compression becomes
especially misleading when a question changes subject category or period but retains the previous number.
The quantities cube therefore connects to person, event, time, provenance, and modality cubes through a
joint key.
DEFINITION 21-D1. A quantity key K=(population,actor,event_predicate,object_class,location,period,u
nit,inclusion_rule,dedup_rule,modality) fixes the interpretation of a value v. Count(K) counts equivalence
classes of qualifying records under the declared deduplication rule. Rate(Kn,Kd)=Count(Kn)/Count(Kd) is
defined only when the denominator is positive and the numerator's relation to the denominator is specified.
The inclusion rule states whether attempted, completed, repeated, uncertain, or deleted events qualify. The
modality distinguishes observed count, remembered estimate, lower bound, upper bound, and hypothetical
forecast. A change to any material component produces a new proposition, even if the numeral and
principal noun remain unchanged.
OBLIGATION 21-O1. Numerical inference must preserve the complete key or provide an explicit
conversion certificate. Shared individual fields do not establish compatible keys. Pairwise links between
actor and period, period and unit, or unit and category cannot replace the full joint constraint.
21.2 Counts, rates, and selection
An observed subset may be selected by an alarm, search term, reporting threshold, or interview focus. Its
proportion does not automatically describe the containing population. A sample of damaged components
answers a different question from a random or complete inventory of all components. The atlas records the
selection process as part of provenance, including whether the process is unknown.
LEMMA 21-L1. If A subseteq B and |B|>0, then 0 <= |A|/|B| <= 1. Proof: inclusion gives 0 <= |A| <= |B|;
division by a positive number preserves both inequalities. If inclusion is absent, the ratio may exceed one
and must not be labeled the proportion of B satisfying A without another interpretation.
EXAMPLE 21.A
A mechanic says, "Ten service visits occurred this month, and three machines failed." An interviewer
summarizes, "Thirty percent of visits involved failure." Let V be service visits and M failed machines. The
values |V|=10 and |M|=3 use different units. No relation specifies which visits involved which machines.
One permitted model has all three machines inspected during one visit; another has each inspected during
three separate visits, producing nine relevant visits. Both preserve the given counts. The proposed
proportion therefore lacks an event mapping. A conversion certificate would require the visit-to-machine
relation and a rule for counting multi-machine visits. The result is a blocked transformation, not a
recalculated percentage.
21.3 Bounds are formulas, not rounded recollections
DEFINITION 21-D2. For a nonnegative integer count n, a certified interval [l,u] means l <= n AND n <=
u. An upper bound alone is [0,u] unless another lower bound is supplied. An approximation requires a
declared tolerance or remains a linguistic qualification. Words such as "maybe," "roughly," and "at most"
must remain attached until their numerical interpretation is explicitly justified.


PAGE 065 OF 108

An utterance such as "perhaps five to ten, at most" does not uniquely encode [5,10]. It may express a
tentative range, an uncertain upper cap, or hesitation over both endpoints. Alternative semantic annotations
can coexist as southern cases. Choosing [5,10] without recording this choice introduces a lower bound that
the speaker may never have endorsed.
PROPOSITION 21-P1. For nonempty intervals [l1,u1] and [l2,u2], every allowed sum lies in
[l1+l2,u1+u2]. Proof: adding lower inequalities yields the lower bound, and adding upper inequalities
yields the upper bound. This enclosing interval remains sound under dependence. Its endpoints need not be
attainable when additional joint constraints restrict the pair. Exact attainable totals require those
constraints.
EXAMPLE 21.B
Two inspectors estimate faulty units in separate locked crates: the first count lies in [2,4], the second in
[3,5]. If the crates contain distinct units, the total lies in [5,9]. A rule requiring exactly eight faulty units
across both crates would shrink the feasible sums to {8}; the interval calculation alone does not discover
that rule.
If the inspectors instead examined overlapping trays from one crate, adding counts would be invalid
because the counting domains may overlap. With exact counts four and five, the distinct union could range
from five to nine, subject to the size of the crate. The example demonstrates both a valid interval operation
and the different identity requirement for summing distinct objects.
21.4 Overlap and deduplication
For finite sets A and B, the shared members must be removed once when calculating the union. A
photograph, forwarding event, and copied file can all concern one underlying object while remaining
distinct artifacts. Deduplication must consequently name its target: identical bytes, a represented scene, a
message event, or a person. One target cannot silently substitute for another.
PROPOSITION 21-P2. If A and B lie in a finite universe of size N, then max(|A|,|B|) <= |A union B| <=
min(N,|A|+|B|). Proof: the union contains each set, proving the lower bound. Counting both sets counts
every union member at least once, proving the sum bound. The union is inside the universe, proving the N
bound. Without overlap information, these are bounds rather than an exact total.
An empty denominator yields an undefined rate, not zero. A missing denominator yields an uncomputable
rate, not an empty denominator. These states must remain separate from a measured zero numerator with a
known positive denominator. The northern diagnostics reject all three conflations.
EXAMPLE 21.C
A clinic clerk reports, "Six cancellations appeared among the twelve appointments reviewed." The
reviewed appointments were selected because a reminder had failed. Let S be the selected set, C
cancellations within S, and P the month's entire appointment population. The record supports |C|/|S|=6/12
within the reviewed group.
No sampling bridge relates this ratio to |cancelled(P)|/|P|. If all other appointments proceeded normally, the
monthly rate is lower; if most others were cancelled, it is higher. Both extensions preserve the selected
records. The 50 percent figure is valid for S and unsupported for P. A report should state the selection rule
and retain the larger population as an unresolved scope.

21.5 Temporal and computational certificates
Periods require boundary conventions: inclusive dates, time zones, and event-time versus recording-time.
A transfer recorded after midnight may concern an earlier action; combining periods without this
distinction can duplicate or omit an event. Rates per week also need a defined exposure period rather than
an assumed calendar length. A frequency estimate is not a probability model until a sample space and
conditioning regime are specified.
LEMMA 21-L2. For a fixed key K and nonempty finite model family M, let l=min_m Count_m(K) and
u=max_m Count_m(K). Every retained count lies in [l,u], and both endpoints have witnesses in M. Proof:
finite nonempty sets of integer counts attain a least and greatest member. Their ordering bounds every
other member. This exactness is relative to M and K. If the represented domain omits possible objects, the
result bounds the represented count only; it does not certify a complete historical inventory. An
open-domain adapter must retain a separate allowance for unrepresented qualifying objects or decline a
finite upper bound.
Each calculation certificate records input keys, source spans, interval assumptions, conversion rules,
arithmetic, and attainable witnesses where exactness is claimed. The finite checker can reject mismatched
keys and verify enumerated bounds, but it cannot authenticate the underlying counts. A correct equation
applied to a changed predicate remains an incorrect evidentiary inference. The linked blocks make the
identity contract visible before arithmetic creates an appearance of precision.

CHAPTER 22. CAUSAL AND ABDUCTIVE ALTERNATIVES
22.1 Explanation is a typed proposal
Interrogation often moves from a reported event to a proposed reason: a file was deleted because discovery
was feared, an answer changed because a lie failed, or a departure occurred because an incident was
anticipated. The movement introduces a causal or motivational claim. Even when the reported event is
established, its proposed explanation requires separate support. A plausible explanation belongs in a case
until the relevant alternatives and assumptions have been examined.
DEFINITION 22-D1. An abductive proposal a for explanandum e relative to premises H is admissible
when H union {a} is consistent and, under declared bridge rules, H union {a} |= e. The proposal must also
satisfy domain and scope restrictions. Admissibility means that a could explain e within the model; it does
not mean H |= a, nor that a is uniquely preferred.
An explanatory ranking may consider simplicity, coverage, independent support, or predictive
discrimination, but these criteria remain explicit. Minimality depends on the representation: one broad
assumption can conceal several unsupported clauses. Counting sentence length or number of named
hypotheses does not supply a neutral measure of explanatory merit.
22.2 The elementary reverse-inference failure
LEMMA 22-L1. From a -> e and e alone, a need not follow. Proof: assign a=FALSE and e=TRUE. The
implication and e are both true while a is false. This countermodel is excluded only by an additional
premise, such as e -> a, whose justification must be supplied separately.
The same failure appears when a familiar behavioral pattern is applied backwards. A person expecting
inspection might remove a file; removal does not establish that expectation. A question that asks only for
details of the proposed reason can background the missing reverse implication. The atlas retains the
observed action and the explanatory proposal in different cells.
EXAMPLE 22.A
An employee says, "The temporary files were removed Friday." An interviewer proposes, "That happened
because the audit was expected." Let D=removal, A=audit expectation, and R=routine retention expiry.
Rules A -> D and R -> D describe two possible mechanisms, with D supported by an accepted log.
One model has A AND NOT R; another has NOT A AND R. Both satisfy D. The models can be split into
disjoint combinations of A and R, including both causes or neither if other causes remain admitted. A
calendar notice received before removal would support an opportunity for expectation, but still requires a
bridge to actual expectation and causal influence. The result is an explanatory question with unresolved
alternatives, not an admission of concealment.
22.3 Observational and intervention models
DEFINITION 22-D2. A simple structural causal model consists of background variables U, endogenous
variables V, and equations assigning each endogenous variable from designated parents. The models here
are finite and acyclic, so parent values determine a consistent evaluation order. An intervention do(X=x)
replaces X's equation with X=x while leaving the others unchanged. This is a declared formal semantics,
not an assumption that real-world intervention is feasible or ethical.
Two such models can agree on all represented ordinary observations while predicting different
intervention outcomes. Consequently, chronological association or perfect observed correlation does not
by itself determine causal direction. The causal cube records equations and intervention assumptions; the


PAGE 068 OF 108

observational cube records what was actually measured. Their interface must identify exactly which
empirical constraints support the chosen equations.
PROPOSITION 22-P1. Let U be Boolean. Model A has X=U and Y=X. Model B has X=U and Y=U.
They agree on every observational pair (X,Y) for every U, but differ under do(X=0) with U=1. Proof:
ordinarily both yield X=Y=U. Under intervention, A gives Y=0 through its Y equation, while B retains
Y=1. Therefore observational agreement does not identify this intervention effect.
EXAMPLE 22.B
Every recorded machine shutdown coincides with a red indicator. Model A treats indicator circuitry as the
shutdown trigger. Model B treats overheating as a shared cause of the indicator and shutdown. The
observational records alone may fit both models. Disabling the indicator has different predicted effects
under the two equation sets.
The example is an analysis of hypotheses, not an instruction to bypass a safety device. An independent
circuit diagram, authorized test, or historical fault record may distinguish the models. The logical result is
that the correlation cannot decide the mechanism by itself. A witness's statement that the red light "caused"
the shutdown is recorded as that witness's causal attribution until corroborating structure is established.
22.4 Motive, opportunity, knowledge, and action
Motive can mean a desire, an incentive, a stated reason, an analyst's interpretation, or a legally defined
element. These meanings require different predicates. Opportunity concerns availability of an action;
capability concerns ability; intention concerns a represented plan or aim; action concerns occurrence. None
automatically implies all the others. A person can have access without acting, act without the alleged
motive, or offer an inaccurate retrospective explanation.
OBLIGATION 22-O1. A causal or motivational conclusion must name the target event, proposed cause,
temporal order, background conditions, alternative paths, and decisive evidence. The certificate
distinguishes a person's explicit stated reason from an independently established causal mechanism. It also
records whether the cause is necessary, sufficient, contributing, or merely associated within the proposed
model.
A claim that a cause precedes its effect uses event time, not transcript order. A later-discovered document
can establish an earlier notice, while a later question cannot establish earlier knowledge merely by
introducing the fact. Different knowledge times belong in different propositions even when their content is
identical.
EXAMPLE 22.C
A receptionist changes a status from "pending" to "complete" after a manager's call. Candidate A says the
manager supplied genuine completion evidence. Candidate B says the receptionist accommodated a
confident request without verification. Candidate C says an unrelated system update arrived at the same
time. All three can explain the observed status change.
The discriminator set includes the call content, system audit trail, permissions, and timestamps. A log
proving automatic update would weaken a manual-change explanation; a recorded request would establish
a cue without proving obedience. Until such evidence is supplied, the three proposals remain cases. The
result is a concrete acquisition plan derived from competing explanations, rather than a favored story
disguised as deduction.


PAGE 069 OF 108

22.5 Comparative explanatory certificates
For each proposal, the atlas records explanatory coverage, additional assumptions, independent
predictions, failed predictions, and unresolved variables. A hypothesis can be tailored to an observation
already used to construct it. Such fit requires an account of hypothesis selection before it receives a
corroborative interpretation. A prediction specified before separately acquired evidence supplies a
different provenance relationship; its evidentiary weight still requires an explicit model. Retrospective fit
alone does not certify a mechanism.
OBLIGATION 22-O2. Before claiming that a hypothesis failed, specify which observation the
hypothesis forbids under its retained auxiliary assumptions. If a mechanism predicts an effect only when
power is present, absence of the effect during a power outage does not refute that mechanism. Conversely,
repeatedly changing the auxiliary conditions after every result can make a proposal impossible to
distinguish from its rivals. The audit records each such revision and checks whether any feasible
observation still separates the alternatives. If none does within the declared observation language, the
correct output is observational indistinguishability at that resolution, not equal truth or equal probability.
The northern diagnostic asks whether rival cases were excluded by evidence or merely omitted. A
conditional conclusion may be appropriate: under bridge rules B and cause assumption a, e follows. That
conclusion preserves its conditions at -y and its derivation at +y. It enters the shared center as a conditional
statement if every retained model satisfies it, while the unconditional cause remains on its signed side.
Explanatory usefulness and established causation consequently remain available as distinct, testable
outputs.

CHAPTER 23. DECISION THRESHOLDS, COSTS, AND
OPTIONAL PROBABILITY
23.1 Logical status and practical action are separate outputs
An unresolved proposition can still require a practical decision, such as whether to request another log,
inspect equipment, or postpone a conclusion. Decision analysis specifies actions and consequences under
uncertainty. It does not convert uncertainty into factual entailment. Conversely, establishing a proposition
does not determine the best action without a goal, feasible actions, and relevant constraints. The atlas
keeps a decision cube linked to evidence and model cubes through explicit interfaces.
DEFINITION 23-D1. A finite decision specification consists of states W, feasible actions A, a loss
function L:A x W -> nonnegative real numbers, and optionally a probability distribution pi on W. The
distribution satisfies pi(w)>=0 and sum_w pi(w)=1. Every loss has a declared unit and interpretation.
Legal or ethical constraints on actions are feasibility conditions; they are not automatically exchangeable
numerical penalties.
No default uniform distribution is inferred from an enumerated model list. Model counts depend on
representation. A free independent Boolean variable doubles every model uniformly and leaves old-query
uniform proportions unchanged. By contrast, refining some old states into more descriptions than others
can change naive uniform weights without adding evidence. Probabilities therefore require a separately
justified measure and a statement of what uncertainty the measure represents.
23.2 Conditional probability under declared assumptions
DEFINITION 23-D2. For events P and E with Pr(E)>0, Pr(P|E)=Pr(P AND E)/Pr(E). If 0<Pr(P)<1 and
the relevant conditional probabilities exist, posterior odds equal prior odds multiplied by the likelihood
ratio. This identity organizes an adopted probability model; it does not supply missing priors or
likelihoods.
Pr(P|E)/Pr(NOT P|E) = [Pr(P)/Pr(NOT P)] [Pr(E|P)/Pr(E|NOT P)].
LEMMA 23-L1. The odds identity follows from the definition of conditional probability when its
denominators are positive. Proof: the common factor Pr(E) cancels in the posterior ratio. Substitute Pr(P
AND E)=Pr(E|P)Pr(P) and the corresponding expression for NOT P. If a denominator is zero, use direct
probabilities and state the boundary case rather than divide by zero.
OBLIGATION 23-O1. Every application names the sample space, prior, likelihood model, conditioning
information, dependence assumptions, and source of numerical values. Hypothetical numbers are labeled
illustrative. Source agreement cannot be translated into a likelihood ratio merely by counting agreeing
speakers.
EXAMPLE 23.A
Consider a purely invented twenty-unit probability table for a sensor exercise. Assign joint masses Pr(P
AND E)=3/20, Pr(P AND NOT E)=1/20, Pr(NOT P AND E)=4/20, and Pr(NOT P AND NOT E)=12/20.
These are mathematical inputs, not measured sensor performance. The prior Pr(P)=1/5 and posterior
Pr(P|E)=3/7 follow directly.
A copied alarm message E2 is exactly E, so Pr(P|E AND E2)=3/7. Treating the copy as conditionally
independent and multiplying its likelihood ratio again would produce 9/13. The incorrect increase comes
entirely from an unsupported dependence assumption. The provenance cube identifies the copy relation,
and the probability cube rejects the second update. The example verifies an algebraic safeguard without
claiming real-world calibration.


PAGE 071 OF 108

23.3 A threshold derives from specified losses
Let actions be a1 and a0, and let p=Pr(P|E). Suppose correct decisions have zero loss, a1 under NOT P
costs cFP>0, and a0 under P costs cFN>0. Expected losses are (1-p)cFP and p cFN respectively. The
optimal choice within this restricted model follows from comparing those expressions.
PROPOSITION 23-P1. Action a1 has no greater expected loss than a0 exactly when p >=
cFP/(cFP+cFN). Proof: (1-p)cFP <= p cFN is equivalent to cFP <= p(cFP+cFN). The positive denominator
permits division without reversing the inequality. Equality gives a tie. Different correct-action costs or
additional actions produce a different threshold.
This is a decision threshold, not a definition of truth, proof, credibility, or legal sufficiency. The loss
values must reflect the actual decision under consideration. A threshold chosen for inexpensive inspection
cannot be transferred to an accusation or consequential restriction.
EXAMPLE 23.B
In a hypothetical equipment exercise, unnecessary isolation costs four common accounting units and
failing to isolate a faulty component costs one. The other two outcomes have zero assigned loss. The
threshold for isolation is 4/5. These deliberately artificial values illustrate the derivation and have no claim
to engineering realism.
If the adopted probability is known only to lie in [1/3,2/3], continued operation has lower modeled
expected loss throughout that interval. If the interval is [3/4,9/10], the preferred action changes within it;
no single action is uniformly optimal under those two choices. The result identifies sensitivity to
unresolved probability assumptions. It does not establish that the component is safe, faulty, or suitable for
continued operation under actual safety rules.
23.4 Robustness and information acquisition
DEFINITION 23-D3. A credal set C is a declared nonempty set of admissible probability distributions.
Action a robustly dominates b when Expected_pi[L(a,W)] <= Expected_pi[L(b,W)] for every pi in C. If no
action dominates, the report may present the crossing assumptions or use a separately declared criterion
such as worst-case loss. That criterion must not be hidden inside an ordinary factual conclusion.
Evidence acquisition has cost, delay, and possible consequences. Its value depends on whether the result
can change a decision, not merely whether it makes a narrative more detailed. Under a finite probability
model and an ideal free observation, a decision-maker can always ignore the observation; this provides a
simple bound.
PROPOSITION 23-P2. The minimum expected loss after an optional, cost-free observation cannot
exceed the minimum expected loss before it, provided the old actions remain feasible and the model is
fixed. Proof: the set of allowed contingent policies includes each constant policy that takes the same action
for every observation. Minimizing over the larger policy set cannot produce a larger minimum.
Acquisition costs can reverse the practical benefit.
EXAMPLE 23.C
An audit team must choose between checking a scheduler log and interviewing a second employee who
read the first employee's summary. The log could distinguish whether a backup ran; the second interview
may merely duplicate the same source. Let Z be each proposed result and let actions include further
inspection or closing the technical inquiry.
For each acquisition, the team specifies possible Z values, their modeled relationship to the fault, and the
resulting action policy. If every Z leaves the preferred action unchanged, its decision value under that


PAGE 072 OF 108

model is zero before costs, although documentation value may remain. The calculation identifies the
assumptions needing evidence; it does not assign invented accuracy rates to the witnesses or log.
23.5 Reporting the model's reach
PROPOSITION 23-P3. Within a fixed probability model, if Pr(P)=0 and Pr(E)>0, then Pr(P|E)=0. Proof:
P AND E is a subset of P, so its nonnegative probability is at most zero. Dividing zero by Pr(E) gives zero.
This exposes a limitation of encoding unexamined possibilities as impossible. Evidence cannot recover a
state that the adopted measure has eliminated merely through ordinary conditioning. Revising the state
space or prior is a separate model revision and must be recorded as such. Likewise, Pr(E)=0 makes the
stated conditioning formula undefined; an unexpected observation does not authorize an arbitrary
posterior. These boundary checks belong before numerical output.
The northern certificate separates logical query status, adopted posterior range, chosen action rule, cost
sensitivity, and unresolved inputs. A probability calculation is reproducible only with its full numerical
specification. Where priors or dependence cannot be justified, symbolic formulas and sensitivity intervals
are appropriate outputs. The central theory continues to contain only propositions shared by the declared
model family; a high probability does not place a proposition there by itself. This interface preserves
practical reasoning without laundering a decision preference into an established fact.

CHAPTER 24. CORRECTIONS, RETRACTIONS, VERSIONING,
AND STALE CONCLUSIONS
24.1 Historical records survive current revision
A correction changes the current status of a claim without erasing the earlier utterance. An analyst may
conclude that a transcript misassigned a speaker, that a summary overstated an answer, or that a witness
retracts a statement. Each event has a different target and effect. The immutable record keeps what
happened at each recording or analysis time; the active theory identifies which premises and
interpretations are currently adopted.
DEFINITION 24-D1. A revision event
r=(id,target,revision_kind,new_content,author,revision_time,reason,source_spans) links to a stable earlier
record. Kinds include transcription correction, attribution correction, scope correction, qualification
restoration, evidentiary withdrawal, explicit retraction, and replacement interpretation. The old version
remains addressable. A revision does not silently reuse its target identifier with a new meaning.
Claim versions contain an active or superseded flag, while utterance records remain historical facts at the
record level. The statement Assert(s,P,t1) can remain supported after Deny(s,P,t2) is recorded. Whether P
is true, whether the speaker changed belief, and whether either statement was deliberate deception remain
separate questions.
24.2 Retraction is not ordinary negation
DEFINITION 24-D2. Retraction removes a commitment or evidentiary reliance. Negation asserts a
proposition's complement. Correction may replace P with Q, where Q need not equal NOT P. These
operations have distinct update rules. A witness can withdraw a confident time estimate while continuing
to affirm the event; an analyst can withdraw an inference without receiving new contrary evidence.
OBLIGATION 24-O1. Every update states which layer changes: raw transcription, speaker attribution,
semantic annotation, evidentiary support, adopted hard premises, defeasible rule, or report wording. A
correction at one layer must not silently propagate as a different kind of correction at another layer.
EXAMPLE 24.A
A clerk initially states, "The parcel arrived Monday." Later the clerk says, "Monday was a guess; arrival
occurred sometime that week." Let P=arrival Monday and Q=arrival within the named week. Assuming
Monday lies in that week, P -> Q, but Q does not entail P or NOT P.
The current commitment replaces a narrow date claim with Q and records uncertainty about P. The old
utterance remains in the historical ledger. The Monday branch and another-day branch both survive unless
separate evidence resolves them. Reporting "the clerk denied Monday arrival" would incorrectly convert a
withdrawal of certainty into negation. The result is a scope revision with preserved provenance.
24.3 Dependency invalidation
The atlas needs more than an amended paragraph. Every derived conclusion carries a certificate listing
premises, rules, versions, and interpretation choices. When one dependency changes, the certificate may
no longer justify its conclusion. Marking it stale is an instruction to recompute, not a declaration that the
conclusion is false.
DEFINITION 24-D3. For a dependency graph D, Affected(r) is the set of certificate nodes reachable from
the revised target through dependency edges. A certificate is fresh only when every referenced version
remains active or a checked compatibility map validates the substitution. Freshness concerns the
derivation, not the historical truth of its conclusion.


PAGE 074 OF 108

LEMMA 24-L1. If all certificates record complete dependencies, changing a premise outside a
certificate's ancestor set does not alter the inputs of that certificate. Proof: by definition, all inputs and their
recursive dependencies lie in its ancestor set. An outside change touches none. This result requires
complete dependency recording; omitted assumptions invalidate the guarantee.
Conversely, reachability is a conservative invalidation test. A reachable conclusion may remain provable
through an independent path, or the changed premise may have been redundant. The system therefore
marks affected certificates, recomputes surviving justifications, and distinguishes stale, revalidated,
unsupported, and contradicted outputs.
EXAMPLE 24.B
An engineer concludes that a pump was running from both a witness report A and a verified current log B.
The two proof certificates are {A,rule1} and {B,rule2}. The witness later retracts A. Only the first
certificate becomes invalid through that revision; the second can continue to support the conclusion if its
own premises remain active.
If a summary had merely listed A and B without recording alternative derivations, automatic withdrawal of
the entire conclusion would lose valid support. If B was actually copied from A, the supposed independent
certificate would also fail provenance review. The result depends jointly on proof structure and source
structure. Dependency invalidation alone cannot substitute for the lineage checks.
24.4 Formula support and version families
Alternative justifications can be represented as a disjunction of conjunctions. If conclusion C has proof
paths requiring {A,B} or {D}, its support expression is (A AND B) OR D, with each letter representing an
active qualified dependency rather than world truth. Removing A disables the first path but leaves D.
Shared subproofs require shared identifiers, preventing repeated assumptions from looking like separate
support.
PROPOSITION 24-P1. For classical hard-premise sets H1 subseteq H2, M(H2) subseteq M(H1). Proof:
every model satisfying all members of H2 satisfies every member of its subset H1. Consequently,
removing premises can enlarge the model family and destroy entailments. It cannot create a new
entailment absent from the stronger consistent theory, under an unchanged language and semantics.
The final qualification matters. A correction that changes meaning, adds a negation, or replaces a rule is
not mere premise removal. Nor should an inconsistent H2 be used as an ordinary source of universal
conclusions. The system first checks nonemptiness and classifies the zero-model case as INCONSISTENT
before comparing ordinary query statuses.
24.5 Revision across topic cubes
A changed person identity can affect event attribution, quantity counts, apparent contradictions, and source
independence simultaneously. A time correction can alter opportunity, knowledge-before-action, and
duplication boundaries. These effects travel through typed interfaces and hyperedges, not through
geometric proximity. Every affected cube records its input version and revalidation result; unrelated cubes
remain intact under the dependency completeness condition.
EXAMPLE 24.C
An interview transcript labels two speakers as one manager. A later audio review separates Manager M
and Technician T. Earlier analysis had treated their statements as one person's contradiction and had
deduplicated two inspection events as a single event. The attribution revision changes the person key and
may change both conclusions.


PAGE 075 OF 108

The system preserves the original transcript version, creates corrected speaker assignments, invalidates the
contradiction certificate, and reevaluates the event identity rule. Two speakers do not automatically mean
two independent observations; source lineage still requires checking. Nor do two utterances automatically
mean two inspection events. The result is a controlled reopening of specific identity-dependent claims,
with unaffected wording records preserved.
24.6 Reproducible continuation
Each released analysis has a version identifier, source manifest, active-premise list, interpretation registry,
and certificate inventory. A revision report states what changed, which conclusions were revalidated,
which became unresolved, and which remain pending. Prior outputs are retained as historical products
rather than silently overwritten. A fresh report can therefore reproduce both the earlier reasoning and the
reason it no longer applies.
The northern checks compare dependency completeness, active versions, source hashes, and stale outputs
before publication. The +y wing keeps revision provenance; the -y wing carries invalidation and
replacement proofs; signed contexts show newly reopened alternatives. Continuation is certified when the
new analysis accounts for changed inputs and preserves unchanged meanings. A larger or later document
alone supplies no such guarantee.

CHAPTER 25. END-TO-END AUDIT WORKFLOW
25.1 An audit as a controlled sequence of transformations
The workflow begins with a question about a record, not a verdict about a person. A workable commission
names the conversations included, the alleged inferences under review, the available media, and the level
of conclusion permitted. A transcript audit might establish that a speaker introduced a proposition; a
historical investigation additionally needs authenticated observations bearing on that proposition. Mixing
these commissions creates a false impression that detailed language analysis has verified the underlying
events. A declared boundary is therefore part of the analysis itself.
DEFINITION 25-D1. An audit state is A_k=(U,E,H,Q,M,V,C), where U contains immutable records, E
contains attributed evidence, H contains adopted premises and bridge rules, Q contains permitted queries,
M is the represented model family, V records versions and dependencies, and C contains coverage
obligations. Each transition A_k -> A_(k+1) has an operation name, input identifiers, output identifiers,
and a justification. A model representation can be symbolic; explicit enumeration is required only when
the chosen checking procedure demands it.
The first pass inventories recordings, timestamps, edits, transcription methods, language changes, and
speaker uncertainty. The second pass types entities and separates actual persons from reported roles. The
third pass annotates speech acts, questions, proposed backgrounds, answers, corrections, and omissions.
The fourth constructs propositions with fixed scope. Only then does the process assemble implications and
alternative cases. This ordering reduces accidental promotion of a polished paraphrase into the primary
evidence record, while allowing later correction when earlier annotation proves mistaken.
25.2 Gates and reversible decisions
OBLIGATION 25-O1. No consequential inference passes the next gate without a source span, typed
inputs, explicit scope, and either a checked rule or a visible assumption. Missing data produce a pending
obligation rather than an invented completion. A consequential inference changes the status of a requested
conclusion, excludes a previously retained alternative, or changes the attribution of a material statement.
OBLIGATION 25-O2. A run must check nonemptiness of M(H) before classifying a world query by
skeptical entailment. If no models remain, the report identifies conflicting premises and suspends ordinary
entailment classification. An empty family cannot be used to certify every requested conclusion through
vacuous quantification.
The gates are semantic typing, source coverage, premise consistency, question-answer linkage,
dependency propagation, branch coverage, and conclusion review. They need not execute once in a rigid
line. A speaker reassignment can force a return to annotation; an authenticated log can justify a new
factual constraint. Each return creates a versioned continuation. Earlier outputs remain reproducible from
their earlier input state even when their conclusions become stale.
EXAMPLE 25.A
An invented interview contains D: "The archive disappeared after the update?" E: "The folder was absent
from the screen." D: "So the update deleted it." The raw record contains three utterances. Let V mean
visible folder absence, X mean archive deletion, and C mean update-caused deletion. E asserts V. D asks
about a temporal sequence and then asserts C. The proposition map includes C -> X if that causal predicate
is defined to require deletion, but includes neither V -> X nor temporal succession -> C.
The workflow stops the proposed promotion at the dependency gate. One retained case has V true and X
false because the interface hides the folder; another has both true. The historical conclusion remains
undetermined under these premises. The record-level conclusion that D introduced the deletion


PAGE 077 OF 108

explanation is supported by source order. An authenticated storage check would address X; a subsequent
confident recap would not.
25.3 Atlas construction and diagnostic movement
The topic cubes should follow distinct evidentiary questions: identity, event, timing, handling, and motive
often require separate cubes. Shared interfaces carry stable proposition identifiers, never merely similar
wording. The center stores conclusions shared across the declared nonempty family. The southern blocks
retain alternatives; the northern pyramid checks typing, consistency, coverage, witnesses, and results. The
negative-y wing carries dependency constructions, and positive-y retains source history and scope. Signed
z contexts examine a selected P and NOT P. These positions organize the record and do not confer
evidentiary weight. [SRC-PREF]
DEFINITION 25-D2. A coverage ledger associates each material span or inference with a disposition in
{represented, excluded_with_reason, pending}. Coverage is complete relative to the declared input set
exactly when every member has a disposition and no requested conclusion depends on a pending item.
This is administrative completeness of the audit, not completeness of available evidence about reality.
Each material conclusion receives a positive test and a negative test. Finding a model of H AND P
establishes compatibility, not entailment. Finding models of both H AND P and H AND NOT P
establishes underdetermination in the declared family. Finding no models on either side identifies
inconsistency. The distinction remains necessary even when one branch appears psychologically more
plausible, because plausibility has not become a hard constraint.
EXAMPLE 25.B
An invented service call contains A: "Was the delivery late?" B: "The driver arrived at eleven." A: "That
confirms lateness." The audit first asks whether "late" refers to a booked window, a personal expectation,
or a contractual standard. Let T=11 and let L=(T>D), with D the agreed deadline. Models with D=10
satisfy L; models with D=12 satisfy NOT L. The time report alone leaves both branches.
An appointment record later establishes D=10 in a designated branch of authenticated-document
assumptions. That new constraint legitimately removes the D=12 branch. Renaming the arrival time "late
arrival" would instead conceal an unsupported restriction. The continuation ledger records the appointment
record as the reason for exclusion and identifies every resulting conclusion that depends on its authenticity.
25.4 Review, release, and bounded assurance
LEMMA 25-L1. Suppose an output assertion has a complete finite derivation whose dependency relation
is well founded: every required premise of every inference is included, every leaf is an admitted input, and
every inference preserves consequence under H. Then the output follows from those admitted inputs under
H. Proof: a finite well-founded dependency graph admits an ordering with every premise before its
conclusion. Induction through that ordering establishes every derived node, including the output. A single
grounded path is insufficient if another required premise is unsupported. The lemma does not establish that
admitted inputs describe the external world accurately.
A review pass checks whether uncertain language survived, whether every decisive "yes" has a justified
antecedent, and whether alternative co-reference assignments change the result. A second reviewer can
challenge the smallest decisive transition rather than merely read the final narrative. Technical checks
additionally verify identifiers, source spans, acyclic proof dependencies where required, and rejected
mutations. These checks complement rather than replace interpretation of language.

CHAPTER 26. QUESTIONING STRATEGIES AND COMPARATIVE
EVALUATION
26.1 Strategy as observable organization
A questioning strategy can be analyzed as the ordering of prompts, responses, proposed explanations, and
opportunities for correction. Intentional manipulation is a separate hypothesis. A prompt can introduce an
unsupported premise without proving that its author intended deception. Conversely, sincere confidence
does not repair a faulty inference. The audit therefore measures observable transformations first and
reserves psychological attribution for evidence that bears on that attribution.
DEFINITION 26-D1. A strategy trace is S=((q_1,r_1),...,(q_n,r_n),J), where q_i is a structured question,
r_i is its candidate response set, and J records interruptions, repairs, summaries, evidence disclosures, and
changes of topic. Candidate response sets can overlap when attachment is uncertain. A strategy profile is a
declared collection of features computed or annotated from S; it is not a latent personality diagnosis.
Useful features include proposition introduction, answer freedom, alternatives explicitly offered,
interruption timing, repetition after denial, correction uptake, and later fidelity to earlier qualifications.
Each feature has an operational definition. The number of occurrences of "so" is merely a lexical count.
The number of unjustified increases in certainty requires semantic comparison. These quantities should
never be reported as though they measured the same phenomenon.
Four recurring forms deserve separate checks. "So" can mark a transition, a faithful consequence, or an
unsupported leap. "Is it safe to say" proposes a candidate formulation whose clauses still require scoped
endorsement. "Assuming" opens a local premise context; a conclusion depending on that premise must
retain the condition unless independent support establishes the premise or justifies the conclusion without
it. A disclaimer about "not putting words into a speaker's mouth" is a statement about the interviewer's
conduct, not a certificate that the resulting paraphrase preserves meaning. The audit compares the actual
before-and-after claims and response. These rules apply equally to a female detective, a male supervisor,
or any other interviewer; role and gender do not determine inferential validity.
26.2 Legitimate narrowing and answer shaping
A broad invitation can obtain a narrative, while a focused question can resolve a precise ambiguity.
Neither form guarantees reliability. A broad invitation may embed an accusation in its background; a
narrow question may accurately distinguish two dates already volunteered. The critical assessment
concerns what information the question supplies and what response would count as disagreement. Some
interviews require exact sequencing, quantities, or device identities; precision becomes leading when a
supplied alternative acquires stronger status than the response warrants.
DEFINITION 26-D2. Let New(q,E) be the material propositions introduced by a question that are not
already attributed to the respondent in E. A question has an introduction burden whenever New(q,E) is
nonempty. This definition identifies provenance work; it does not classify every such question as
improper. Questions necessarily introduce some information, including ordinary topic labels.
OBLIGATION 26-O1. When a proposition introduced by a question later appears as an admission, the
audit must identify an intelligible endorsement, its scope, and any surviving qualifications. The
endorsement can supply additional evidence of agreement, but cannot retroactively make the respondent
the original introducer.

An invented exchange begins E: "The package arrived around lunch." D: "Was that before or after the

26.3 Compound prompts and conversational cost
A compound prompt can reduce conversational effort while increasing logical ambiguity. Agreement with
"separate and temporary account" may endorse both descriptions, one salient description, or the broader
conversational point. Linguistic context can resolve that ambiguity, but the decision needs justification.
The audit retains alternative attachments when the record does not settle them. The same applies to
politeness, brief acknowledgments, and attempts to end a call: their possible social functions do not prove
either assent or nonassent.
DEFINITION 26-D3. A correction opportunity is a recorded interval or explicit invitation in which a
respondent can contest a particular formulation. Correction uptake requires an additional condition: the
revised account is retained in later summaries and dependent conclusions. Opportunity alone is not uptake,
and silence during an opportunity is not automatically endorsement.
A conversational burden can be described without pretending to measure a hidden mental state. Record the
number of new propositions, alternative interpretations, and temporal shifts inside a prompt. These are
annotation-dependent structural counts. They can motivate close review, but no universal threshold
establishes coercion, error, or credibility. Likewise, an accommodating tone may coexist with strong
control of the available narrative categories.
EXAMPLE 26.B
An invented exchange contains D: "The account was anonymous, temporary, and created to hide the

A=identity not discoverable, T=temporary intention, and H=purpose of hiding the purchase. E asserts N.

That explanation remains testimony rather than automatic historical truth. Separating the prompts clarifies
the source and scope of each commitment.
26.4 Evidence presentation and sequence effects
When an interviewer presents a photograph, log, or reported witness account, subsequent answers occur
after that disclosure. The audit records the information available at each turn. A later correct detail may be
independently remembered, inferred from the exhibit, adopted from the interviewer, or a mixture. The
transcript can establish that disclosure preceded the detail; it cannot by itself identify the internal memory
mechanism.

26.5 Comparing entire strategies
PROPOSITION 26-P1. A strategy can preserve every recorded correction while still introducing
unsupported propositions elsewhere. Proof: consider a trace with one corrected date retained accurately
and a separate unsupported motive assertion. Correction fidelity holds for the date, while the motive
transition fails. Therefore a single global label cannot replace proposition-level assessment.
Comparison reports should state the evaluation task: eliciting a free narrative, resolving dates, checking an
allegation, or testing a competing explanation. Metrics can include unresolved attachment counts,
preserved qualifications, newly supplied material propositions, and audited corrections. A smaller count of
leading prompts does not prove a more accurate account. The defensible output is a pattern of observable
strengths, defects, and unresolved effects, with each judgment traceable to specific turns.

CHAPTER 27. REPORTS, DOMAIN ADAPTERS, AND AUDIT
CONCLUSIONS
27.1 A common kernel with explicit boundaries
The same record discipline can support workplace investigations, administrative calls, journalism,
historical testimony, customer complaints, and research interviews. Portability comes from preserving
entities, claims, provenance, scope, and inference obligations. It does not come from forcing every domain
into one substantive definition of truth or wrongdoing. "Released," "authorized," "delivered," and "aware"
can carry different operational meanings across institutions. An adapter must expose those meanings
before the kernel compares statements containing them.
DEFINITION 27-D1. A domain adapter D=(T,F,I,B,Q) consists of a type vocabulary T, a translation F
from selected domain expressions to typed formulas, identity conditions I, declared bridge rules B, and
permitted queries Q. F may be partial or return several candidate formulas. Unresolved translation is
retained as alternatives rather than replaced by whichever meaning makes a desired conclusion follow.
OBLIGATION 27-O1. The adapter must specify the interpretation of each material predicate, the
temporal scope of state changes, and the evidentiary status of imported rules. Institutional documentation,
analyst assumptions, and verified event observations remain different source classes. A software policy
that usually runs at midnight is not an observation that a particular job actually ran then.
27.2 Report layers and conclusion language
A report should begin with the strongest warranted finding and then make its dependency structure
inspectable. The principal layers are record findings, interpretive findings, model consequences,
unresolved alternatives, and external questions. Record findings concern what appears in the material.
Interpretive findings concern attribution and meaning. Model consequences follow from declared
premises. External questions identify authentication, legal, medical, or factual decisions beyond the
present kernel.
DEFINITION 27-D2. A conclusion certificate is
K=(query,status,premise_version,semantics,source_spans,proof_or_witness,limits). For ENTAILED or
REFUTED, the certificate provides a proof or finite model check under the declared semantics. For
UNDETERMINED, it supplies opposing witnesses. For INCONSISTENT, it supplies a conflicting subset
when available and prohibits ordinary certainty claims derived from emptiness.
Terms such as "confirmed," "admitted," "established," and "proved" should have locally declared uses.
"Admitted in the conversation" can mean an identifiable communicative commitment, not verified
occurrence. "Established under H" can be mathematically correct while H contains disputed assumptions.
A readable report makes those distinctions once, then applies them consistently rather than burying every
sentence in repetitive qualifications.
EXAMPLE 27.A
An invented delivery complaint contains Clerk: "The parcel was delivered." Customer: "The tracking page
says delivered, but the doorstep was empty." A shipping adapter separates S=status marked delivered,
P=parcel placed at the intended address, and R=recipient obtained the parcel. The clerk's statement may
refer to S or P. The customer's statement supports a report of S and an observation of absence at a
particular time, not an unqualified assertion that no delivery ever occurred.
A faulty report converts S into R. A corrected certificate classifies R as undetermined under premises
limited to the conversation and the stated observation. A photograph, scanner location, or neighbor


PAGE 083 OF 108

account could constrain P separately. The analogy transfers to any system where a workflow label is being
treated as the event that label is intended to represent.
27.3 Adapter validation and conservative translation
LEMMA 27-L1. If a translation F preserves the truth of every permitted query across a mapping between
source models and target models, then source-query classifications are preserved whenever that mapping
covers all relevant source and target possibilities. Proof: apply truth preservation to each related model;
coverage prevents an unrepresented model from changing the universal or existential classification.
Without coverage, truth preservation on a few matched cases is insufficient.
This lemma sets an obligation, not an automatic achievement. Ordinary language rarely arrives with a
complete source semantics. Practical adapters therefore document representative counterexamples and
known ambiguities. A definition such as "removed means absent from the live index" must not silently
become "destroyed from all storage." Where full preservation is unproved, the report states that the
translation is a reviewed abstraction and lists which distinctions it intentionally leaves outside scope.
EXAMPLE 27.B
An invented laboratory discussion contains Technician: "The sample was negative." Reviewer: "Then the
substance was absent." The adapter distinguishes a reported test result N, a nonnegative concentration C, a
positive detection threshold d>0, and a validated test model. Even a stipulated deterministic rule N <->
C<d would establish a range, not C=0. Actual error rates and validity conditions require their own
evidence and model.
The faulty inference replaces a threshold claim with an absence claim. A model with C=d/2 and N true
witnesses the failure under the simple rule. A clinical or regulatory interpretation cannot be supplied by the
kernel merely because this arithmetic example is transparent. The resulting report identifies the
overstatement and names the additional domain decision needed for practical action.
27.4 Reviewable conclusions and adversarial use
An adversarial reader should be able to reconstruct the strongest competing interpretation without
rebuilding the entire audit. The report therefore includes the decisive source spans, exact disputed
transition, retained alternative, and evidence that would discriminate the branches. This structure makes
disagreements local. One reviewer may contest an entity match while accepting the rest of the derivation;
another may accept the match but reject a causal bridge.
OBLIGATION 27-O2. Every adverse characterization must identify whether it describes an observable
conversational operation, an inferred strategy, a historical action, or a legal or clinical category. Evidence
adequate for one category is not automatically adequate for another. "Introduced an unsupported premise"
is a record-based analytic conclusion; "intended to fabricate testimony" requires additional support about
intent.
EXAMPLE 27.C
An invented historical interview contains Witness: "I heard the factory would close." Interviewer: "So
management had decided on closure." The adapter separates Hearing(w,rumor,t),
Decision(management,closure,t), and announcement. The first statement can be accurately recorded even
if the rumor was false. A later newspaper copying the interview has a provenance edge to the same
statement, not an independent witness edge.
The corrected report states that closure rumors were reported and that the interviewer introduced the
decision characterization. Both a world with an actual decision and a world with only speculation satisfy
the limited historical premises. A dated board resolution could resolve the decision question while leaving

open whether the witness knew of it. Historical truth, public rumor, and personal knowledge therefore
remain separate linked topics.
27.5 Release package and continued use
A portable report package should contain the readable report, a proposition dictionary, an evidence ledger,
model assumptions, issue dispositions, and reproducible checks. Personal data can be replaced by stable
pseudonyms in a public edition, while a controlled mapping preserves authorized internal verification.
Redaction creates a new edition; it must not falsely suggest that omitted material never existed.
Checksums verify file identity, not correctness, authenticity of original events, or lawful access.
PROPOSITION 27-P1. A report remains reproducible after extension if its input versions, translation
version, rule set, and checking configuration remain available. Proof: these jointly determine the
documented run; executing that run reconstructs its formal outputs, subject to deterministic
implementation and recorded dependencies. Reproducibility does not guarantee agreement on annotations.
The package consequently preserves both machine results and the human decisions that gave those results
their meaning.

28.1 Invented record and question under review
This case is entirely synthetic. Dana, a supervisor, interviews Mira, an engineer, after a project archive
disappears from a dashboard. Omar is a technician mentioned in the conversation, not another name for

U01 Dana: "What changed this morning?"
U02 Mira: "At eight forty I renamed the project folder. Approval had not been granted when I made that
change."
U03 Dana: "So that removed the backup?"
U04 Mira: "It disappeared from the dashboard. The files might still be in live storage."
U05 Dana: "Omar says the archive was gone at nine."
U06 Mira: "I did not check the snapshot service."
U07 Dana: "Is it safe to say the only backup was deleted by that change?"
U08 Mira: "I cannot say that. I only changed the name."
U09 Dana: "The report will record an unauthorized change and loss of the backup."
DEFINITION 28-D1. Let R mean Mira renamed the project folder at 08:40, A mean approval had been
granted before that act, V mean the dashboard lacked its previous archive entry at 09:00, X mean the live
archive files were deleted, B mean another usable backup existed at 09:00, and I mean Mira intentionally

of intentional deletion. No rule R -> X is included. P is a deliberately reduced proxy, not a formal
equivalent of the full allegation: it omits the original archive's usability and the absence of all usable
backups at the deletion time.
28.2 Claim ledger and evidentiary boundary
The ledger records C01=Assert(Mira,R AND NOT A), introduced in U02; C02=Ask(Dana,whether R
caused X), introduced in U03; C03=Assert(Mira,V_u), where V_u concerns dashboard absence at U04's

C05=Assert(Dana,Reports(Omar,gone,09:00)), U05; C06=Assert(Mira,not_checked(snapshot_service)),
U06; and C07=Rejects(Mira,proposed_conclusion), U08. V_u is not automatically identical to V. U09 is
Dana's planned characterization. The word "gone" in C05 remains unresolved between index absence and

The admission of acting without prior approval originates with Mira. An audit that attributes every adverse
fact to leading would distort the record. Her account does not establish intentional destruction. Calling the
change "unauthorized" additionally requires a policy bridge: delegated authority might permit some
changes without prior approval. The kernel establishes who supplied the admission and its scope;
organizational consequences require the policy adapter.
OBLIGATION 28-O1. A conclusion about P requires links among the actor, act, affected data, event
time, intention, and absence of alternatives. Agreement about a folder name cannot substitute for those
joint constraints. Omar's observation also requires clarification of what interface or storage location he

28.3 Opposing cases in the linked atlas

all. The same visible result thus has different local explanatory witnesses. Selecting the delay witness in
one branch cannot export discharge into the center. Nor does the existence of any explanatory witness
show that the institution actually followed that mechanism. Further observations must connect the
mechanism to this event.
EXAMPLE 29.B
A synthetic variant adds Bea: "The directory refreshes every thirty minutes." Avery: "Then the release was

Possible(T), AcceptForDiscussion(Bea,T,c), and Assert(Bea,T) are distinct annotations. The word

APPENDIX A. NOTATION, TYPES, AND SEMANTIC CONTRACTS
A.1 The four layers
The record layer contains events such as a person uttering a sentence, a camera capturing an action, a clerk
searching a database, or an analyst making an annotation. The interpretation layer contains candidate
readings of those events. The model layer contains declared objects and assignments satisfying explicit
premises. The assessment layer contains conclusions whose scope is determined by the preceding layers. A
transition between layers is a substantive operation. It must retain its source and the rule or judgment that
authorizes it.
For example, a recorded sentence can support the fact that its speaker asserted a proposition. That assertion
can provide evidence for the proposition. The proposition may then be adopted as a defeasible premise or,
under an explicit policy, a hard premise. These are different transitions. The source record alone does not
silently execute all of them. Likewise, a finite countermodel can refute an entailment while leaving the
factual allegation open. The countermodel is a witness to a logical possibility within the declared
abstraction.
A.2 Basic objects
U denotes a collection of immutable utterance or observation records. Ent denotes a typed entity registry.
C denotes a context registry. T_e denotes event times; T_u denotes utterance times; T_a denotes analysis
or acquisition times. E denotes the evidence ledger. H denotes the selected hard premises and bridge rules.
L denotes the declared language. M(H) denotes the class of models of H in that language and semantics.
The family used for ordinary skeptical classification must be nonempty. An empty family receives an
inconsistency status instead.
A proposition phi is not merely a phrase. Its identity includes predicate, arguments, context, event-time
scope, and bound-variable structure. The atomic proposition Delete(employee_7,file_12,t_3) is different
from Delete(employee_7,file_13,t_3), Delete(employee_7,file_12,t_4), and
Believe(manager_2,Delete(employee_7,file_12,t_3)). The spelling of a name is an attribute of an entity
record, not a sufficient identity criterion. A stable ID remains attached to the same declared object even
when the displayed label is corrected. A substantive identity revision requires an explicit versioned map.
A claim record stores the formula, the person who uttered it, the person who first introduced it in the
available record, its speech act, its time fields, its context, modality, quantifier scope, source spans,
dependencies, qualifications, corrections, and version. Unknown fields are marked unknown. Empty or
null fields are not permission to infer a convenient value. A missing answer is an absence in the available
representation, not a negative answer in the underlying event.
A.3 Formula notation
NOT phi is negation. phi AND psi is conjunction. phi OR psi is inclusive disjunction unless an exclusive
operator is separately defined. phi -> psi is material implication in the Boolean kernel. phi <-> psi is
equivalence. H |= phi means that every model of H satisfies phi under the declared semantics. It is not a
probability statement or a claim that H describes the actual world.
forall x:D phi(x) and exists x:D phi(x) specify the quantifier domain D. A change of domain changes the
claim. The statement exists x:D P(x) permits different witnesses in different models. It does not by itself
identify one particular individual, establish uniqueness, or supply a uniform witness across all contexts. An
existential fact about a sender cannot be silently joined to an existential fact about a recipient as though
both variables name the same person.


PAGE 095 OF 108

An implication edge is directed. A compatibility relation is symmetric when it means that two formulas
have a common satisfying model. An identity relation requires its declared equivalence conditions. A
temporal relation has its own ordering properties. A provenance edge records derivation or copying and
does not by itself establish the truth of the copied content. These edge types cannot be substituted for one
another because they have different meanings.
A.4 Speech and epistemic operators
Assert(s,phi) records assertion. Deny(s,phi) records denial. Ask(s,q) records a question. Presuppose(q,phi)
records background introduced by a question under an explicit reading. Assume(c,phi) records a local
assumption. AcceptForDiscussion (s,phi,c) records a conversational commitment of the declared kind.
Believe (s,phi) and Know(s,phi) belong to an adopted epistemic model. A report that a person knows phi is
still a report and is not automatically factive.
DEFINITION A-D1. In the default record semantics, Assert(s,phi) and phi are distinct atoms or
separately interpreted formulas. No bridge from the former to the latter is available unless explicitly
supplied. The same default applies to belief attribution and acceptance for discussion.
This distinction does not make testimony worthless. It makes the use of testimony visible. A
domain-specific policy can assign weight, establish corroboration requirements, or admit an assertion as a
premise. The policy itself then appears among the dependencies of every conclusion that relies on it.
A.5 Support and model classification
The support pair e(phi)=(s_plus,s_minus), with each component in {0,1}, records whether the declared
evidence policy supplies positive or negative support. (0,0) is NEITHER; (1,0) is POSITIVE_ONLY; (0,1)
is NEGATIVE_ONLY; (1,1) is BOTH. These four labels are not four alternative truth values of the world.
They also do not define a full paraconsistent calculus for compound formulas. Such a calculus would
require its own connective tables and proof rules.
For a nonempty finite M(H), phi is ENTAILED when all retained models satisfy phi; REFUTED when all
satisfy NOT phi; UNDETERMINED when each side has a witness. When M(H) is empty, the classifier
returns INCONSISTENT before evaluating those three categories. The classical definition of entailment
permits vacuity on an empty model family; this reporting policy deliberately prevents that mathematical
convention from appearing as evidentiary certainty in an audit.
EXAMPLE A.A. A maintenance supervisor states that a valve was closed. The record supports
Assert(supervisor,Closed(v1,t1)). A model with the assertion true and the valve open is allowed until an
evidentiary bridge is added. A sensor record may provide a separate bridge. The correct result is a recorded
assertion with a separate factual assessment, not automatic certainty and not automatic disbelief.
EXAMPLE A.B. A clinic worker reports that a patient is absent from the active list. The relevant atom is
Absent(active_list,patient,t_query). It is not identical to Discharged(patient,t_previous). A branch in which
the person was discharged and a branch in which the list is incomplete can both satisfy the observation.
The additional inference requires a rule about the list's coverage and updating.
EXAMPLE A.C. Two witnesses disagree about whether a package was delivered. Under a policy that
records each testimony as support, e(Delivered)=(1,1). Both speech records can be true even if the delivery
proposition has only one world truth value. A selected hard-premise view that adopts both Delivered and
NOT Delivered is inconsistent. The evidence ledger itself need not be logically inconsistent.

APPENDIX B. RULE CARDS AND PROHIBITED AUTOMATIC
PROMOTIONS
B.1 How to read a rule card
Each rule card states an input type, a permitted output, a guard, and a residual obligation. A guard checks a
necessary condition for a transition; passing the guard does not automatically prove every substantive
premise. A rule can be logically valid and still be unsound as an account of reality because one of its input
premises is unsupported. Conversely, an interpretation can be plausible without being a strict entailment.
Its output must then remain a defeasible reading with explicit alternatives and defeat conditions.
RULE B-R01, RECORD. From an authenticated span and a defensible speaker attribution, create a
record that the identified utterance occurred. Preserve the exact span, uncertainty, recording time, and
attribution basis. This rule does not prove the truth of the utterance's content. If attribution is ambiguous,
create separate candidate records or a set-valued attribution, not a fabricated single speaker.
RULE B-R02, PARSE. From a record and a declared language/domain interpretation, create one or more
typed candidate formulas. The output includes a mapping from words or gestures to formula components.
Competing readings remain separate. Parsing does not authenticate events, establish intention, or delete
uncertainty.
RULE B-R03, CONTEXTUAL ANSWER. Link an answer to a recoverable question or other proposal.
Preserve temporal order, the target proposition, answer polarity, scope, and qualifications. Adjacency is
evidence for linkage but is not always decisive, particularly with interruptions, delayed answers, multipart
questions, or edited recordings. An affirmative answer can communicate the target proposition; it remains
a statement with a provenance history.
RULE B-R04, LOCAL ASSUMPTION. Place phi in a named hypothetical context c and derive
consequences within c. The output must retain that context. Exporting a consequence as an unconditional
world claim requires an additional discharge or bridge rule. A useful discharge yields phi -> psi from a
derivation of psi under phi; it does not yield phi itself.
RULE B-R05, CONJUNCTION. From phi and psi in compatible scopes and a declared world-premise
layer, derive phi AND psi. From phi AND psi derive either conjunct. This requires that the two records
refer to the relevant same context and that the premises are being used at the correct layer. A statement
about one file and another statement about a different file cannot be conjoined into a single-file account
without an identity premise.
RULE B-R06, DISJUNCTION. From phi derive phi OR psi. From phi OR psi and NOT phi derive psi in
a classical view. From phi OR psi alone neither individual disjunct follows. The alternatives must match
the same scope. A disjunctive reporting category such as uploaded, stored, or forwarded cannot be silently
resolved into one particular act.
RULE B-R07, SUMMARY. A strict faithful summary sigma of source content phi requires H |= phi ->
sigma, with the selected scope and interpretation declared. Operational approval also requires M(H) and
M(H AND phi) to be nonempty. An inconsistent context or impossible source condition is flagged instead
of allowing a vacuously true implication to certify an unrelated summary. If equivalence is claimed,
require H |= phi <-> sigma. A summary may omit details while remaining a consequence, but omission can
still be pragmatically misleading when it removes a material qualification from the communicated
conclusion. Logical preservation and communicative fairness are separate checks.
RULE B-R08, QUANTITY TRANSFER. A numerical value can move only through a declared mapping
that preserves or converts actor, activity, population, period, event unit, denominator, and inclusion


PAGE 097 OF 108

criteria. Unit conversion requires a known conversion factor; population conversion requires a justified
relationship. Intervals remain intervals unless a separate estimator is introduced. An upper bound does not
become an exact count by repetition.
RULE B-R09, SOURCE PROPAGATION. A derived record inherits the union of its declared source
roots and the transformations applied to them. Union is idempotent: adding another copy of the same root
does not increase the number of distinct roots. Distinct roots are necessary metadata for independence
assessment, but distinct identifiers alone do not establish statistical or causal independence.
RULE B-R10, REVISION. A correction creates a new version and a relation to the earlier record. The
underlying ledger retains both versions. An active view selects which versions remain eligible as premises
and recomputes dependent results. Retraction of a premise withdraws its support path; it does not prove the
opposite proposition. A conclusion with another independent surviving path may remain supported.
B.2 The forbidden automatic arrows
The following are not default rules: assertion to truth; belief to truth; assumption to actuality; question
background to admitted occurrence; silence to denial; non-objection to verified agreement; agreement to
independent origination; repetition to corroboration; possible to actual; some to all; general behavior to a
particular event; observed range to preference; absence from a partial record to non-occurrence; observed
effect to intended effect; correlation to cause; present interpretation to earlier knowledge; and conditional
inventory to recovered inventory. Each can become supportable in a suitably strengthened context, but the
additional premises must be stated and tested.
The audit records a missing bridge rather than declaring the underlying claim false. For example, a
statement about deletion can be accurate despite an invalid inference used to obtain it. The error concerns
the asserted route to knowledge. This is especially important when a later investigation supplies evidence
that was unavailable during the interview. Later evidence can change the factual assessment without
retroactively changing the source of an earlier assertion.
B.3 Counterfactual deletion of an assumption
DEFINITION B-D1. An assumption-deletion test removes a disputed premise a from the declared
hard-premise set and recomputes the query phi. If phi was entailed under H union {a} but is undetermined
under H, the derivation depends on a in that view. This establishes a logical dependence, not the falsity of
a and not the psychological cause of the respondent's answer.
A stronger analysis examines minimal sufficient subsets of H for phi, subject to a finite bound. A premise
may be unnecessary in one proof and necessary in another. Therefore a single derivation's dependence
should not be confused with global indispensability. The provenance graph records all available support
paths within the chosen scope and identifies whether an independent path survives removal of the
questioned source.
EXAMPLE B.A. A manager asks why an operator deleted a backup. The operator says that the contents
were worrying. Let d be deletion and w be concern. The question supplies d; the answer directly supplies
w. The valuation w=true, d=false shows that w alone does not entail d. A contextually interpreted answer
may communicate more, but that additional reading needs a recorded justification.
EXAMPLE B.B. A technician states that a switch was either replaced or reset. Let r be replacement and s
be reset. The record supports a report of r OR s. An invoice that covers only inspection does not settle
either disjunct. A later summary saying replacement occurred is a strengthening. A serial-number change
could provide a new route to r, provided the device identity and dates match.
EXAMPLE B.C. A clerk says that a permit probably expired before the inspection. A summary says that
the permit had expired and the holder knew it. This changes modality and adds a mental-state claim. The

APPENDIX C. UNIFORM RESULTS AND THEIR PROOF
OBLIGATIONS
C.1 Record nonfactivity
PROPOSITION C-P1. If the semantics imposes no bridge from Assert(s,p) to p, then Assert(s,p) does not
entail p in general.
Proof. Consider a model with Assert(s,p) true and p false. The absence of a bridge leaves this assignment
permitted. The premise is true and the proposed conclusion false, which is a countermodel to entailment.
The result concerns logical implication under the declared semantics. It does not assign a probability to
testimony and does not imply that every assertion is unreliable.
C.2 Conditional discharge
PROPOSITION C-P2. In classical semantics, if H union {a} |= b, then H |= a -> b. This does not imply H
|= a or H |= b.
Proof. Take any model m of H. If a is false in m, a -> b is true. If a is true, m is a model of H union {a}, so
b is true by the premise. Therefore the implication is true in every model of H. For non-entailment of a or
b, take H empty and a=b=p. The discharge p -> p is valid, but the valuation p=false shows that neither p
conclusion follows. The proof also identifies the exact meaning of discharging an assumption: the
assumption becomes an antecedent, not a discovered event.
C.3 Orthogonal refinement
PROPOSITION C-P3. If a finite collection of nonempty sets A_i partitions a set M, then the nonempty
sets A_i intersect P and A_i intersect (M minus P) also partition M. Their union within each parent
reconstructs A_i.
Proof. Every m in A_i belongs to exactly one of P and M minus P, so the two children are disjoint and
cover A_i. Children of different parents are disjoint because their parents are. Taking the union over all
parents therefore covers M without overlap. Removing an empty child changes no union. Removing a
nonempty child requires a new exclusion premise and changes the admissible family. Induction repeats the
argument for any finite sequence of splits.
C.4 Pairwise compatibility is insufficient
PROPOSITION C-P4. Three Boolean constraints can be pairwise jointly satisfiable while their
conjunction is unsatisfiable.
Proof. Let A say p differs from q, B say q differs from r, and C say p differs from r. A and B are satisfied
by p=true,q=false,r=true. A and C are satisfied by p=true,q=false,r=false. B and C are satisfied by
p=false,q=false,r=true. All three cannot hold: A and B imply that p and r have the same Boolean value,
contradicting C. Every pair has a witness, while the joint constraint has none. This establishes why a full
set of pairwise grids cannot replace hyperedges.
C.5 Conservative extension
PROPOSITION C-P5. Let pi map every model in a new family M_new to its restriction in an old
language. Suppose pi(M_new)=M_old and old formulas preserve their truth under restriction. Then every
old-language formula has the same skeptical classification in the two nonempty families.
Proof. If phi is true in every old model, it is true in every restriction of a new model, hence in every new
model. Conversely, if phi is true in every new model, surjectivity supplies a new extension for each old
model, so phi is true in every old model. Apply the same argument to NOT phi. If neither side is universal,


PAGE 100 OF 108

witnesses for both polarities also lift and restrict. Thus entailment, refutation, and undetermined status
agree. Without surjectivity, an old possible case may have been removed; preservation cannot be inferred
merely from a larger vocabulary or a more elaborate display.
C.6 Local and uniform witnesses
PROPOSITION C-P6. For every context c, existence of some witness x satisfying P(c,x) does not imply
existence of one x satisfying P(c,x) in every context.
Proof. Let contexts be c1,c2 and objects be a,b. Let P(c1,a) and P(c2,b) be true, with the other two
instances false. Each context has a witness, but neither object works in both contexts. The example refutes
interchange of the quantifiers forall c exists x and exists x forall c. A choice of local witnesses records a
function dependent on c; it does not establish one universal sender, one common file, or one shared cause.
C.7 Finite classification
PROPOSITION C-P7. Enumerating all 2^n Boolean assignments over a declared set of n atoms and
evaluating every hard premise and the query gives an exact classification for that finite propositional
instance, provided the evaluator implements the declared connectives correctly and no assignment is
skipped.
Proof. Every Boolean model over those atoms is exactly one enumerated assignment. Filtering retains
precisely those satisfying all premises. If none remain, the instance is inconsistent. Otherwise the query is
either true in all, false in all, or true in some and false in others. Those cases are disjoint and exhaustive.
The proof is uniform in finite n; the executable implementation imposes a resource cap and its tests verify
selected evaluator and enumeration families. The proof does not authenticate the annotation that chose the
atoms or premises.
C.8 Adding premises and adding records
PROPOSITION C-P8. If H subseteq H_new, then M(H_new) subseteq M(H). If both families are
nonempty, a previous entailment cannot be lost by adding hard premises. By contrast, adding a correction
record can change an active premise selection policy and need not be monotone in its conclusions.
Proof. Every model satisfying H_new satisfies each member of H. Therefore any formula true throughout
M(H) remains true on its subset. The nonempty guard prevents the reporting system from turning a
contradiction into universal certainty. For the second statement, take an initial active view {p}, then add a
record withdrawing p so that the new active view is empty. The ledger has grown, but p changes from
entailed to undetermined. The difference is between growth of records and growth of a selected
hard-premise set.
C.9 Copies and information
PROPOSITION C-P9. If Y is a deterministic copy of X, observing the pair (X,Y) distinguishes no more
states of a finite underlying sample space than observing X.
Proof. Any two states with the same value of X have the same value of Y, so the partition induced by
(X,Y) is no finer than the partition induced by X. Because X is a component of the pair, it is also no
coarser. The partitions are equal. This proves a precise no-new-distinction result for exact copies. It does
not show that two separately produced reports are independent, nor that a transformed report is always an
exact copy. Those are source-history questions.


PAGE 101 OF 108

C.10 A bounded certificate is not a universal interpretation
The kernel's certificates concern the explicit formal input. A valid certificate can coexist with a mistaken
transcription, an omitted alternative, or an incorrect domain rule. Consequently the chain from raw
recording to final assessment has at least two distinct correctness obligations: adequacy of the
formalization and correctness of reasoning within it. Formal validity can be mechanically checked in the
finite kernel. Adequacy requires a documented mapping to evidence and attention to alternatives outside
the current abstraction.
EXAMPLE C.A. A cupboard contains either the red key or the blue key. Splitting the model family by
RedPresent partitions it into two disjoint cases. Both cases retain the fact that a key is present. The
common existential belongs in the center, while the identity of the key remains in the signed branches.
EXAMPLE C.B. Three witnesses each impose one of the difference constraints in C-P4 on three indicator
lights. Every pair of reports can be accommodated, but all three together cannot. A pairwise dashboard can
look complete while missing the contradiction. The joint hyperedge exposes the smallest inconsistent set.
EXAMPLE C.C. A new field records whether a machine is blue, while old claims concern whether it runs.
If every old running/stopped model admits a blue or non-blue extension, the old query is preserved. If the
new rule permits only running machines, the vocabulary extension has also filtered cases. Calling the
change merely descriptive would conceal an evidentiary restriction.

APPENDIX D. ANNOTATION RECORDS AND AUDIT FORMS
D.1 The evidence packet
A complete evidence packet has a stable packet identifier, a description of the available material,
acquisition dates, source identities, file hashes where applicable, and an explicit list of missing or
inaccessible material. A hash supports byte-identity checking under the algorithm's collision assumptions.
Digest agreement is not a logical proof of identity and does not establish that a recording is authentic,
complete, correctly dated, or accurately interpreted. A faithful copy of an edited recording is still a faithful
copy of an edited recording. The packet therefore distinguishes byte integrity from evidentiary provenance
and completeness.
The transcript record retains original text separately from normalized text. Normalization can correct
spacing, expand a clearly identified abbreviation, or assign stable labels, but the original remains available.
Every substantive repair records the editor, date, source span, proposed replacement, reason, and
confidence category. A guessed word cannot enter the normalized transcript as though it had been heard
clearly. Alternative repairs receive separate versions or candidate readings. Overlapping speech and absent
responses receive their own markers rather than punctuation that silently assigns an answer to a question.
D.2 Minimum claim record
The following is a conceptual record, not a promise that every field is consumed by the finite reference
implementation. The executable API is documented with the code. A full implementation can serialize the
conceptual record into JSON, a relational database, or another lossless format while preserving these roles.
claim_id: stable identity of the scoped proposition record
formula_id: stable identity of the typed formula
formula: predicate, arguments, binders, context, event-time scope
utterer: identified entity or explicit candidate set
first_introducer: first available source of the formulation
speech_act: assertion, denial, question, proposal, assumption, quotation, other
event_time: time or interval of the described event, possibly unknown
utterance_time: position or time of the communicative event
acquisition_time: time the analyst obtained the record
context_id: local conversational or hypothetical context
modality: actual, possible, probable, conditional, reported, unknown
quantifier_scope: domain, count, bound, unit, denominator, exceptions
source_spans: exact supporting locations with file or record identity
dependencies: typed directed or hypergraph links
qualifications: preserved hedges, exclusions, and conditions
corrections: versioned relations to revised or rejected readings
status: record status, support status, and model-query status separately
version: immutable revision identifier and predecessor relation
A field can be unknown without making the entire record unusable. The purpose is to preserve partial
information. A known actor and uncertain object should remain known actor plus uncertain object, not an
entirely blank record and not a guessed object. The matching procedure may propagate constraints, but
every narrowing must show why excluded alternatives no longer satisfy the evidence contract.

D.3 Question and answer records
A question record stores its surface wording, presupposed background under each reading, foreground
answer slot, offered alternatives, requested time and population, and candidate target propositions. An
answer record stores its own wording and a set of possible links to those targets. A polar answer to a
compound question requires a policy for scope. Without a clear reading, the answer may support the
overall bundle, one component, an immediately preceding repair, or merely receipt of the question. The
analyst should not select the most incriminating interpretation by default or dismiss clear agreement
merely because the interviewer introduced the proposition.
Answer alignment is a constrained inference, not simple proximity. A response can answer an earlier
unfinished question after an interruption. A speaker can correct an assumption before answering its
subordinate request. An edited clip can remove the actual question. Timing, syntax, topic, explicit
references, and the available audio jointly inform the link. The kernel can validate an annotated link
against declared guards; it cannot discover the intended link from unrestricted conversation by itself.
D.4 Transformation record
A transformation has an input formula or record set, an output, a declared operation, preserved fields,
changed fields, supporting rule, and residual obligations. Changes can concern actor identity, object
identity, event time, tense, modality, quantifier, negation scope, mental-state predicate, action, source
attribution, or evidentiary strength. A transformation that changes several fields should not be approved
merely because one change is justified. Each changed field is a separate obligation, though the evidence
for several obligations may be joint rather than separable.
The audit form for a questioned summary can use the following plain fields:
Original span and version:
Original typed claim:
Proposed summary and version:
Added predicates or stronger quantifiers:
Removed conditions or uncertainty:
Changed person, object, time, population, or source:
Explicit assent, correction, or unresolved reply:
Model or countermodel:
Independent support path:
Finding and exact scope:
Smallest additional evidence needed:
D.5 Translation, quotation, irony, and indirect speech
A translated transcript adds an interpretation layer. The original expression, translator's rendering, and
analyst's formalization remain distinct records. Different languages can encode tense, evidentiality,
politeness, omitted subjects, and indirectness differently. The system requires preservation of those
distinctions where they affect the claim; it does not assume that a literal word-for-word replacement
preserves speech-act force. A domain adapter states the available language expertise and the unresolved
alternatives.
Quotation introduces a speaker inside a speaker's report. If Mara reports that Ivo said p, the immediate
record is Report(Mara,Assert(Ivo,p)). It is not automatically Assert(Mara,p), and neither entails p. Sarcasm
or irony can invert the intended evaluation without changing surface words, but that reading needs
contextual support. When literal and ironic readings remain possible, both are retained. No universal rule
turns laughter, hesitation, or a polite response into a fixed truth value or intention.

EXAMPLE D.A. An exported meeting transcript places "Nora: yes" between two questions sharing a
one-second timestamp. Ordering within that timestamp is unverified in this export; coarse timestamps
alone would not invalidate a reliably preserved turn order. The first displayed question concerns receipt of

EXAMPLE D.B. A bilingual clerk translates a phrase as "the permit must have expired." One reading
expresses an inference from missing paperwork; another expresses an obligation under a rule. The
formalizations Infer(clerk,Expired) and Required(Expired) have different types. Keeping both readings
prevents a modal verb from silently becoming a factual expiry certificate.
EXAMPLE D.C. A technician says, "The manager called the unit safe." The speaker reports a manager's
characterization. If a summary becomes "the technician confirmed the unit was safe," both attribution and
evidentiary status change. The corrected record preserves the nested report and separately asks whether the
technician endorsed it or had independent inspection results.

APPENDIX E. VERIFICATION CONTRACT AND
REPRODUCIBILITY
E.1 Three different verification questions
The first question is whether the formal rules are valid under their stated semantics. This is addressed by
proofs and countermodels in the manuscript. The second is whether the reference software implements its
declared finite rules. This is addressed by executable tests, independent comparison methods, and
adversarial inputs. The third is whether a particular annotation adequately represents a real conversation.
This is addressed by evidence review and interpretive audit. A success in one category cannot
automatically answer the other two.
The phrase "the system works" therefore receives a precise operational meaning. The kernel must accept
legitimate transitions, reject declared invalid ones, preserve uncertainty and source dependence, return
countermodels where required, and report inconsistency without universal certification. The document
package must preserve the complete manuscript, remain ASCII in its plain-text edition, render legibly, and
reproduce its declared results. These are inspectable requirements. They do not constitute a
population-level study of interview interpretation or an accuracy guarantee on unseen natural-language
material.
E.2 The finite semantic contract
The core accepts explicitly annotated Boolean formulas and bounded structured records. The declared
atom cap prevents exponential enumeration from becoming an unbounded operation. A request above the
cap must fail visibly or be marked unsupported; it must not silently sample a subset and label the result
exhaustive. The constants true and false, empty premise sets, contradictory premise sets, single-atom
cases, and irrelevant additional atoms are important boundary cases.
For every successful enumeration, the result records the atom vocabulary, hard premises, query, number of
assignments considered, retained model count, and witnesses appropriate to the classification. An entailed
result has no retained countermodel; a refuted result has no retained positive witness; an undetermined
result includes both; an inconsistent result has no retained models. Checking these output invariants is
separate from checking the recursive evaluator.
Independent implementation means a materially different route to the same finite result, not merely calling
the same function from another wrapper. A bit-set or truth-table oracle can evaluate formula behavior
independently of a recursive evaluator. Hand-computable fixtures check the intended meaning of operators
and guards. Metamorphic checks test changes that should preserve the answer, such as renaming atoms or
permuting premises. Their preservation claims must themselves be valid: arbitrary deletion of premises is
not such a change.
E.3 Adversarial transformations
The suite includes invalid transformations that resemble persuasive summaries: assertion promoted to fact,
assumption promoted to occurrence, possible promoted to actual, changed identity, changed event time,
changed denominator, copied source counted again, and an answer assigned to a proposition introduced
later. It also includes valid controls: an explicit answer linked to the right question, a declared premise
supporting its consequence, a correctly scoped paraphrase, and a refinement that preserves all parent cases.
A checker that rejects every input would pass only the invalid examples and fail the control set.
Mutation checks deliberately alter one relevant feature of a previously valid fixture. The expected effect
must be specified before evaluating the mutation. For example, changing an answer's target ID while
keeping the words identical should expose an attachment problem when the new target lacks the necessary


PAGE 106 OF 108

temporal and contextual link. A result count reports actual evaluated cases and comparisons. Repeated
assertions inside a loop are not presented as independent scientific replications.
E.4 File and edition checks
The build uses canonical source chapters rather than separately composed text and PDF narratives. The
integrity checks verify the source inventory, required chapter and example markers, ASCII restrictions,
declared references, and matching manuscript content in the rendered edition. PDF extraction is compared
with the canonical body after documented removal of running headers and page numbers. Layout checks
examine page count, text boundaries, and rendered pages. Extraction alone cannot establish legibility
because clipped or overlapping text may still be extractable.
The ZIP has a manifest of file paths and cryptographic hashes. Archive integrity is checked by opening
and reading its members, verifying the expected inventory, and comparing bytes with the packaged source
files. The archive's own hash can be reported outside the archive; placing a hash of the entire archive
inside itself would create a self-reference problem. Generated timestamps and metadata can prevent
byte-for-byte PDF reproducibility unless deliberately fixed. The package distinguishes content
reproducibility from an identical binary build.
E.5 Reproducibility record
The accompanying verification report provides the command actually run, software versions, relevant
input hashes, runtime, exit status, tested families, bounds, result counts, and residual limits. No
placeholder result is treated as completed evidence. A failed test is classified as a mathematical
counterexample, an implementation defect, an artifact defect, an environment failure, or an inconclusive
bounded run. After a correction, the affected checks are rerun and the final outputs receive new hashes.
The core checker uses the Python standard library so it can be rerun without a network connection. PDF
generation and inspection use separately listed dependencies. An inability to rebuild the PDF in a different
environment does not invalidate a successful semantic test, and a semantic pass does not prove that an
uninspected PDF is well laid out. The report keeps these outcomes separate and states the actual coverage
of each.
EXAMPLE E.A. A fixture containing only Assert(worker,Deleted) is queried for Deleted. The expected
world result is UNDETERMINED unless a bridge is explicitly added. A mutation adds the hard premise
Deleted, making the result ENTAILED. This pair checks both resistance to unauthorized promotion and
acceptance of a declared factual premise.
EXAMPLE E.B. A valid rate refers to failures per inspected device during one month. A mutation changes
the denominator to all devices ever manufactured. The numerical value is unchanged, but the
quantity-transfer guard should reject the move. A separate valid unit conversion preserves the population
and changes only a known time unit with a justified conversion.
EXAMPLE E.C. A document build accidentally omits the final example of Chapter 12. The PDF may
open normally and have plausible page count. A marker-inventory check detects the missing example; a
canonical-content comparison identifies the omitted text. The example demonstrates why file opening,
appearance, content integrity, and mathematical verification are distinct gates.
